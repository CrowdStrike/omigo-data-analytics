"You reap what you sow"

Folk wisdom dissected into its component fallacies — what the saying hides and why it feels true anyway.

Why people believe it: appealing just-world hypothesis — the universe is fair and effort is rewarded proportionally.
#FallacyHow it hides
1Just-world fallacyAssumes a causal link between input quality and output quality. Ignores systemic factors, randomness, and external interference.
2Survivorship biasPeople who sowed well and reaped well are the confirming examples. People who sowed well and reaped nothing (due to drought, theft, market crash) are invisible.
3Ignores systemic factorsA farmer can sow perfectly and lose everything to a flood. The quality of input doesn't deterministically control output in complex systems.
4Victim-blaming scaffoldIf bad things happened to you, this saying implies you "sowed" something wrong. It's used to explain away injustice.
5Single-factor attributionOutcome = f(effort) ignores f(luck, timing, privilege, connections, market, health).
Undefined terms: "sow" (effort? intent? quality?), "reap" (outcome proportional to input? eventual? in same domain?)
Counterexamples:
  • Fraud — some people sow deception and reap billions (until caught, or never)
  • Inherited wealth — reaping without sowing
  • Natural disasters — destroying crops regardless of how well they were sowed
