Druid & Pinot

Real-time OLAP stores that answer aggregations over event streams in under a second, on data that is only seconds old — the combination a warehouse can't deliver

The Dashboard That Shows the Last Five Minutes

core ideareal-time OLAPevent streams
  • The dashboard — an ad platform lets advertisers watch impressions and clicks live, per campaign
  • The window — every refresh asks: totals for MY campaign over the last 5 minutes, right now
  • The stream — ad events pour into a Kafka topic; Druid and Pinot ingest straight from it
  • The freshness — an impression served at 2:00:00pm is queryable on the dashboard seconds later
  • The warehouse gap — a batch-loaded warehouse would show that same impression an hour late

An advertiser pauses a broken ad at 2:04pm and watches its impressions fall to zero on the very next refresh — with hourly batch loads the drop would appear at 3pm.

Key point: Druid and Pinot exist for exactly one job: sub-second aggregations over event streams that are fresh to the last few seconds, ingested directly from Kafka.

Summing Five Minutes of Impressions by Hand

worked exampletime segmentscolumn indexes
  • The layout — events land in time-partitioned segments, one per minute, columns indexed on write
  • The query — impressions and clicks for campaign 42 over the last 5 minutes = touch 5 segments only
  • The index — a pre-built index on the campaign column jumps straight to campaign 42's rows
  • The sum — per-minute impressions 12,400 + 12,900 + 13,100 + 12,700 + 13,300 = 64,400 (exact)
  • The clicks — per-minute clicks 310 + 322 + 335 + 318 + 328 = 1,613, so CTR = 1,613 / 64,400 ≈ 2.5% (rounded)
  • The speed — five small indexed scans, no full-table pass: the answer returns in ~80 ms

At 2:05pm the dashboard shows campaign 42 at 64,400 impressions, 1,613 clicks, 2.5% CTR — computed from just the five 1-minute segments in the window.

Key point: Time partitioning prunes the scan to the queried window and column indexes prune it to the queried campaign — that double pruning is why the aggregation takes milliseconds, not minutes.

The Niche Warehouses Can't Serve

where it's useduser-facing analytics
  • Druid's origin — built at Metamarkets to power interactive ad-analytics dashboards
  • Pinot's origin — built at LinkedIn for "who viewed your profile", analytics shown to every member
  • The load — user-facing means every user fires queries: millions of queries, not a few analysts
  • Two demands at once — results in under a second AND data fresh to the last few seconds
  • The warehouse miss — warehouses batch-load (stale) and serve few concurrent queries (slow at scale)

"Who viewed your profile" runs an analytics query for each of millions of members opening the page — a load no analyst-facing warehouse is built to take.

Key point: The niche is user-facing dashboards where freshness and latency both matter at high query volume; both systems were open sourced out of companies that hit that exact wall.

Not a Faster Warehouse

common mistakeright tool
  • The confusion — teams read "sub-second SQL" and try to replace the warehouse with Druid or Pinot
  • The trade — pre-built indexes and time segments are tuned for anticipated aggregation shapes
  • What suffers — large arbitrary joins and exploratory full-history SQL are the warehouse's job
  • The pairing — the common architecture runs both: stream to Druid/Pinot, batch to the warehouse
  • The tell — if the query has no time window and no dashboard behind it, it belongs elsewhere

The same team's "last 5 minutes by campaign" query takes 80 ms in Pinot, but their analyst's 6-month join against the customer table still runs in the warehouse.

Common mistake: Treating Druid or Pinot as a general-purpose warehouse. They buy sub-second latency by pre-organizing data around time-windowed aggregations — ad-hoc joins over full history is the workload they deliberately gave up.
