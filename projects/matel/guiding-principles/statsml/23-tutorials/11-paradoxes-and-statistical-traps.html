Paradoxes & Statistical Traps

Situations where a perfectly reasonable reading of the data gives the wrong answer — because of how the rows were selected, sampled, or incentivized.

SAMPLING PARADOXES

1. Berkson's Paradox

Keep only the rows that cleared a bar built from two traits, and a negative correlation appears between them that never existed in the full population.

SAMPLING PARADOXES

2. The Inspection Paradox

Buses average one every 10 minutes, yet your wait is nearly 10 — random arrivals land in long gaps more often, so what you sample is bigger than the average.

3. Goodhart's Law

When a measure becomes a target, people optimize the number instead of the thing it measured — and the number quietly stops meaning anything.

4. Anchoring, Decoys & Loss Aversion

The first number you see drags your estimate toward it — menus plant decoy prices and "don't lose it" wording to steer choices the same way.
