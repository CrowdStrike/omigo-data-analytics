Seasonal & Holiday Commerce — Distribution Patterns

5 distributions that expose the extreme asymmetries hiding inside holiday retail data

99TH PERCENTILE DAY

Black Friday Hourly Volume (Extreme Spike)

Normal day volume is flat and low. Black Friday concentrates 10x volume into a 4-hour window. Logistics infrastructure sized for this ONE day sits idle 364 days/year — the distribution shape IS the capacity planning problem.

  • Normal day: uniform 50-150 transactions/hour
  • Black Friday hours 6-10: spike to 800 avg
  • Peak is 10x the normal daily maximum
  • Excess capacity cost = 364 days × idle infrastructure
BREAKAGE REVENUE

Gift Card Redemption (Bimodal + Breakage)

Gift cards create a trimodal distribution: immediate redeemers (week 1), delayed redeemers (6+ months), and never-redeemed (breakage). The zero-bin IS the profit center — $3B/year industry-wide in pure breakage revenue.

  • 45% redeemed within first week
  • 20% redeemed after 6+ months
  • 35% NEVER redeemed = pure profit
  • Breakage = $3B/year industry-wide
PREDICTABLE WAVE

Post-Holiday Return Wave (Spike + Decay)

Returns spike massively Dec 26-28 then decay exponentially over 30 days. The shape is perfectly predictable year-over-year — if you know the distribution, you can pre-schedule staff and save 20% on labor costs.

  • 60% of returns happen in first 3 days
  • Exponential decay τ ≈ 5 days after spike
  • Shape is identical year-over-year
  • Smart retailers pre-staff to the curve
ASYMMETRIC BUILD

Inventory Pre-Build (Asymmetric Triangle)

Inventory builds linearly Sept-Dec (4 months of slow ramp) then collapses to zero on Dec 26 in a single day. The distribution is a sawtooth — slow accumulation, instant liquidation. Cash flow is this shape inverted.

  • Linear build: Sept → Dec (4 months)
  • Cliff collapse: Dec 26 (1 day)
  • Asymmetry ratio: 120:1 (build:collapse)
  • Cash flow = this shape inverted
TWO POPULATIONS

Doorbuster Cannibalization (Bimodal Basket)

The same promotion attracts two opposite customer types: doorbuster hunters ($15 avg, negative margin) and full-trip shoppers ($120 avg, 25% margin). The mean basket of $60 describes NEITHER population — it's a statistical ghost.

  • Doorbuster hunters: $15 avg, buy loss leader only
  • Full-trip shoppers: $120 avg, 25% margin
  • Mean = $60 describes nobody
  • Same promo attracts OPPOSITE value customers
