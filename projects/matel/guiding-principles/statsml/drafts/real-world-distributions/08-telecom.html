Telecom & Networks — Distribution Patterns

Exponential + Spikes
Call Duration

Mostly short calls following exponential decay, with spikes at voicemail cutoff durations (30s, 60s) and a long tail from business calls.

  • Short calls exhibit the memoryless property — the probability of hanging up is constant regardless of elapsed time
  • Voicemail systems create fixed-duration spikes at 30s and 60s cutoffs
  • Business vs personal usage creates a mixture distribution — the long tail is not noise, it is a separate population
  • Mean call duration is dominated by the heavy tail; median is far more representative

Trimodal
Network Packet Sizes

Three distinct protocol modes produce a trimodal distribution: ACK packets (64 bytes), standard frames (576 bytes), and jumbo frames (1500 bytes).

  • Protocol-defined sizes create tight clusters — each mode has low variance because of rigid protocol constraints
  • MTU (Maximum Transmission Unit) creates a hard ceiling at 1500 bytes for standard Ethernet
  • Each mode corresponds to a different traffic type: control, web content, bulk transfer
  • Fitting a single normal to this data would be meaningless — always check modality first

Bursty / Self-Similar
Inter-Arrival Times

Network traffic is not Poisson: arrivals come in bursts followed by quiet periods. This produces clustered inter-arrival times rather than a smooth exponential.

  • Self-similar (fractal) traffic: bursts within bursts at multiple timescales
  • The Poisson assumption fails for real networks — it underestimates both very short and very long gaps
  • Long-range dependence means correlations persist across many time lags
  • Queue sizing based on Poisson models dramatically underestimates overflow probability

Bimodal with Gap
Signal Strength (RSSI, dBm)

Bimodal distribution: devices near the tower (-50 to -70 dBm) and devices at cell edge (-90 to -110 dBm), with a handover gap in between.

  • Inverse-square law of RF propagation creates the separation between near and far populations
  • Urban canyon effects (reflections, shadowing) widen each mode
  • Handover decisions create a transition zone — devices oscillate between towers in this gap
  • Coverage maps lie: they show median signal, not the bimodal reality users experience

Zero-Inflated Heavy Tail
Data Usage Per User (Monthly GB)

Extreme right skew: most users consume 1-5GB, heavy streamers use 50-100GB, and dormant SIMs produce near-zero usage creating zero-inflation.

  • Whale effect: top 5% of users consume more data than the bottom 60% combined
  • Dormant SIM zero-inflation: a structural zero mass that is not part of the continuous distribution
  • Unlimited plans remove price friction, creating a heavy tail that grows over time
  • Average usage per user is meaningless — it overestimates light users and underestimates heavy users simultaneously
