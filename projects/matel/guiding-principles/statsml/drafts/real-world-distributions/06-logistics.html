Logistics & Supply Chain — Distribution Patterns

Mixture Model Delivery Time

Delivery times are a mixture of success modes: on-time performance, delayed shipments, very late arrivals, and lost/redelivered packages. The gap between promised and actual creates an SLA compliance cliff.

  • On-time cluster near 3 days (majority of deliveries)
  • Delayed cluster around 5 days from warehouse/routing issues
  • Very late tail (7+ days) from customs, weather, capacity
  • Lost/redelivered packages create a flat tail out to 30 days
  • SLA thresholds create artificial cliff in the distribution — everything past a cutoff gets escalated differently

Intermittent / Lumpy Intermittent Demand (Spare Parts)

Spare parts demand is mostly zero, with occasional single-unit requests and rare bulk orders. Standard time-series forecasting (ARIMA, exponential smoothing) fails because the series is dominated by zeros.

  • 70% of periods have zero demand — mean is misleading
  • Single-unit requests from maintenance schedules
  • Occasional Poisson-like bursts from equipment failures
  • Rare bulk orders (10, 25, 50 units) from fleet replacements
  • Croston's method separates demand size from demand occurrence — requires treating the two processes independently

Workforce Heterogeneity Warehouse Pick Rate (Items/Hour)

Pick rates appear near-normal at first glance but reveal workforce heterogeneity: new workers in training, end-of-shift fatigue dips, and experienced/incentivized workers forming a right tail.

  • Core workforce clusters around 45 items/hour
  • New workers (training period) pull left tail to ~30 items/hour
  • End-of-shift fatigue creates a secondary mode near 38
  • Incentive programs create a right-tail mode at ~50 for top performers
  • Shift boundary effects mean time-of-day matters — aggregating across shifts masks the structure

Bounded / Beta-like Container Fill Rate

Fill rate is bounded 0-100% with operational targets clustering most containers at 85-95%. Penalties for under-filling (wasted capacity) and over-filling (safety/weight limits) create a compressed distribution near the optimum.

  • Empty containers (0-10%) from repositioning and scheduling gaps
  • Partial loads (30-60%) from LTL consolidation or urgent shipments
  • Main mass at 70-95% from operational optimization
  • Weight vs volume constraints mean 100% fill on one dimension may be 60% on the other
  • Beta distribution is natural for bounded rates — but the mixture of empties breaks it

Multimodal / Supplier Mix Lead Time Variability

Lead times from mixed supplier bases are inherently multimodal. Domestic, international, and ocean freight each have their own distribution, and customs clearance adds random jumps that further blur the modes.

  • Domestic suppliers cluster at ~14 days (narrow variance)
  • International air freight at ~45 days (moderate spread)
  • Ocean freight at ~90 days (wide variance from port congestion)
  • Exponential delay tail from customs holds, inspections, documentation
  • Mixing suppliers into one "lead time" field creates a distribution that no single parametric model fits — must decompose by source
