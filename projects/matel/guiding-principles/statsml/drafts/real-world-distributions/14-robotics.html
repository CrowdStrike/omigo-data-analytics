Robotics & Simulation — Distribution Patterns

SIGMOID (PHASE TRANSITION)

Grasp Success (Binary Phase Transition, Not Gradual)

Near-zero success below a pose threshold, then rapid S-curve to near-100% above it. Not a gradient — a cliff. Robotic grasping has a binary phase transition between graspable and ungraspable. Sigmoid steepness = gripper tolerance. Squishy gripper = gentle slope. Rigid jaw = vertical cliff.

  • Not gradual — binary phase transition at threshold angle
  • Sigmoid steepness = gripper mechanical tolerance
  • Squishy gripper = gentle slope (forgiving)
  • Rigid parallel jaw = near-vertical (one degree off = fail)
RIGHT-SKEWED (FIDELITY METRIC)

Sim-to-Real Gap (Tail Thickness = Simulator Quality)

Most actions transfer well (sim ≈ real), but fat right tail where reality is dramatically worse. The tail = everything simulation doesn't model (friction, cable snag, lighting). Tail thickness directly measures simulation fidelity. Companies benchmark simulators by tail shape, not average.

  • Body: sim error ≈ real error (good transfer)
  • Right tail: reality dramatically worse than sim predicted
  • Tail = unmodeled physics (friction, cables, lighting)
  • Tail thickness = simulator fidelity benchmark
BOUNDED + SATURATION SPIKES

Joint Torque (Saturation Spikes = Hardware Bottleneck)

Gaussian body within operating range, then delta spikes at ±max torque (motor saturation). The spikes = moments the robot hit its physical limit. Spike mass / body mass ratio = how often hardware bottlenecks software. If spikes >5%, the robot is undersized — no algorithm fix helps.

  • Gaussian body = normal operation range
  • Spikes at ±max = motor saturation (physical limit)
  • Spike ratio = hardware bottleneck frequency
  • >5% spike mass = robot undersized for task
BIMODAL (TWO SYSTEMS)

Path Planning Time (Familiar vs Novel Environment)

Spike at <1ms (cached/known path, fast recall) and broad mode at 50-500ms (novel scene requiring full planning). Reveals two-system architecture: fast habitual execution vs slow deliberate planning. The ratio between modes = environment novelty. Factory (95% fast) vs home (60% slow).

  • Spike at <1ms = cached path recall (System 1)
  • Broad mode 50-500ms = full planning search (System 2)
  • Ratio = environment novelty measure
  • Factory robot: 95% fast. Home robot: 60% slow
BIMODAL (CLIFF DYNAMICS)

Simulation Reward (Why RL Needs Millions of Episodes)

Cluster at high reward (task completed) and cluster at low/negative (catastrophic failure), thin middle. The thin middle proves cliff dynamics — you succeed completely or fail completely. RL gets almost no gradient from the middle. Bimodality explains why RL for robotics needs millions of episodes — most give binary signal.

  • High-reward cluster = task completed successfully
  • Low-reward cluster = catastrophic failure
  • Thin middle = cliff dynamics (no partial success)
  • Binary signal = why RL needs millions of episodes
