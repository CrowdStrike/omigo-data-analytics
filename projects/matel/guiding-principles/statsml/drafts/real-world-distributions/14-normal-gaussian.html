Normal (Gaussian) — Real-World Examples

The Central Limit Theorem's gift: sums of many independent factors converge to this. Symmetric, thin-tailed, fully described by mean and variance. But real data is normal far less often than assumed.

Adult Human Heights (μ=170cm, σ=7cm)

CLT IN ACTION
  • Sum of many genetic + nutritional factors; CLT applies well.
  • Mixing male + female populations creates bimodal — conditioning on sex matters.
  • Within a single-sex group, normality holds remarkably well.

Measurement Errors (μ=0, σ=0.5)

GAUSS'S MOTIVATION
  • Gauss's original motivation for deriving the distribution.
  • Systematic bias shifts the mean; instrument precision sets σ.
  • Averaging n measurements reduces σ by √n — the basis of all precision engineering.

IQ Scores (μ=100, σ=15)

BY CONSTRUCTION
  • Designed to be normal — tests are explicitly calibrated to produce this shape.
  • Floor and ceiling effects distort the tails at extremes.
  • Flynn effect shifts the mean upward over decades; re-norming resets it.

Daily Temperature Anomaly (μ=0, σ=2°C)

APPROXIMATELY NORMAL
  • Weather anomaly is the sum of many atmospheric factors.
  • Removing the seasonal trend (anomaly) makes it roughly symmetric.
  • Fat tails appear for extreme events — not quite normal, but close for the bulk.

Blood Pressure in Healthy Adults (μ=120, σ=10)

CONDITIONAL NORMALITY
  • Only normal WITHIN the healthy sub-population.
  • Adding hypertensives creates a right-skewed or bimodal mixture.
  • "Normal range" in medicine ≠ "Gaussian" — it means clinically acceptable.

Bolt Lengths from a Well-Calibrated Machine (μ=50.00mm, σ=0.02mm)

PROCESS CONTROL
  • The poster child for normality — process in statistical control.
  • 6σ = 0.12mm total spread; virtually all output falls within this range.
  • Cpk index measures how centered the process is within spec limits.
