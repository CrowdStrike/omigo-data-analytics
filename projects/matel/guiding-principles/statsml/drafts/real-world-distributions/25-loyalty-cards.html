Loyalty & Credit Card Analytics — Distribution Patterns

5 distributions that expose how customer behavior clusters, concentrates, and fractures across loyalty programs

HEAVY BUYER DOMINANCE

Purchase Frequency (Negative Binomial)

Most cardholders transact once or twice a year. A tiny elite visits weekly. The "average" purchase frequency describes nobody — it sits in the empty middle between casuals and power users.

  • 50% of customers visit 3x/year or fewer
  • Top 5% visit 40+ times/year
  • Mean is 3-4x higher than median
  • Negative binomial: overdispersed count data
WHALE CONCENTRATION

Customer Lifetime Value (Pareto)

Revenue follows a Pareto distribution — the top 5% of customers generate roughly 50% of total revenue. Losing one whale equals losing 100 casuals. CRM strategy must be bifurcated, not averaged.

  • Pareto(alpha=1.2): extreme right tail
  • Top 5% = ~50% of revenue (Gini ~0.6)
  • Losing 1 whale = losing 100 casuals
  • Mean CLV is meaningless for resource allocation
BATHTUB CURVE

Churn Hazard (Bathtub Curve)

Churn is NOT uniform over time. It follows the classic bathtub shape: high early (onboarding failures), low and stable in the middle, then rising again as fatigue sets in. Three distinct failure modes, each requiring a different intervention.

  • Month 1-2: onboarding failures (never activated)
  • Month 3-22: stable retention (habit formed)
  • Month 24+: fatigue churn (boredom, better offers)
  • One churn model for three populations = wrong
TWO PSYCHOLOGIES

Reward Redemption Timing (Bimodal)

Redemption behavior splits into two camps: instant gratifiers who redeem small rewards immediately, and planners who hoard points for big-ticket items. Same program, opposite strategies — the mean redemption point describes neither group.

  • 60% redeem at ~50 points (instant gratification)
  • 40% save to ~500 points (goal setters)
  • Valley at 150-300 points (no man's land)
  • One reward tier structure fits neither psychology
ROUND-NUMBER SCARS

Transaction Amount (Log-Normal + Spikes)

Transaction amounts follow a log-normal base (most purchases small, long right tail) — but with visible spikes at $20, $50, and $100. These aren't natural spending patterns; they're policy scars: gift card denominations, authorization limits, and round-number psychology.

  • Base shape: log-normal (median ~$33)
  • $20 spike = gift card denominations
  • $50 spike = pre-authorization limits
  • $100 spike = round-number psychology
