Manufacturing & IoT — Distribution Patterns

Common distributional shapes in manufacturing processes, sensor data, and industrial IoT systems.

CNC Machining Tolerances Mixture Drift

Extremely tight normal distribution (±0.01mm) with rare outlier clusters from progressive tool wear. The main process is in-control, but worn tooling introduces a shifted sub-population.

  • Process capability: Cp/Cpk indices assume normality — hidden mixture inflates variance and understates defect rate
  • Six Sigma: 3.4 DPMO target assumes stable single-mode process; tool wear violates stationarity
  • Drift signature: Mean shifts gradually within a batch as tool edge degrades — time-ordered control charts catch what histograms miss
  • Generation: 95% N(50.000, 0.003), 5% N(50.015, 0.008)

Vibration Sensor (Bearing Health) Right-Skew Shoulder

Normal baseline vibration develops a right-skew shoulder as bearing surfaces degrade. The shoulder grows over time, eventually dominating the distribution before catastrophic failure.

  • Condition monitoring: Healthy bearings show tight symmetric RMS vibration; degradation adds energy at higher amplitudes
  • Early warning: The shoulder appears weeks before failure — detecting distribution shape change is more reliable than threshold alarms
  • Progressive failure: Mixture weights shift over bearing lifetime: healthy mode shrinks, degraded mode grows
  • Generation: 70% N(0.5, 0.1), 20% N(1.2, 0.3), 10% Exp(0.5)+1.5

Time-to-Failure (Weibull) Bathtub Curve

Classic reliability bathtub: infant mortality (early defective units), a stable random-failure period, and eventual wear-out. The composite of three Weibull regimes.

  • β < 1 (infant): Decreasing failure rate — manufacturing defects, assembly errors, burn-in candidates
  • β = 1 (random): Constant failure rate — equivalent to exponential; failures are memoryless
  • β > 1 (wear-out): Increasing failure rate — fatigue, corrosion, cumulative damage mechanisms
  • Pitfall: Fitting a single Weibull to bathtub data gives misleading parameter estimates — use mixture or piecewise models
  • Generation: 15% Weibull(0.5, 10), 70% Weibull(1, 500), 15% Weibull(3, 800)

Batch Quality Score Strong Bimodal

Bimodal distribution: good batches cluster at 95-100 and reject batches at 60-75, with almost nothing in between. The process is either in-control or out-of-control — there is no gradual degradation.

  • Binary nature: Manufacturing quality often follows go/no-go physics — a seal holds or it leaks, a weld bonds or it cracks
  • Mean is meaningless: Average quality score (~91) describes no actual batch — every batch is either ~97 or ~68
  • Root cause: The gap implies a discrete assignable cause (wrong material lot, machine miscalibration, operator error)
  • Generation: 80% N(97, 1.5) clamped [0,100], 20% N(68, 4) clamped [0,100]

Sensor Reading with Saturation Ceiling Censoring

Normal measurement that clips at ADC maximum (1023 for 10-bit). Values that would exceed the ceiling pile up at the boundary, creating a spike that distorts all summary statistics.

  • Ceiling censoring: True values above 1023 are unobservable — the spike contains no information about how far above they actually are
  • Distorted statistics: Mean is biased downward, variance is compressed, normality tests reject due to the spike
  • Tobit models: Maximum-likelihood estimation that accounts for censoring recovers the true underlying distribution parameters
  • Prevention: Choose sensor range so operating point is at ~60-70% of full scale, not 80%+
  • Generation: 1000 samples N(800, 150), clamped at max 1023
