Log-Normal Distribution — Real-World Examples

When a quantity is the product of many independent positive factors, its logarithm is normal. Always positive, right-skewed, and often confused with power-law in the tail. Median is more informative than mean.

Household Income

μ_log = 10.8, σ_log = 0.6

Annual household income in USD. The distribution arises from multiplicative factors: education × industry × experience × location.

  • Median ~$60k but mean pulled far up by the long right tail
  • Gini coefficient ~0.4 is a direct consequence of log-normal shape
  • Policy debates confuse mean and median — the gap is the skewness
  • Top 10% holds disproportionate share precisely because of multiplicative compounding

Particle Sizes from Grinding

μ_log = 2, σ_log = 0.8

Diameter in micrometers after ball-mill crushing. Fracture is inherently multiplicative — each break multiplies the particle size by a random fraction.

  • Fundamental physics of comminution (Kolmogorov's breakage theory)
  • Critical for cement fineness, pharmaceutical dissolution rates
  • Quality control uses D50 (median) and D90 (90th percentile), not mean
  • Sieve analysis data fits log-normal remarkably well

Duration of Hospital Stays

μ_log = 1.5, σ_log = 0.9

Days in hospital. Base recovery time is multiplied by complications and co-morbidities — a multiplicative process.

  • DRG coding creates artificial modes but underlying process is log-normal
  • Mean length-of-stay is meaningless for capacity planning — use percentiles
  • A few extreme stays (ICU, complications) dominate total bed-days
  • Insurance actuaries model this explicitly as log-normal

Latency of Biological Infections

μ_log = 2.3, σ_log = 0.5

Incubation period in days for a virus. Viral replication is multiplicative growth until a detection threshold is crossed.

  • Serial interval estimation depends on this distribution shape
  • Epidemiologists report median incubation, not mean — precisely because of skew
  • Quarantine duration set by upper percentile (e.g., 95th) not mean
  • COVID-19 incubation was well-fit by log-normal (median ~5 days)

Size of Oil/Gas Deposits

μ_log = 3, σ_log = 1.5

Recoverable barrels in millions. Geological accumulation is a multiplicative process — trap size × porosity × saturation × pressure.

  • A few giant fields hold most of the world's reserves
  • Exploration economics depend entirely on tail probability
  • Discovery-size distributions well-documented as log-normal since 1960s
  • Expected value of exploration dominated by rare large finds

Time to Complete a Task

μ_log = 3, σ_log = 0.7

Minutes for a knowledge worker to complete a ticket. Interruptions and dependencies multiply the base time.

  • Hofstadter's Law: "It always takes longer than you expect, even when you account for Hofstadter's Law"
  • Sprint planning should use percentiles (P75, P90), not averages
  • Story point velocity is noisy because underlying time is log-normal
  • Summing log-normals does not produce a log-normal — portfolio effects are complex
