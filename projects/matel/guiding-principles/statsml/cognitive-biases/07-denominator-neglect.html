Denominator Neglect

Getting drawn to the percentage while ignoring the tiny sample behind it. A large number from a small bucket is noise masquerading as signal.

Small-n Percentages

Product A: 5.0 stars (3 reviews). Product B: 4.2 stars (1,847 reviews). Three ratings are trivially easy to game or coincidence. Product B's 4.2 has been validated by nearly 2,000 independent observations.

In data: "Dr. Smith has 98% success rate!" (50 procedures). Dr. Jones: 94% (5,000 procedures). Dr. Smith's 98% could easily be 90% with a few more patients. Dr. Jones's 94% is real.

Real damage: Viral statistics ("people who X earn 20% more!" from n=23) circulate widely. Decisions are made on percentages without asking "out of how many?"
When given a percentage, first question: "out of how many?" If they can't answer, the percentage is meaningless.
