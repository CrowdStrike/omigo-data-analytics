Causal Reasoning Errors

1. Correlation ≠ Causation

Two variables moving together does not establish that one causes the other. The most common trap: a hidden third variable (confound) drives both, creating the illusion of a direct relationship.

Family Dinners & Academic Performance

Children who eat dinner with their family nightly score higher on standardized tests. Should policy mandate family dinners?

No — parents who prioritize family dinners also monitor homework, limit screen time, and maintain stable routines. Parental involvement is the confound. The dinner is merely a marker of engaged parenting.

Firefighter Count & Fire Damage

Cities with more firefighters report greater fire damage. Do firefighters cause fires?

No — larger cities have more of both: larger fire departments and more structures at risk. City size is the confound driving both variables upward.

Hospital Admissions & Mortality Rates

Data shows that people who visit hospitals have higher death rates than those who stay home. Do hospitals cause death?

No — severely ill people go to hospitals. The underlying health condition causes both the admission and the outcome. Sickness severity is the confound driving both variables.

Vitamin Supplements & Good Health

Adults who take daily vitamins have lower rates of heart disease and better overall health. Do vitamins directly cause the health boost?

No — people who buy vitamins generally have higher incomes, eat better food, exercise more, and have access to superior healthcare. Socioeconomic status and lifestyle are the true drivers. The vitamins are a marker of health-conscious affluence.

Asbestos Treatment & Poor Outcomes

Patients with lung damage who received a specific treatment showed worse outcomes than untreated patients. Is the treatment toxic?

No — doctors only gave the treatment to patients with extreme, life-threatening asbestos exposure. The severe exposure caused both the treatment decision and the poor outcome. This is confounding by indication — the severity that triggers intervention is the same variable that drives mortality.
The test: "If A were forced to change in isolation, would B change too?" Without that experimental control, causation cannot be claimed. Most "A causes B" headlines are driven by an unmeasured confound C.

2. Base-Rate Neglect in Co-occurrence

When two events coincide, the mind perceives a meaningful connection. The error: failing to account for the large number of opportunities for the coincidence to occur. Hits are remembered; misses are discarded.

Black Cat Crosses Your Path

A person encounters a black cat, then has a bad day. Over time, the association feels real: black cat = bad luck.

A person has ~100 "bad" moments per month and encounters cats ~15 times per month. Expected overlaps by chance: ~5/month. Only the coincidences are encoded — the 10 cat sightings followed by normal days and the 95 bad moments with no cat are instantly forgotten.

Targeted Ads After Conversations

A person is exposed to ~5,000 advertisements daily. They discuss or consider hundreds of products weekly. This creates millions of potential "matches" annually.

The 3 coincidental matches are noticed and remembered. The 4,997 irrelevant ads from that same day and the 200 discussed topics that never appeared as ads are disregarded. The overlap is mathematically inevitable.

Thunderstorms & Bad Outcomes

A person recalls that "something bad always happens during thunderstorms" — a car accident, a power outage ruining food, a cancelled event.

A region experiences ~60 thunderstorm days per year. Negative events occur ~200 times per year. Expected overlap: ~33 bad-event-on-storm-days annually. The emotional salience of storms makes coincidences hyper-memorable. The 27 storm days with nothing bad and the 167 bad events on clear days vanish from recall.

Retail Shopping & Impulse Regret

"Every time I go to Target, I overspend." The store is blamed as a causal agent of financial harm.

A person makes ~50 purchases per month across all channels. Target visits: ~4/month. Impulse purchases happen at a base rate of ~30% regardless of store. The 1-2 Target impulse buys are attributed to the store's "powers" while the 13 impulse purchases at other stores, online, and at gas stations receive no narrative attention.
The pattern: Selective recall of hits, systematic disregard of misses, and failure to compute the expected number of coincidences given the base rates involved.

3. Crediting the Wrong Active Ingredient

An intervention is introduced and outcomes improve. The most visible change receives full attribution. However, the actual drivers are often the invisible behavioral shifts that accompany it — yielding 80% of the benefit independently.

Gym Membership & Fitness Gains

The exercise itself receives full credit for the physical transformation.

The membership also triggered: early wake times (circadian reset), morning sunlight exposure, improved nutrition, consistent sleep schedule, increased hydration, and reduced alcohol intake. These behavioral changes independently account for the majority of the improvement.

Supplement & Energy Levels

The supplement is credited with restoring vitality.

Concurrent with starting the supplement: alcohol consumption decreased, sleep duration increased to 8 hours, food intake became tracked. The supplement functions as a placebo riding genuine lifestyle modifications.

Standing Desk & Back Pain Resolution

The desk is credited with eliminating chronic back pain.

The desk also prompted: regular break intervals, daily stretching, walking during lunch, and reduced sedentary time at home. The movement pattern changes account for approximately 85% of the relief — the desk surface itself contributes ~15%.

The test: Remove the headline intervention but retain the bundled behaviors. If the outcome persists, the attribution was incorrect.
The pattern: Attribution gravitates toward the most visible, expensive, or dramatic change — it provides the narrative. The quiet behavioral shifts that accompany it receive no credit because they are free, mundane, and feel automatic rather than deliberate.
