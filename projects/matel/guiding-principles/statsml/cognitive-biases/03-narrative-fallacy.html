Narrative Fallacy

Post-hoc causal story building around noise — compelling but unpredictive

The Trap

Constructing a compelling causal story around noise because the brain cannot tolerate "it's random" as an explanation. The story is written AFTER the outcome — it explains nothing.

  • Model accuracy improved after adding "time_of_day"
  • Team writes: "users are more impulsive at night!"
  • Actual cause: a pipeline bug duplicating evening records
  • The story was beautiful and completely wrong

Example: Three burglaries on Elm Street in one month — "serial burglar pattern!" With 200 streets, some street WILL have 3 in a month by pure chance. The cluster is expected, not meaningful.

Defense: Before building a causal story: does the CI exclude zero? Would I have predicted this BEFORE the data? Could randomness alone produce this?

Where It Strikes

The narrative fallacy thrives wherever an outcome is known and a cause is demanded. These are real patterns from business, ML, and science — each "explanation" ignores competing factors.

Pattern: The more confident the story sounds, the less likely it was tested against alternatives.
Narrative What data shows What was ignored
"Revenue grew because we launched X" Revenue up 12% post-launch 3 other changes shipped same sprint
"Model failed because data was dirty" Accuracy dropped on new data Architecture was wrong for the task
"Users left because of redesign" Churn rose after UI change Price increase same month
"Churn spiked after outage" Churn elevated post-incident Seasonal pattern every Q4
"Feature X drives conversions" Users of X convert 2x more Confounded with user tenure
"Team B outperforms due to manager" Team B ships faster Also has more senior engineers
"Drug works via mechanism Y" Drug effective in trial Mechanism unknown, correlation only
"Startup failed due to timing" Market was crowded at launch Product-market fit was never validated

Story vs Statistics

The brain prefers "A caused B" over "A and B co-occurred within noise." Given any correlation, humans will generate a causal narrative — and different humans will generate different, contradictory ones from the same data.

  • Story 1: "Experience drives salary" (X causes Y)
  • Story 2: "Higher-paid roles attract retention" (Y causes X)
  • Story 3: "Company tenure confounds both" (Z causes X and Y)

Same r = 0.42 scatter. Three teams, three "obvious" explanations. Zero causal tests run. All three stories feel true — at most one can be.

Rule: If you can construct 2+ contradictory causal stories from the same correlation, you have evidence for NONE of them.

Pipeline Defense

Force the pipeline to acknowledge uncertainty at every stage. No result passes without quantified doubt.

  • Effect size + CI: Not just "significant" — how large, and how uncertain?
  • Pre-registration: State your hypothesis BEFORE looking at data
  • Alternative explanations field: Mandatory — list 3 other causes
  • Multiple testing correction: If you tested 20 features, adjust for that
Principle: A pipeline that allows conclusions without confidence intervals is a story-generation machine, not a statistical tool.
