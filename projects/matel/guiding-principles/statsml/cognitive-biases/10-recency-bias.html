Recency Bias

One recent tick overwrites the full trend — the last price illusion

The Trap

The most recent data point overwrites your entire model of reality. Yesterday's number becomes "the truth" — even when it's one tick in a noisy stream.

  • ACME Corp drops 3% at 3:58pm on 200 shares
  • Monday headline: "ACME loses $80B!"
  • But that "price" was ONE transaction
  • The other 15 billion shares weren't involved

Example: Dashboard shows 2.1% conversion at 9am Monday — urgent Slack message. But Monday 9am is always low (people opening Friday tabs). By Tuesday it's back to 3.4%. The most recent reading overrode the trend.

Defense: Never react to a single data point. Ask: "What's the trend over the last 30 observations?" If you don't know, you're not informed — you're just startled.
