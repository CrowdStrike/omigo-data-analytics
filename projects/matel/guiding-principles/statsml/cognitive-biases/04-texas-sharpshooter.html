Texas Sharpshooter — Analysis Biases

Drawing the target after the bullets hit — looking at results first, then defining the hypothesis to match. You didn't predict the pattern; you found it by scanning everything.

Subgroup Mining

A/B test fails overall. Slice by 20 dimensions — "Women aged 35-44 in the Midwest show +12%!" That's 1 of ~200 possible subgroups. At alpha=0.05, you expect 10 significant results BY CHANCE.

In data: Town finds 8 cancer cases on one block — "contamination!" But with millions of blocks and random incidence, SOME block will have 8 purely by chance. The cluster is the target painted around random bullet holes.

Real damage: Exploratory findings get reported as confirmatory. One-time coincidences become product strategy. False discoveries consume resources.
Pre-register your hypothesis. If you didn't predict it BEFORE looking, it's exploratory — validate on holdout data before claiming discovery.
