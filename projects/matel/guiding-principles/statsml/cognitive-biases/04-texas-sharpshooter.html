Texas Sharpshooter

Drawing the target after the bullets hit — subgroup mining without pre-registration

The Trap

Looking at results first, then defining the hypothesis to match. You didn't predict the pattern; you found it by scanning everything.

  • A/B test fails overall
  • Slice by 20 dimensions — "Women aged 35-44 in the Midwest show +12%!"
  • That's 1 of ~200 possible subgroups
  • At alpha=0.05, you expect 10 significant results BY CHANCE

Example: Town finds 8 cancer cases on one block — "contamination!" But with millions of blocks and random incidence, SOME block will have 8 purely by chance. The cluster is the target painted around random bullet holes.

Defense: Pre-register your hypothesis. If you didn't predict it BEFORE looking, it's exploratory — validate on holdout data before claiming discovery.

The Arithmetic

With each test at α = 0.05, the expected number of false positives grows linearly with tests conducted:

  • 1 test: 0.05 expected false positives
  • 20 tests: 1 expected false positive
  • 100 tests: 5 expected false positives
  • 100 variables (4,950 pairs): ~248 "significant" correlations by pure chance
Rule: Expected false positives = number of tests × α. With enough tests, you WILL find "significance" in pure noise.

Pipeline Defense

Four defenses against post-hoc target drawing:

  • Pre-registration: State your hypothesis before touching the data
  • Split before selection: Explore on one half, confirm on the other
  • Bonferroni / FDR correction: Adjust α for the number of tests actually run
  • Cross-validation: Never evaluate on the data used to find the pattern
The test: "Did I choose what to test BEFORE or AFTER seeing the data?" If after, it's exploration — not confirmation.
