Availability Bias

Loudest signal vs largest signal — vividness overrides frequency

The Trap

Whatever comes to mind easily feels more common. Vivid, dramatic, or recent events dominate your sense of probability — even when they're extremely rare.

  • One customer writes a scathing email to the CEO
  • Entire engineering team spends a week on it
  • Meanwhile, 500 customers quietly churned from slow page loads
  • No email, no drama, no urgency for the bigger problem

Example: Shark attacks vs vending machines. People fear sharks; nobody fears vending machines. But vending machines kill more annually. Fear calibration is driven by storytelling, not statistics.

Defense: Look at the data, not your memory. Your memory is a highlight reel, not a representative sample. Ask: "am I reacting to frequency or to vividness?"
