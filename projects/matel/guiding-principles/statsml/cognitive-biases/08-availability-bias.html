Availability Bias

Whatever comes to mind easily feels more common. Vivid, dramatic, or recent events dominate your sense of probability — even when they're extremely rare.

Loudest Signal vs Largest Signal

One customer writes a scathing email to the CEO — entire engineering team spends a week on it. Meanwhile, 500 customers quietly churned from slow page loads — no email, no drama, no urgency.

In data: Shark attacks vs vending machines. People fear sharks; nobody fears vending machines. But vending machines kill more annually. Sharks have movies and Discovery Channel. Fear calibration is driven by storytelling, not statistics.

Real damage: Priorities get set by the loudest signal, not the largest signal. The most dramatic anecdote overwrites the aggregate.
Look at the data, not your memory. Your memory is a highlight reel, not a representative sample. Ask: "am I reacting to frequency or to vividness?"
