6. Gradient Boosting (XGBoost/LightGBM)

Assumptions & What Breaks
  • Smooth loss landscape: gradient descent assumes smoothness. Noisy features create jagged loss surfaces.
  • No data leakage: later trees overfit to artifacts of earlier trees without careful regularization.
  • Sufficient data: more prone to overfitting than RF on small datasets. Residuals get noisier with depth.
  • Stationarity: assumes future data from same distribution. Concept drift breaks model silently.
  • Violation: boosting AMPLIFIES noise because it focuses on hard examples (which may be mislabeled or noisy).
  • Violation: near-perfect training accuracy but poor generalization; later trees memorize residual noise.
  • Verify: early stopping on validation, learning curves (train vs val gap), SHAP for post-hoc explainability.
  • Alternative: statistical validation per split. Drift detection via distribution monitoring.

Real example: XGBoost on 500 samples with 200 features. Training AUC=0.99, validation AUC=0.61. Trees 50-300 are memorizing noise in hard examples that are actually mislabeled.
