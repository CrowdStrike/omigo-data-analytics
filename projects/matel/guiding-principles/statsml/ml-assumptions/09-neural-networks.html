9. Neural Networks

Assumptions & What Breaks
  • Large sample size: need orders of magnitude more data than parameters. 1000-param network needs 10K+ samples.
  • Feature scaling: unscaled inputs cause vanishing/exploding gradients. Must be ~N(0,1) or [0,1].
  • Smooth manifold: nearby inputs should produce nearby outputs. Discontinuous boundaries are hard to learn.
  • No class imbalance: optimizes for majority class. 99/1 split learns "always predict majority" = 99% accuracy.
  • Appropriate architecture: no theory for choosing. Too small = underfits. Too large = overfits.
  • Violation: small data = memorizes training set. Validation loss diverges while training loss drops to zero.
  • Verify: learning curves, feature scaling confirmed, class-weighted loss for imbalance.
  • Alternative: for tabular data < 10K rows, gradient boosting typically wins. For explainability, not suitable.

Real example: MLP on 800 patient records with 50 features. Training accuracy: 98%. Test accuracy: 54%. Network memorized every training patient by their unique feature combination.
