13. EM / Gaussian Mixture Models

Iteratively estimates latent cluster assignments and Gaussian parameters — soft clustering with probabilistic assignments
  • Assumes Gaussian components: Each cluster is modeled as a multivariate Gaussian. Non-Gaussian subpopulations (uniform blobs, skewed groups, ring-shaped clusters) are poorly fit — EM uses multiple Gaussians to approximate, wasting components.
  • Assumes correct number of components (K): Like K-means, must specify K. Too few → merges distinct groups. Too many → splits coherent groups. BIC/AIC help but aren't definitive.
  • Sensitive to initialization: EM finds local optima, not global. Different starting points → different solutions. Can converge to degenerate solutions where one component collapses to a single point (variance → 0).
  • Assumes sufficient data per component: Each Gaussian needs enough points to estimate its mean AND full covariance matrix. With d features, need at least d(d+1)/2 + d parameters per component. 10 features = 65 params per cluster.
  • Covariance estimation can fail: Singular covariance matrices when component has fewer points than dimensions. Regularization (diagonal, tied, or spherical covariance) helps but changes the model's expressiveness.
  • Soft assignments are a strength AND weakness: Points get probability of belonging to each cluster (not hard assignment). Great for overlapping populations. But makes "which cluster does this point belong to?" ambiguous when probabilities are near 50/50.
  • Violation: Fitting GMM to income data that's actually right_skew + a spike at zero (unemployed). EM uses 3-4 Gaussians to approximate the right_skew tail, "discovering" fake subpopulations that are artifacts of the wrong distributional assumption.
  • Real example: Cholesterol with two modes (normal ~200, high ~280). GMM with K=2 correctly finds both. But with K=3 it "discovers" a third group at ~240 that's just the overlap region — not a real subpopulation. BIC says K=2 but only barely.
  • Alternatives: Non-Gaussian clusters → kernel density estimation, DBSCAN. Unknown K → DPGMM (Dirichlet Process — auto-selects K). High-dimensional → factor analyzers or mixture of factor analyzers. Singular covariance → regularized GMM or diagonal constraint.
