Pitfall: Label Noise (Incorrect Ground Truth)

When the target variable itself is wrong for 5-20% of records, capping model performance and misleading evaluation.

The Problem

The labels used for training and evaluation are systematically or randomly incorrect. Human annotators disagree, upstream data bugs propagate, proxy labels approximate the true outcome but are noisy.

  • Human annotation: Medical diagnosis labels with 10-15% inter-rater disagreement; content moderation with subjective boundaries
  • Proxy labels: "Clicked ad" as proxy for "purchase intent"; "watched 30 seconds" as proxy for "liked video"
  • Pipeline bugs: Join errors, off-by-one bugs, timezone issues corrupt 5% of labels; no validation
Impact: Performance ceiling at 85% AUC when 15% of labels are wrong; model learns the noise not the signal. Detection: Compare model predictions to human expert review on a sample; measure inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's kappa); audit label distribution for impossible values.

Example: A fraud detection model uses "transaction_refunded" as the label for fraud. But 20% of refunds are legitimate returns, not fraud. The model learns that "expensive electronics purchase" is fraud, harming legitimate customers. True performance is unknowable without clean labels.

Why It Happens

  • Annotator disagreement (15-30%): Subjective tasks like sentiment, toxicity, or medical diagnosis have inherent ambiguity — different annotators label the same example differently
  • Proxy labels: Using "clicked" as proxy for "purchase intent" or "refunded" as proxy for "fraud" — approximations that systematically mislabel 10-20% of cases
  • Time-dependent definitions: Fraud criteria change yearly, content policies evolve quarterly — labels from 2022 may be wrong by 2024 standards
  • Pipeline bugs: Join errors, timezone mismatches, or off-by-one bugs silently corrupt 5-10% of labels with no validation check
Core issue: Teams assume labels are ground truth. But "ground truth" is often a noisy approximation — and nobody measures how noisy.

The Correct Approach

  • Estimate noise rate: Compute inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's kappa, Krippendorff's alpha) — if kappa < 0.6, your labels are too noisy for standard training
  • Noise-robust loss functions: Use symmetric cross-entropy, forward correction, or confident learning to down-weight likely-mislabeled examples
  • Multiple annotations: Get 3-5 labels per example, use majority vote or soft labels (probability distributions) instead of hard 0/1
  • Audit before training: Sample 500 examples, have an expert relabel, compute agreement with existing labels — reject dataset if noise > 15%
Principle: Treat label quality as a measurable property with acceptance criteria. No model can overcome garbage labels — measure and fix the labels first.
