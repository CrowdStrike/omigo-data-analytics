z-test / Proportion test

Tests whether an observed proportion differs from a hypothesized value

Overview

What it measures

Whether an observed proportion differs significantly from a hypothesized value (or two proportions differ).

Key assumptions
  • np ≥ 10 and n(1-p) ≥ 10 (normal approximation to binomial)
  • Independent observations (no clustering)
  • Fixed sample size (not sequential)
  • Simple random sampling
What breaks when violated
  • Small n: confidence intervals are too wide OR too narrow (asymmetric binomial)
  • np < 10: normal approximation fails; actual coverage of CI drops below nominal
  • Clustering: effective n is smaller than apparent n, inflating significance
Bucket purity test: 12 out of 15 positive (80%).
z-test CI: [0.55, 1.05] — upper bound exceeds 1.0 (impossible!)
Exact binomial CI: [0.52, 0.96] — valid bounds
np = 15 * 0.5 = 7.5 < 10: normal approximation invalid.
Use instead: Exact binomial test (Fisher) when n < 30 or np < 10. Wilson score interval for confidence intervals at any n. Bayesian posterior (Beta-Binomial) for sequential updates.

Real-World Examples

🚀 Feature launch: Crash rate monitoring
After shipping a mobile app update, the crash team sees 3 crashes out of 18 sessions in the first hour. They run a z-test against the baseline rate of 2% — it says p=0.001, triggering a rollback. But np=18×0.02=0.36, far below the threshold of 10. The exact binomial test gives p=0.06 with a Wilson CI of [4%, 40%]. The "crisis" was just small-sample noise — waiting 2 more hours (n=200, 5 crashes) confirmed the rate was 2.5%, indistinguishable from baseline.
📊 Survey research: Voter preference polling
A political poll of 25 likely voters in a rural district shows 72% favor candidate A. The z-test CI is [54%, 90%]. But with np=18 and n(1-p)=7 < 10, the normal approximation is invalid for the minority. Wilson score CI: [51%, 87%] — note the asymmetry that z-test misses. The 3-point difference at the lower bound means the race could actually be competitive, which the symmetric z-interval obscures.
