Ad Tracking Pixels

Invisible 1x1 images hidden on web pages that tell Facebook and Google you were here.

What is it?

A tiny invisible image — literally 1 pixel. It loads from Facebook or Google's servers when you visit a page. Your browser asks for the image. That request tells the ad company exactly which page you're on and who you are.
What does it collect?
  • Which pages you visit on other people's websites
  • Whether you bought something after seeing an ad
  • Your Facebook/Google account linked to browsing
  • Timestamps of every page with a pixel
  • Whether you added items to cart but didn't buy
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: It helps show you more relevant ads instead of random ones.

What they don't tell you: It builds a complete map of everywhere you go online. Advertisers bid on you specifically in real-time auctions. It creates a shadow profile even if you never signed up.
How much can they see?

They follow you across millions of websites. Every site with a Facebook Like button or Google ad has these pixels. They know your shopping habits, news reading, medical searches, dating activity — everything on any page with their pixel.

Intrusiveness: Very High. Cross-site surveillance at massive scale.
