Bluetooth Beacon Scanning

Your phone, AirPods, and watch constantly broadcast "I'm here!" signals — and receivers in malls, airports, and stores are always listening.

What is it?

Every Bluetooth device (phone, earbuds, smartwatch, fitness band) sends out tiny radio advertisements many times per second. These say "I exist" along with a unique ID. Stores, malls, and airports install cheap receivers that pick up these signals to count people, measure foot traffic, and track how you walk through a building.
What does it collect?
  • Your device's Bluetooth ID (changes periodically but can be correlated)
  • Signal strength (calculates your distance from receiver)
  • Time of entry and exit from each zone
  • Walking path through a building
  • How long you stood at each display or store
  • Which devices travel together (identifying groups and families)
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: Better store layouts. Shorter wait times. Improved customer experience.

What they don't tell you: They sell foot traffic data to hedge funds betting on retail stocks. They measure how many people walked past an ad. They identify "loyal" vs "one-time" visitors by recognizing returning devices.
How much can they see?

They see your entire journey through a space. Where you entered, every turn you made, where you paused, how long you browsed, and when you left. If you carry the same device daily, they recognize you across multiple visits. They know you came 3 Tuesdays in a row.

Intrusiveness: High. Always broadcasting. You cannot stop it without turning Bluetooth completely off — which breaks your headphones.
