Store Facial Recognition

Cameras in stores match your face against databases in real time. They call it "theft prevention" but it profiles every person who walks in. Age, gender, mood, repeat visits — all logged.

What is it?

Cameras at store entrances scan your face and match it against a database. Originally sold as a way to catch known shoplifters. Now it tracks every customer — how often you visit, where you walk in the store, how long you browse, your estimated age and gender.
What does it collect?
  • Your face as a biometric template
  • Timestamps of every visit
  • How long you stay each time
  • Which aisles you walk through
  • Estimated age, gender, and ethnicity
  • Emotional state (smile detection)
  • Whether you visited other stores in the same chain
  • Who you came in with
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: "To prevent theft and improve store safety."

What they don't tell you: They build a profile of every shopper's habits. They test which displays attract which demographics. They share data between stores. Some systems flag people as "suspicious" based on appearance alone.
How much can they see?

They recognize you even if you never gave your name. They track you across every location in the chain. They know your shopping frequency, time per visit, and movement patterns. All without asking permission.
