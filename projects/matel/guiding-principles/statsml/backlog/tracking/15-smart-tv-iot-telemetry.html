Smart TV Spying

Your TV firmware uses audio fingerprinting to identify what's on screen. Then it reports back to the manufacturer.

What is it?

Your smart TV listens to what you watch. The firmware captures short audio samples from whatever is playing — Netflix, cable, gaming, even Blu-rays. It matches these samples against a database to identify the exact content. Then it reports back to the manufacturer what you watched and when.

This runs silently in the background. You never see it happen. There is no blinking light. The TV just quietly phones home.
What does it collect?
  • What show or movie is on screen at any moment
  • What channel you switched to
  • How long you watched before switching
  • Whether you paused or fast-forwarded through ads
  • What apps you use on the TV
  • Your viewing schedule patterns
They don't just know what you watch. They know how you watch. Every pause, skip, and channel flip is logged.
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: "To recommend content you'll enjoy."

What they don't tell you: They sell your viewing data to advertisers who then target you on other devices. They know which ads you skipped. Content deals are negotiated based on actual viewership they track.

Your TV cost less than it should. The discount is subsidized by selling your attention data.
How much can they see?

They know you watched a horror movie at 1am alone. They know you binged 6 episodes without stopping. They know you watch the news every morning at 7. They know which streaming service you use most.

Some TVs have cameras and mics that can detect how many people are in the room.

Your TV builds a behavioral profile richer than your browser history. And it runs 24/7 in your living room.
