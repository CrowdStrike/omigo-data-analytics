DNA & Genetic Data

23andMe and Ancestry have your DNA. They share it with researchers, sell it to drug companies, and police can access it. You can't change your DNA. You can't take it back.

What is it?

Spit-in-a-tube genetic testing services. You pay them to learn about your ancestry or health risks. In return, they own a digital copy of your most personal, unchangeable information.
What does it collect?
  • Full genetic sequence
  • Health predispositions — cancer risk, Alzheimer's, heart disease
  • Ancestry and ethnicity breakdown
  • Family connections — who else tested
  • Physical traits — eye color, hair type predictions
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: "To help you learn about yourself."

What they don't tell you: The real business is selling aggregated genetic data to pharmaceutical companies for billions. Police can subpoena it to identify your relatives.
How much can they see?

Everything. Your DNA reveals disease risk, ancestry, family secrets — unknown siblings, paternity surprises — and it exposes your relatives who never consented to testing. Once submitted, it's forever. You can't change your DNA.
