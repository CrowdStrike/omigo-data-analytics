Stock Trading & Order Flow

Every trade is recorded. Robinhood sells your orders to Citadel before execution. High-frequency traders see what you are buying and trade ahead of you.

What is it?

When you buy stock on Robinhood or similar "free" trading apps, your order does not go directly to the market. It is sold to big trading firms like Citadel who can see what regular people are buying milliseconds before executing the trade. They profit from the information in your order.

This is called "payment for order flow." The app is free because your trading data is the product being sold.
What does it collect?
  • Every buy and sell order with exact timing (to the millisecond)
  • Your position sizes and portfolio composition
  • Your trading patterns and frequency
  • Which stocks you add to your watchlist
  • When you panic-sell during market drops
  • Your reaction time to breaking news
  • How much money you have invested total
  • Your risk tolerance (inferred from behavior)
  • How often you check the app
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: "Commission-free trading to democratize the markets. Everyone deserves access to investing."

What they don't tell you: You ARE the product. Your orders are sold for billions to firms that trade against you. The "free" app makes money by giving faster traders a peek at your moves before they execute. Citadel paid Robinhood $2.6 billion in a single year for this access.

If the product is free, you are the product. Your trades are worth more sold to high-frequency firms than any commission you would have paid.
How much can they see?

Every single trade, every watchlist add, every time you open the app to check prices. They know your risk tolerance, your emotional reactions to market drops, and exactly how much money you have.

High-frequency firms use this to front-run millions of small trades. They buy the stock you want milliseconds before your order executes, then sell it to you at a slightly higher price. Multiply that tiny margin by millions of trades per day.

The speed difference is measured in microseconds. You will never see it happen. The price you get looks normal — but it is slightly worse than it would be without the middleman.
