Session Replay / Screen Recording

Companies record your entire browser session as video — every scroll, click, hesitation, and typo — then someone watches it back.

What is it?

Tools like FullStory, Hotjar, and LogRocket embed code in websites that records everything you do as a replayable video. Mouse movements, scrolls, clicks, form typing, hesitations — all captured. An analyst can literally press play and watch you use the website, like a security camera for your screen.
What does it collect?
  • Every mouse movement and click location
  • Everything typed into forms (sometimes including passwords)
  • How long you stare at each section
  • Every scroll up, down, and back
  • What you almost clicked but didn't
  • Your screen size, browser, and device info
  • Rage clicks (frustrated rapid clicking)
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: "To improve user experience and fix bugs."

What they don't tell you: They watch individual sessions to understand hesitation and confusion. Marketing teams use it to optimize manipulation — finding exactly where you almost left but didn't. Some accidentally record passwords and health information in form fields. Your "private" browsing session has an audience.
How much can they see?

Everything on the page. It is literally a video recording of your screen while you use the site. They see your mouse shake with indecision. They see you type a credit card number. They see you re-read the fine print three times. They see you almost click "unsubscribe" but stop. Every micro-behavior is captured.
