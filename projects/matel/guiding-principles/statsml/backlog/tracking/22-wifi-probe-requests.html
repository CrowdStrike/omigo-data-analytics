22. WiFi Probe Requests

Your phone shouts the names of every WiFi network it remembers — and anyone nearby can listen.

What is it?

When your phone's WiFi is on but not connected, it broadcasts "probe requests" — short radio signals asking "Is [network name] here?" for every saved network. This happens constantly. Your phone announces your home WiFi name, your office WiFi, the hotel you stayed at, the coffee shop you visit. Anyone with a cheap receiver can hear these broadcasts and log them.

You don't need to connect to WiFi to be tracked. Having WiFi turned on is enough.
What does it collect?
  • MAC address of your device (hardware ID)
  • List of network names (SSIDs) your phone is looking for
  • Signal strength (estimates distance)
  • Timestamp of each probe
  • Device type (from MAC prefix — Apple, Samsung, etc.)
  • Frequency of probes (how long you stay nearby)
Your saved network list is a fingerprint. "HomeWiFi_Smith, Hilton_NYC, Starbucks_Main" identifies you uniquely even without your name.
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: Retail stores use it to count foot traffic. Malls measure how long shoppers stay. Cities plan pedestrian flow. Conference venues track attendance.

What they don't tell you: They track you across visits without your knowledge. They sell movement patterns to advertisers. They can tell if you visited a competitor's store. Some systems correlate your phone's MAC with your identity when you log into their WiFi once — then track you forever after.
How much can they see?

A single receiver in a doorway knows every phone that passed and how long it lingered. A network of receivers across a mall tracks your exact path — which stores you entered, how long you browsed, which displays you stopped at. Your saved WiFi names reveal where you live, where you work, what hotels you visit, and what countries you travel to. All from passive radio listening.

Modern phones randomize MAC addresses to fight this. But if you connect to WiFi even once, the real MAC is revealed and linked to all your randomized ones.
