Traffic Cameras (Pure Video)

Government cameras at every major intersection record continuous video of everything passing by — stored for days or weeks.

What is it?

City and state departments install cameras at traffic intersections. They record continuous video footage — every car, pedestrian, and cyclist that passes. The footage is stored for days to weeks on local servers. No real-time analysis. Just raw recording that can be retrieved later if needed for an investigation.
What does it collect?
  • Continuous video of all traffic at the intersection
  • Every vehicle that passes (make, model, color visible)
  • Pedestrians and cyclists
  • Timestamps for every frame
  • License plates (readable if close enough)
  • Patterns: traffic volume, peak hours
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: Traffic management and accident investigation.

What they don't tell you: Police retrieve footage retroactively to track suspects' routes. Footage can reconstruct anyone's travel path by combining multiple cameras. Private companies sometimes get access for commercial research. There's rarely any policy limiting how long footage is kept.
How much can they see?

At each camera: a full view of the intersection. Cars are clearly identifiable. Pedestrians less so. Combined across dozens of cameras on a route, your entire journey can be reconstructed after the fact. Not real-time, but permanent record.

Intrusiveness: Medium-High. Passive recording. Not targeted. But retrievable retroactively, which means your past movements are never truly gone.
