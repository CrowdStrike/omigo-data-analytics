Store WiFi & Bluetooth Beacons

Sensors in stores detecting phone MAC addresses for foot traffic, dwell time, and path.

What is it?

Stores install small wireless sensors throughout the building. Your phone constantly broadcasts signals looking for WiFi and Bluetooth connections. These sensors pick up those signals and track your phone's movement through the store.

Your phone shouts "anyone there?" every few seconds. The store listens and triangulates your position from multiple sensors.
What does it collect?
  • Your phone's unique hardware ID (MAC address)
  • Which aisle you walked through
  • How long you stood in front of each display
  • Your walking path through the store
  • How often you visit
  • Whether you entered but left without buying
They don't need you to connect to WiFi. Your phone broadcasts its identity just by having WiFi turned on.
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: "To improve store layout and customer experience."

What they don't tell you:

  • They sell foot traffic data to landlords, brands, and hedge funds
  • They test whether moving a display changes dwell time
  • They know you visited three times before buying
Hedge funds buy mall foot traffic data to predict quarterly earnings before they're announced.
How much can they see?
  • Your full path like a GPS trail on a map
  • You spent 4 minutes at shoes but only 10 seconds at socks
  • They recognize repeat visitors across days and weeks
  • Which store window display made you walk in
They see your hesitation. Three laps past the same shelf means you're deciding. That data gets sold.
