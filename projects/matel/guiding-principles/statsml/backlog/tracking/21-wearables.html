21. Apple Watch & Fitbit

A medical-grade sensor array strapped to your wrist, collecting body data 24 hours a day — even while you sleep.

What is it?

A wearable device with optical heart rate sensors, accelerometers, gyroscopes, GPS, blood oxygen sensors, skin temperature sensors, and microphones. It measures your body continuously and syncs data to cloud servers. It knows when you are awake, asleep, stressed, exercising, or sitting still. It never takes a break.

Your phone tracks where you go. Your watch tracks what your body does at every moment.
What does it collect?
  • Heart rate — every 5 seconds, 24/7
  • Steps, distance, elevation climbed
  • Sleep stages — light, deep, REM, awake periods
  • Blood oxygen saturation (SpO2)
  • Skin temperature variations
  • GPS location during workouts (and sometimes always)
  • Wrist motion patterns (can infer typing, gestures)
  • Menstrual cycle tracking (if enabled)
  • ECG readings (irregular heart rhythm detection)
  • Fall detection events
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: Health insights. Fitness goals. Early heart problem detection. Better sleep. Emergency fall alerts.

What they don't tell you: Insurance companies want this data to price premiums. Employers use aggregate wellness data to assess workforce health costs. Advertisers infer stress, mood, and activity to target you. The data can reveal pregnancy, illness, substance use, or mental health conditions before you tell anyone.
How much can they see?

Your resting heart rate reveals stress and fitness level. Heart rate spikes reveal arguments, fear, excitement, and sexual activity. Sleep data shows insomnia, alcohol use, and shift patterns. GPS plus heart rate together show exactly what you were doing, where, and how your body reacted. Across months, it detects health deterioration before your doctor does.

You wear it voluntarily. You charge it every night. You opted into the most intimate surveillance device ever built.
