WiFi Signals as Radar

Your router's radio waves bounce off people and walls — and by reading the echoes, it can detect movement, count people, even monitor breathing.

What is it?

Your WiFi router sends radio waves in all directions. When people move through a room, those waves get disrupted. New technology reads these disruptions like radar. It can tell if someone walked across a room, how many people are home, and even detect breathing patterns. No cameras. No microphones. Just your existing WiFi signal.
What does it collect?
  • Movement detection through walls
  • Number of people in a room
  • Breathing rate and sleep patterns
  • General body position (sitting, standing, lying down)
  • Which rooms are occupied and when
  • Entry and exit times
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: Smart home automation. Fall detection for elderly. Energy savings by knowing occupied rooms.

What they don't tell you: Insurance companies want to know if you live alone. It can detect multiple occupants in "single person" units. Law enforcement can use it for surveillance without a camera warrant.
How much can they see?

They can see through walls. They know which rooms you use, when you sleep, if someone else is over. They can detect falls or unusual stillness. The resolution is getting better every year. Current systems can distinguish between a person and a pet.

Intrusiveness: Very High. Sees through walls. No camera to cover. No light to notice. Completely invisible surveillance.
