WiFi Location Databases

Google and Apple mapped every WiFi router on Earth — now your phone uses them as invisible GPS satellites to locate you indoors.

What is it?

Google Street View cars and Apple devices quietly recorded the location of every WiFi router they passed. Now when your phone sees a WiFi signal, it checks this massive database to figure out where you are — even indoors where GPS doesn't work. Your home router helps locate strangers walking past your house.
What does it collect?
  • Your router's unique ID (BSSID) and its GPS coordinates
  • Signal strength from your phone to nearby routers
  • Your calculated indoor position (within 1-3 meters)
  • Movement paths inside buildings (floor by floor)
  • Which stores/offices you visit inside a mall
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: Indoor navigation. Find your friends in a mall. Better Maps directions inside airports.

What they don't tell you: Retailers pay for foot traffic heatmaps. Your router is used to track others without your permission. They can place you inside specific rooms of buildings.
How much can they see?

They know which aisle of a store you're in. Which floor of a building. How long you lingered at a specific shelf. Indoors, this is more accurate than GPS. Your home router participates in tracking others whether you like it or not.

Intrusiveness: High. Your router is drafted into a surveillance network you never agreed to join.
