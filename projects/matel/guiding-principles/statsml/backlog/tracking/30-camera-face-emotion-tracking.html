Face & Emotion Tracking

Cameras in stores, billboards, and your phone estimate your age, gender, mood, and identity — just from looking at your face.

What is it?

Cameras powered by AI that look at your face and guess who you are, how old you are, what gender you are, and how you're feeling. Used in stores to count visitors and guess demographics. Smart billboards change ads based on who's looking. Your phone uses it for Face ID. Some companies claim they can read emotions from micro-expressions — happy, sad, confused, angry.
What does it collect?
  • Face geometry (distance between eyes, nose shape, jaw line)
  • Estimated age range and gender
  • Claimed emotional state (happy, neutral, angry, surprised)
  • Whether you've been seen before (re-identification)
  • Attention direction (are you looking at the display?)
  • Dwell time in front of products/ads
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: Security. Unlock your phone faster. Personalized experiences. Counting store visitors.

What they don't tell you: Advertisers adjust prices and content based on your apparent mood. Stores track which demographics browse which sections. Emotion AI is used in job interviews to "score" candidates. Police use it for surveillance without consent.
How much can they see?

They build a mathematical map of your face. They claim to know if you're happy or frustrated. They can recognize you across multiple visits without you knowing. Some systems work from 30+ feet away. The emotion detection is scientifically questionable, but companies sell it anyway and make decisions based on it.

Intrusiveness: Very High. No consent asked. Can't opt out of having a face. Emotion claims are pseudoscience applied at scale.
