19. Red Light & Speed Cameras

They photograph every car that passes — not just the ones breaking the law.

What is it?

Cameras mounted at intersections and along roads. They use OCR to read license plates and radar or loops to measure speed. They fire for every vehicle, not just violators. The system stores a full record whether you get a ticket or not.

The ticket is the visible output. The database of all plates passing through is the invisible one.
What does it collect?
  • License plate number (OCR from image)
  • Timestamp — exact second you passed
  • Vehicle speed at that moment
  • Lane position and direction of travel
  • Photo of the vehicle (front and/or rear)
  • Vehicle color, make, model (from image)
  • Location of the camera (GPS coordinates)
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: To catch red-light runners and speeders. To reduce accidents. To make roads safer.

What they don't tell you: The system logs every plate that passes. Police can query "where was plate ABC-1234 at 3pm Tuesday?" for any car, any time. Cities sell aggregate traffic data. Insurance companies buy speed pattern data. The retention period is often years, not days.
How much can they see?

A single camera sees one intersection. A network of cameras across a city builds a movement map. They know your commute pattern, what time you leave home, which route you take, and when you deviate. Combined with plate-to-owner databases, they know exactly who drove where and when — for everyone, always.

You don't need to break any law to be tracked. Driving on a public road is enough.
