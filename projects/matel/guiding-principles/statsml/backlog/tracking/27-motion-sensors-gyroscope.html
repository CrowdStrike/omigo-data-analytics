27. Phone Motion Sensors

Your phone's accelerometer and gyroscope reveal what you're doing — and websites could read them silently.

What is it?

Your phone has tiny motion sensors built in. The accelerometer detects movement and tilt. The gyroscope detects rotation.

Together they know exactly how your phone is oriented and moving in 3D space. Every shake, tilt, step, and turn.

Until 2019, websites could read this data silently. No permission popup. No notification. The browser just handed it over to any page you visited.

No permission was required. Any website could silently read your phone's motion data just by you visiting the page.
What does it collect?
  • Walking rhythm and gait pattern
  • Driving vs sitting vs lying down
  • Typing patterns and speed
  • Whether phone is in hand or on table
  • Orientation changes (portrait/landscape)
  • Vibration from nearby machinery
  • Steps, pace, direction changes
  • Whether you're on stairs or elevator
The raw sensor stream reveals your physical activity in real time — without GPS, without camera, without microphone.
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you:

  • Better gaming experience (tilt controls)
  • Auto-rotate the screen
  • Step counting and fitness
  • Shake to undo

What they don't tell you:

  • Detect if you're actually watching an ad
  • Fingerprint your unique walking pattern
  • Determine your transportation mode
  • Tell if you're asleep or distracted
  • Infer if phone is shared between people
How much can they see?

They can distinguish walking from running from driving. They know when you pick up your phone. They can detect your typing rhythm.

Combined across visits, your gait becomes a fingerprint. Your walking pattern is as unique as your face.

Researchers have shown motion data alone can identify individuals with over 90% accuracy.

Intrusiveness: Medium-High. Silent collection. No permission needed until 2019 in most browsers. Still available to native apps with minimal permissions.
