Robotics & Warehouse Tracking

Amazon warehouse robots track every worker's movement, break time, pick speed, and bathroom visits. The robot is the manager — and it never looks away.

What is it?

In large warehouses, robots and scanners track every single action a worker takes. How fast they pick items, how long they rest, how far they walk, how many seconds between scans. The system automatically flags "underperformers" and can fire people without a human manager involved.
What does it collect?
  • Items picked per hour
  • Time between scans ("time off task")
  • Walking speed and route efficiency
  • Bathroom break frequency and duration
  • Exact location at all times via scanner
  • Error rate per shift
  • Fatigue patterns over multiple shifts
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: "To optimize warehouse operations and improve delivery speed."

What they don't tell you: The system is designed to push workers to their physical limits. It automatically generates termination notices. It treats bathroom breaks as lost productivity. Workers are ranked against each other every hour.
How much can they see?

Every second is tracked. Workers say they can't pause for even 30 seconds without being flagged. The robots set the pace — a human who can't keep up gets a warning, then gets fired by an algorithm. No human reviews the decision.
