Cookies

Small files websites store in your browser to remember you — or to let ad companies follow you everywhere.

What is it?

Tiny text files that websites save on your computer. First-party cookies are helpful — they keep you logged in and remember your cart. Third-party cookies are from ad companies. They track you across every website you visit.
What does it collect?
  • Your login state and preferences (first-party)
  • A unique ID that follows you across websites (third-party)
  • Your shopping and browsing history across the internet
  • Which ads you've seen and clicked
  • How many times you've visited a site
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: Cookies improve your experience and keep you logged in.

What they don't tell you: Third-party cookies build a complete dossier of your online life. That profile gets auctioned off to advertisers in real-time bidding — hundreds of times per day.
How much can they see?

First-party cookies only work on one site — mostly harmless. Third-party cookies see you across thousands of sites. They know you researched divorce lawyers, then baby clothes, then antidepressants — and sell that profile to anyone willing to pay.

Intrusiveness: Low (first-party) to Very High (third-party).
