Ring & Nest Cameras

Doorbell cameras and home security systems record everything — and share it with police and neighbors without telling you.

What is it?

Ring doorbells and Nest cameras record video 24/7 or on motion triggers. The footage goes to cloud storage owned by Amazon or Google. Ring's "Neighbors" app creates a surveillance network across your street. Police can request footage without the owner's consent through special partnerships.
What does it collect?
  • Video of everyone who walks past your house
  • Audio from conversations near the door
  • Motion detection events with timestamps
  • Faces of delivery drivers, neighbors, visitors
  • License plates of parked and passing cars
  • Your coming-and-going schedule
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: Home security and package theft prevention.

What they don't tell you: Ring has partnerships with 2,000+ police departments. Neighbors who didn't buy a Ring still get filmed. Amazon uses the video data for computer vision AI training. The "neighborhood watch" aspect creates a surveillance network that covers entire blocks.
How much can they see?

Your front door, driveway, sidewalk — 24 hours a day. They see everyone who visits you. They know when you leave for work. They know when your kids come home. The Neighbors network means one camera covers far more than one house.

Intrusiveness: Very High. Records people who never consented (neighbors, pedestrians). Shared with police. Creates permanent video records of daily life.
