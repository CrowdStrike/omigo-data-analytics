Dissecting Popular Metrics BACKLOG

One card per metric: what it assumes, what it hides, and a concrete case where optimising it produces a worse system.

The Recurring Failure

Every metric on the list below fails the same way. It is a proxy that correlates with the thing you want, and the correlation holds until someone optimises against it.

  • CTR correlates with relevance — until headlines are written for clicks.
  • BLEU correlates with translation quality — until output is tuned for n-gram overlap.
  • Watch time correlates with satisfaction — until autoplay is tuned for duration.
The proxy does not break gradually. It holds, then decouples once optimisation pressure exceeds the slack in the correlation.

Card Structure

Each metric gets the same six fields, so cards stay comparable:

  • Formula — written out, with each term's assumption named.
  • Hidden assumption — what must hold for the number to mean anything.
  • Failure mode — a scenario where optimising it makes the system worse.
  • Distribution sensitivity — how skew, imbalance and outliers distort it.
  • Better alternative — what to reach for when it breaks.
  • One chart — the pathological case, drawn.
A metric is not wrong or right; it encodes a claim about what matters. The card should make that claim explicit enough to disagree with.
