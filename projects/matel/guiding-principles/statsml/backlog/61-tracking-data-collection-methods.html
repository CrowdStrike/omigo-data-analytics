Tracking Data Collection Methods BACKLOG

A catalog of how tracking data actually reaches the collector — the mechanism, who initiates it, and what makes each one visible or invisible to the user.

The Collection Surface

Tracking is not one thing. It differs on three axes:

  • Where it executes — server, browser, device OS, network edge
  • Who initiates it — the user's own action, a third-party script, an OS service, or the network itself
  • What consent gate exists — none, implied, opt-out buried in settings, explicit permission prompt
The same "click" can be captured by the site backend, a JS tag, a third-party pixel, a cookie sync, and the app's SDK — five independent collection points from one gesture.

Traffic cameras
(pure video)		 Government-operated cameras at intersections and highways record continuous video. Raw footage stored for days/weeks. No real-time extraction — but retrievable after the fact for any incident or investigation. Raw visual record of all vehicles and pedestrians passing a point, usable for retroactive investigation, accident reconstruction, crowd estimation Government Transportation
Red-light / speed cameras
(video + structured metadata)		 Enforcement cameras that extract structured data in real time — plate OCR, speed measurement, violation timestamp. Every vehicle is read, not just violators. The metadata is queryable; the video is evidence. Plate number, vehicle speed, timestamp, lane, direction — for ALL passing traffic. Violator subset gets photos linked to registered owner. Government Enforcement
Flock Safety / ALPR networks Automatic License Plate Recognition cameras on residential streets, parking lots, HOA entrances. Pure metadata extraction — no video retained, just plate + timestamp + location + vehicle attributes (make, model, color). Every plate that passes is read and indexed. Searchable retroactively by law enforcement for weeks/months. Intrusiveness Gradient

Not all collection is equal. The gradient runs from transparent and user-initiated to ambient and invisible:

  • Declared — user explicitly submits data (form, survey). They know.
  • Observed — system watches what user does within the product (clicks, views). Expected.
  • Inferred — derived from observed data (interest scores, intent, demographics). Not shown back.
  • Ambient — collected without any direct interaction (WiFi probe, local network scan, audio fingerprint). Not expected.
Short-video platforms operate across all four levels simultaneously — declared (likes), observed (dwell time), inferred (interest graph), and ambient (device scan, network topology).
