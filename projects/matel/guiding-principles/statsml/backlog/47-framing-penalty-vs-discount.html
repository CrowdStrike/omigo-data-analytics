Framing: Penalty vs Discount

Two sides of the same coin — identical math, opposite psychology. How loss-frame pricing destroys relationships while gain-frame pricing builds loyalty.

Status: Raw thought. Needs expansion with more examples and possibly a full page in section 20 (Applied Game Theory & Behavioral Design).
The observation:

Companies charge 5% penalty for late payment (even by a single day).
Result: fights, support tickets, churn, resentment. Customers feel punished.

The reframe:
Bake the 5% into the base price upfront. Offer 5% discount for autopay.
Result: customers feel rewarded for choosing autopay. Same cash flow. Zero fights.
Why it works — Prospect Theory (Kahneman & Tversky, 1979):

• Losses hurt ~2× more than equivalent gains feel good
• Penalty = loss frame → anger, "unfair", dispute
• Discount = gain frame → reward, "smart choice", opt-in

Same money. Opposite emotional response.
Other examples of the same pattern:

• Gas station: "credit card surcharge" vs "cash discount" — legally different framing, same ¢/gallon gap
• Insurance: "penalty for lapse" vs "loyalty discount for continuous coverage"
• SaaS: "price increase after promo" vs "introductory discount for new members"
• Shipping: "$5 shipping fee" vs "free shipping on orders over $35" (price baked in)
Deeper pattern: The framing IS the product experience, even when the math is identical. A company choosing the penalty frame is optimizing for short-term extraction at the cost of long-term relationship. The discount frame costs nothing extra but earns goodwill. This connects to decoy effect, subscription traps, and charm pricing — all cases where perception engineering matters more than arithmetic.
