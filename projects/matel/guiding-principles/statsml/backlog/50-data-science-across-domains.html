BACKLOG

Data Science Across Domains

What data science actually looks like in each industry — the real problems, common techniques, required skills, and what's different from textbook ML.

Goal

For each domain, build a card that answers: What problems do data scientists actually solve here? What data do they work with? What techniques dominate? What skills are non-obvious? What makes this domain different from generic "build a model" tutorials?

Domains to Cover

DomainCore DS ProblemsWhat Makes It Different
E-Commerce & MarketplaceRanking, recommendation, demand forecasting, pricing, fraud detection, search relevance, A/B testing at scaleExtreme feedback loops — your model changes what users see, which changes the data. Cold start. Position bias everywhere.
Finance & BankingCredit scoring, fraud detection, AML, risk modeling, portfolio optimization, churn predictionRegulatory constraints on model explainability. Imbalanced classes (fraud is 0.1%). Model decisions have legal liability.
Stock Market & TradingAlpha signal discovery, execution optimization, market microstructure, alternative data, risk attributionNon-stationary distributions. Signal-to-noise ratio is tiny. Overfitting is the default failure mode. Backtesting bias.
Healthcare & BiotechClinical trial analysis, diagnostic imaging, drug discovery, EHR mining, survival analysis, epidemiologySmall n, high stakes. IRB/HIPAA constraints. Class imbalance in rare diseases. Causal inference matters more than prediction.
CybersecurityAnomaly detection, threat classification, log analysis, vulnerability prioritization, user behavior analyticsAdversarial setting — attackers adapt to your model. Extreme class imbalance. Alert fatigue. Labeled data barely exists.
Advertising & AdTechCTR prediction, bid optimization, attribution, audience segmentation, creative optimization, frequency cappingReal-time inference at 1M+ QPS. Privacy regulations killing features. Delayed/partial labels. Multi-touch attribution is unsolvable.
Ride-sharing & LogisticsETA prediction, surge pricing, dispatch optimization, demand forecasting, route optimizationSpatial-temporal data. Real-time optimization under uncertainty. Two-sided marketplace dynamics. Supply/demand coupling.
Streaming & ContentRecommendation, content understanding, engagement prediction, churn prediction, A/B testing on experienceLong-term preference vs short-term engagement. Popularity bias. Filter bubbles. User satisfaction ≠ watch time.
GamingMatchmaking, churn prediction, economy balancing, bot detection, player segmentation, LiveOps optimizationDesigned systems — you control the data generating process. Whale identification ethics. Engagement vs addiction line.
Climate & EnergyWeather forecasting, grid optimization, carbon modeling, renewable output prediction, anomaly detectionPhysics-informed models. Extremely long feedback loops. Spatial correlation. Distribution shift is the problem, not a bug.
Manufacturing & IoTPredictive maintenance, quality control, yield optimization, sensor fusion, supply chain forecastingRare failure events. Sensor drift. Domain expertise required to label. Deployment on edge devices with constraints.
Social Media & Content ModerationContent ranking, moderation/safety, network analysis, trend detection, influence measurementScale (billions of items/day). Adversarial content creators. Cultural context. Speed requirement (remove harmful content in minutes).

Per-Domain Card Structure

  • Top 5 real problems — what DS teams actually spend their time on (not the glamorous stuff)
  • Data landscape — what data exists, its quality, volume, latency, and labeling situation
  • Common techniques — what actually works here (often simpler than expected)
  • Required skills beyond ML — domain knowledge, regulatory awareness, stakeholder management
  • Unique challenges — what makes this domain's DS problems unlike others
  • Career entry points — how to break in, what to study, what portfolio projects actually demonstrate domain fit
  • Common mistakes — what junior DS people get wrong in this domain specifically
  • Day-in-the-life — realistic split: 60% data wrangling, 20% stakeholder, 15% modeling, 5% deployment (varies by domain)

Key Insights to Surface

  • 80% of DS work is the same everywhere — data cleaning, feature engineering, stakeholder communication, metric definition
  • The 20% that differs is what makes or breaks you — domain constraints, regulatory environment, deployment context
  • Technique sophistication ≠ business impact — logistic regression with the right features beats deep learning with wrong ones in most domains
  • The hardest part is rarely the model — it's defining the problem correctly, getting labeled data, and measuring the right thing
  • Domain switching cost is real but overestimated — 6 months to be productive, 2 years to have genuine domain intuition
