Earthquake - Domain-Specific Pitfalls

Seismic data combines tiny sample sizes, incomplete historical catalogs, and fundamentally unpredictable events — a domain where standard statistical intuition fails badly.

Prediction Is Fundamentally Impossible

50+ Years of Precursor Research — No Reliable Signal Found
  • Failed precursors: Radon emissions, animal behavior, electromagnetic anomalies, and seismic gaps have all been studied for decades — none reliably precedes earthquakes.
  • Chaotic trigger: Rupture initiation depends on stress details at scales no instrument can observe, so the trigger mechanism is effectively chaotic.
  • Hazard ≠ prediction: "30% chance of M7+ in 30 years" is probabilistic hazard, a valid statement; "M7 next Tuesday" is prediction, which remains unsolved.
  • Model implication: Any ML pipeline claiming to predict individual earthquakes is fitting noise — the honest target is long-run rate estimation.

Recurrence Interval Estimated From n=3-5 Events

Events in 1700, 1857, 1906 → "Average Interval 100 Years" Is Nearly Meaningless
  • The math: Three events give two intervals — a "mean recurrence" computed from n=2 gaps has an enormous confidence interval.
  • The range: The true interval could plausibly be 60 years or 200 years; the point estimate of ~100 conveys false precision.
  • Wrong model: The Poisson (memoryless) assumption may not hold — faults can cluster or quasi-cycle, and n=3 cannot distinguish these regimes.
  • Downstream damage: Building codes and insurance pricing built on this point estimate inherit the unquantified uncertainty.

Catalog Incompleteness — Detection, Not Occurrence

"Earthquakes Are Increasing!" — No, Seismometers Are
  • The artifact: Pre-1970 catalogs contain only large earthquakes; as seismometer networks expanded, small events started being recorded.
  • The trap: Counting events per decade shows a steep rise — an instrumentation improvement misread as an actual increase in seismicity.
  • Invalid comparison: Any historical trend analysis must restrict to magnitudes above the completeness threshold of the oldest period.
  • General lesson: When the measurement system changes over time, raw counts confound detection capability with the underlying rate.

Aftershock or Foreshock? Classification Is Uncertain for Days

M7.0 Then M5.5 Two Days Later — Decaying Aftershock or Foreshock of a Coming M8?
  • The ambiguity: A M5.5 after a M7.0 is usually an aftershock, but occasionally the whole sequence is a foreshock series to something larger.
  • Statistics vs instance: Omori's law describes aggregate aftershock decay well, but classifying an individual event stays uncertain for days.
  • Why it matters: Evacuation, emergency response, and public messaging hinge on a distinction the data cannot resolve in real time.
  • Label problem: Training labels ("aftershock" vs "foreshock") are assigned retrospectively — the model learns a distinction unavailable at decision time.

Induced Seismicity — Human Activity Breaks the Historical Baseline

Fracking, Wastewater Injection, Reservoirs → Earthquakes Where the Record Says "Stable"
  • The mechanism: Wastewater injection, fracking, and reservoir filling change subsurface stress and trigger earthquakes in previously quiet regions.
  • Not in the record: These events have no historical precedent, so catalog-based hazard models assign the area near-zero risk.
  • Systematic underestimate: Hazard maps calibrated on "natural" seismicity underestimate risk wherever human activity has begun.
  • Non-stationarity: The data-generating process changed — extrapolating from the pre-injection era is extrapolating from a different distribution.

Building Vulnerability Is Unknown Until It Fails

Damage = Ground Motion × Building Response — And the Second Factor Is Unobserved
  • The equation: Predicting damage needs both ground motion (measurable) and building response (unknown without physical inspection).
  • Code ≠ construction: A building permitted under modern code may not have been built to it — compliance on paper is not compliance in concrete.
  • Post-facto discovery: Soft-story and other vulnerable configurations are often identified only after they collapse.
  • Model gap: Loss models that plug in code-assumed fragility curves systematically understate damage for the non-compliant stock.
