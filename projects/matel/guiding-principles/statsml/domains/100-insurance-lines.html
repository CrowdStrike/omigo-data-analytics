Insurance Lines of Business — Each Line Has Unique Data Challenges

Beyond the general actuarial pitfalls, each insurance line of business has data challenges all its own.

Homeowners / Property

Catastrophe Correlation: 1000 "Independent" Claims Arrive at Once
  • Catastrophe correlation: Individual claims are "independent" until one hurricane makes 1000 homes claim simultaneously.
  • Demand surge: Post-disaster, every contractor in the state is booked → reconstruction costs rise 40%.
  • Wrong baseline: "Replacement cost" estimated from pre-disaster prices is wrong post-disaster.
  • Hidden renovation value: Owner improved the kitchen but never notified the insurer → underinsured.

Auto / Motor

Telematics Privacy vs Pricing Accuracy; ADAS Repair Inflation
  • Telematics tradeoff: Speed, braking, cornering, time-of-day, and locations are a perfect risk signal — but who owns the data, and can the insurer share it with police?
  • Privacy boundary: Tracking a teenager driving to a therapy clinic may constitute health-information disclosure.
  • Repair inflation: A $200 bumper repair in 2015 is a $2000 sensor recalibration in 2024 (ADAS cameras in every panel).

Health Insurance

Claims Reflect What's BILLED, Not What's Wrong With the Patient
  • Definition drift: What counts as "pre-existing" changes with diagnostic technology — with whole genome sequencing, everyone has a "pre-existing genetic risk."
  • Billed ≠ wrong: Claims data reflects upcoding for revenue, defensive medicine, and procedure-happy providers — not the patient's actual condition.
  • Network effects: Cost depends on WHICH hospital the patient goes to — 10× price difference for the same procedure.

General Liability

Set a Reserve in 2024 That Must Predict a 2034 Jury
  • Long tail: Claim filed today → lawsuit → discovery → trial → appeal → final resolution 5-10 years later.
  • Impossible forecast: Today's reserve must predict an outcome decided under different judges, juries, laws, and social norms.
  • Social inflation: Jury awards rise 10-15% annually regardless of actual damages, so historical data understates future costs.

Umbrella / Excess Liability

Gaps Fall in From Below; Pricing From n=5-10 Claims
  • Stacking gaps: Umbrella sits ABOVE primary policies (auto + home + general), so any underlying coverage exclusion falls into the umbrella.
  • No visibility: The primary insurer changes an exclusion → creates a gap the umbrella didn't price for.
  • Tiny n, fat tail: The umbrella only triggers for large claims — rare and extreme — priced from 5-10 historical claims over 20 years.

Business / Commercial Property

Business Interruption: Lost Revenue Is an Unobservable Counterfactual
  • Counterfactual claim: "What would revenue have been?" for a business that was GROWING when the event happened can't be observed.
  • Adversarial estimates: Both insurer and insured have incentives to manipulate the counterfactual estimate.
  • Supply chain cascade: Factory burns in country X → 100 businesses in country Y can't get parts → correlated BI losses from one upstream event.

Cyber Insurance

The "Uninsurable" Line: Fat-Tailed, Correlated, Evolving, Adversarial
  • Fat-tailed and correlated: One breach can cost $1B, and SolarWinds hit 18,000 organizations simultaneously.
  • Rapidly evolving: Yesterday's risk model is tomorrow's joke.
  • Adversarial: Attackers STUDY what's insured and target accordingly — "ransom demand = exactly the policy limit."
  • No actuarial history: ~15 years of data for a risk that changes every 6 months — can't price from history.

Life Insurance

Longevity Risk and Mortality Risk Point in Opposite Directions
  • Opposite bets: Die too soon → the life policy pays out early; live too long → the annuity business loses money.
  • COVID shock: The mortality spike caused life-insurance losses — and revealed some "dead" policyholders were alive (fraud).
  • Snapshot underwriting: Healthy at 35 at application, obese at 45, still paying healthy rates → adverse selection after issue.

Workers' Compensation

"Is the Injury Work-Related?" Is a Legal Question, Not a Medical One
  • Legal, not medical: The same back pain is covered if it happened at work and not covered if it happened at home.
  • Intent prediction: Malingering detection vs legitimate claim means the model must predict INTENT, not just medical facts.
  • Incentive effects: Duration of disability claims correlates with benefit generosity, not just injury severity.

Reinsurance (Insurance of Insurance)

The Risk of the Risk: Layers Nobody Can See Through
  • Information asymmetry: The insurer knows more about its book than the reinsurer and may CEDE its worst risks selectively.
  • Retrocessional layers: Reinsurance of reinsurance of reinsurance lets one major event cascade through 5 layers.
  • Unknown total exposure: Risk is distributed across layers — nobody knew AIG's 2008 concentration until it collapsed simultaneously.
