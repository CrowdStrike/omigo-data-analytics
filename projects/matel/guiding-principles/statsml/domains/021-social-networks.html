social network - Platform-Specific Data Pitfalls

Selection bias, measurement error, and attribution traps baked into social network engagement data.

Demographic Skew Shifts Over Time

The "Average User" Aged From 28 (2015) to ~40 (2024)
  • The migration: Young users (13-24) moved to short-video and photo-sharing platforms while 45+ cohorts grew substantially.
  • Stale calibration: Models trained on 2015 demographic distributions produce biased predictions on 2024 data — click behavior calibrated on younger users mispredicts for older ones.
  • Geographic compounding: Growth in the Global South while stagnating in US/EU adds a second axis of skew.
  • Impact: Any longitudinal study spanning 2015-2024 conflates platform composition changes with behavioral changes.

Algorithmic Content Filtering as Selection Bias

The Observed Population Is Already Filtered by an Unobserved Selection Function
  • Censored window: Users only interact with algorithmically-selected content, so high engagement conflates content quality with algorithmic affinity.
  • SUTVA violation: A/B tests on group content show inflated effect sizes because treatment and control contaminate each other through shared group membership.
  • Spillover: Network effects make individual-level randomization invalid — one user's treatment spills over to connections via shared content.
  • Impact: Models trained on algorithmically-filtered data cannot generalize to the unfiltered population.

Share ≠ Endorsement

Recommending and Criticizing Are Counted as the Same Signal
  • Conflated intent: "Look at this great article!" and "Can you believe this nonsense?" are counted identically.
  • Debunk effect: Misinformation often has high share counts precisely because people share it to debunk it.
  • Missing signal: The sentiment of accompanying text is needed but rarely incorporated into engagement metrics.
  • Impact: Content quality models overweight shares, inflating scores for polarizing content by 30-40%.

News Feed Ranking Creates Observable Reality

Of ~1,500 Potential Stories per Session, Only ~300 Are Shown — 80% Are Invisible
  • What you can't measure: Engagement metrics only reflect responses to shown content, not latent demand for unshown content.
  • Censored history: Historical data is filtered by past ranking decisions — you cannot learn about items never shown.
  • Position bias: Top-ranked items get disproportionate engagement regardless of quality.
  • Impact: Any model trained on engagement data inherits the biases of the ranking system that generated it (feedback loop).

Cross-Platform Attribution Impossible

Every Platform Claims Credit for the Same Conversion
  • The journey: User sees a social network ad, searches the product, reads forum reviews, buys on an e-commerce platform — and the social network claims "view-through" credit.
  • Window inflation: 28-day attribution windows capture coincidental exposure as causal.
  • Tracking blackout: iOS App Tracking Transparency eliminated cross-app tracking for ~75% of iOS users after 2021, so modeled (probabilistic) conversions replace actual measurement.
  • Impact: Reported ROAS is systematically inflated 2-3x compared to incrementality-tested ground truth.

Memory/Nostalgia "On This Day" Bias

Selective Resurfacing Rewrites the Historical Record Upward
  • Self-selection: Users preferentially reshare positive memories; negative or neutral ones are ignored or actively hidden.
  • Algorithmic survivorship: The platform filters out potentially painful memories (breakups, deaths), biasing the observable archive.
  • Retroactive inflation: Reshared memories get new engagement, inflating historical content metrics after the fact.
  • Impact: Platform happiness metrics are inflated 15-25% by recycled positive content appearing multiple times.
