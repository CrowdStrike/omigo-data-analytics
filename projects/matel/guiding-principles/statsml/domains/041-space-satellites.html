Space & Satellites

Domain-specific pitfalls in satellite data analytics.

Downlink Bandwidth Limits

Downlink Bandwidth Limits
  • The bottleneck: A satellite collects ~100 TB of raw data per orbit but can transmit only ~1 TB per ground-station pass.
  • 99% discarded: Data must be dropped or aggressively compressed on-board using prioritization logic uploaded days or weeks earlier.
  • Blind decisions: The keep/discard logic operates without knowledge of current intelligence needs or what other satellites already collected.
  • The result: Ground analysts receive a narrow, pre-filtered slice of reality — never the full picture the sensor captured.

Orbital Mechanics Constrain Collection

Orbital Mechanics Constrain Collection
  • Sparse passes: A LEO satellite passes over a given point only once every several hours to days, with an imaging window of seconds to minutes per pass.
  • No real-time: Continuous monitoring is fundamentally impossible for most of the Earth most of the time.
  • Revisit range: From 12 hours for agile high-priority constellations to 5+ days for single-satellite systems.
  • Patchwork: Analysts fill gaps with interpolation and fusion, stitching a narrative from momentary glimpses rather than continuous observation.

Radiation-Induced Data Corruption

Radiation-Induced Data Corruption
  • Bit flips: Cosmic rays and solar particle events corrupt roughly 1 in 10^6 pixels per image frame.
  • Looks like noise: Corruption is spatially and temporally random — statistically significant across large mosaics but sparse enough to resemble ordinary sensor noise, and it cannot be calibrated out.
  • ECC limits: Error-correcting codes catch most multi-bit errors, but single-bit flips in data buses often pass undetected.
  • ML risk: Downstream models may learn these artifacts as features, especially in rarely-observed terrain where training data is sparse.

Ground Truth Inaccessible

Ground Truth Inaccessible
  • No site visits: Detections made from 500+ km altitude typically cannot be physically verified on the ground.
  • Resolution limit: "Tank or truck? Refugee camp or construction site?" cannot be answered from imagery alone at 0.3-5 m/pixel.
  • Proxy truth: Verification requires separate intelligence sources — field agents, drone overflights, cooperative reporting, or social media signals.
  • Consequence: Model accuracy metrics are themselves estimates, and systematic biases can persist undetected for years.

Atmospheric/Weather Interference

Atmospheric/Weather Interference
  • Cloud blackout: Optical sensors are completely blocked by cloud cover — monsoon season makes 3+ continuous months essentially uncollectable.
  • SAR isn't equivalent: Radar penetrates clouds but measures backscatter intensity, not spectral reflectance.
  • Seasonal bias: Models trained on "available" data over-represent dry-season conditions and under-represent wet-season reality.
  • Coverage floor: In Southeast Asia, the Amazon basin, or Central Africa, annual optical coverage can be 20-30% of days, skewing trend estimates.

Constellation Coordination

Constellation Coordination
  • Heterogeneous fleet: Constellations of 100+ satellites differ in sensors, spectral bands, orbital parameters, ages, and calibration states.
  • Stitching artifacts: Different sun angles, radiometric responses, and ground sample distances mean the "consistent global view" is constructed through heavy post-processing, not directly observed.
  • False changes: Cross-calibration drift between satellites can masquerade as real ground change, and swath-boundary mosaicking artifacts trigger false-positive detections.
