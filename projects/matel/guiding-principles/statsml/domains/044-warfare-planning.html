Military Planning / C2 Domain Pitfalls

Statistical and analytical traps in warfare planning and command-and-control systems.

Fog of War: Deliberately Incomplete Data

Commanders Decide on 10-30% of Ground Truth
  • Adversarial gaps: The enemy actively hides capabilities, conceals force disposition, and masks intent — intelligence is fragments, not a full picture.
  • Not like civilian data: In civilian analytics missing data is accidental; in warfare the gaps are intentional, engineered by the adversary.
  • Model failure: Any model that assumes data completeness will catastrophically fail when confronted with adversarial concealment.

Deception as Deliberate Signal Pollution

Your Data Is Adversarially Poisoned — The Signal-to-Noise Ratio Is Weaponized
  • Fake signals: Dummy tanks, decoy communications, false radio traffic, inflatable equipment, and spoofed electronic emissions are crafted to be collected.
  • Studied collection: Intelligent adversaries study your collection methods and feed you exactly what they want you to believe.
  • The trap: Trusting collected intelligence at face value means trusting your enemy's narrative — the noise is not random.

Classification Prevents Data Sharing

The Full Operational Picture Requires Joins That Are Legally Forbidden
  • Hard boundaries: TOP SECRET/SCI data cannot be joined with UNCLASSIFIED sensor data, and SIGINT analysts cannot share with HUMINT analysts without proper channels.
  • Coalition friction: Partners operate at different clearance levels, so analysis is compartmented by design.
  • Forced silos: The security mechanisms that protect sources and methods also prevent the data fusion that would yield the best decisions.

After-Action Reports Are Survivor-Biased

Training Data Censored by Death — The Most Important Points Are Missing
  • Only survivors report: The patrol that was ambushed and annihilated leaves no debrief; destroyed units cannot tell you what went wrong.
  • Biased lessons: "Lessons learned" are lessons from survivors — you learn what successful units did, never the critical errors that led to destruction.
  • Systematic censoring: The failures that matter most are permanently absent from the training data.

Wargame ≠ Reality Gap

Simulations Have No Real Casualties, No Genuine Fog, No Panic
  • Missing friction: Exercises omit logistics breakdown, morale collapse, exhaustion, and the thousand small failures that compound in real operations.
  • Systematic optimism: Models calibrated on clean exercise data overestimate unit effectiveness, underestimate attrition, and ignore cascading failures.
  • The dictum: "No plan survives contact with the enemy" (Moltke) — wargame-trained models drastically underestimate friction.

Tempo and OODA Loop

A Perfect Model at 30-Minute Latency Loses to an Imperfect One at 30 Seconds
  • Data decay: Data that is 6 hours old is tactically worthless — the enemy has moved, regrouped, or struck.
  • Decision advantage: The side with the faster OODA loop (Observe, Orient, Decide, Act) forces the opponent into reactive mode.
  • Speed beats accuracy: "Good enough now" dominates "perfect later" in every engagement — analytical perfection is the enemy of operational tempo.
