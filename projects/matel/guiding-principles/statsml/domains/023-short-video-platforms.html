short-video platform - Platform-Specific Data Pitfalls

Cold-start gates, misaligned reward signals, and 24-hour trend cycles that break standard ML assumptions.

Extreme Cold-Start on For You Page

A Single Noisy Signal From 300 Users Gates All Downstream Distribution
  • The gate: Every new video is shown to ~300 random users, and the 5-second watch rate from that tiny cohort decides entry into the 1K, 10K, and 100K+ impression tiers.
  • Noise kills: If the initial cohort happens to be disinterested (wrong time zone, wrong demographic slice), a potentially viral video dies immediately.
  • Data problem: Massive survivorship bias — you only observe engagement for videos that passed the gate, and the counterfactual (a different initial cohort) is never observed.

Watch-Time ≠ Enjoyment

"Car Crash" Videos: 100% Completion Rate, 0% Satisfaction
  • The mechanism: Users watch the entire video out of morbid curiosity or inability to look away, not because they want more.
  • Misaligned reward: The reward signal (completion) diverges from the objective (satisfaction), so models trained on completion rate systematically promote addictive-but-regrettable content.
  • The evidence: Post-session surveys show negative correlation with high-completion content — optimizing watch-time yields a feed users complete but regret.

Trend Half-Life: 24-48 Hours

A Sound Goes Viral Monday, Saturates Tuesday, Is Dead Wednesday
  • Compressed lifecycle: Trends live 24-48 hours, so by the time a batch-trained model learns a sound is trending, the trend is over.
  • Broken splits: Traditional train/test splits are meaningless when feature distributions shift every 24 hours.
  • Degradation: A model trained on Monday's data and evaluated on Thursday's shows catastrophic performance loss on trend-related features — real-time feature freshness is existential, not optional.

Sound/Music as Hidden Feature

Same Dance, Different Sound = 100x Different Reach
  • The viral vector: Audio carries the network effect — not the visual content, the creator, or the caption.
  • Pipeline blind spot: Most ML pipelines feature-engineer visual and text signals while treating audio as a categorical ID, missing the platform's core distribution mechanism.
  • The fix: Sound_id alone is too sparse — virality prediction needs sound embeddings plus trend velocity (tempo, novelty, meme-ability, current trend status).

Creator Burnout Cycles

Output → Viral Hit → Pressure → Burnout → Hiatus → Comeback
  • The cycle: Creators follow a predictable loop where a viral hit raises audience expectations, pressure builds, and a 2-8 week burnout hiatus follows.
  • Stale averages: A creator's historical average engagement is meaningless in the burnout phase — recency-weighted models underpredict comeback virality and overpredict burnout-phase performance.
  • Hidden state: Creator state modulates all observable features; unmodeled, it produces unexplained variance that looks like noise but is a deterministic periodic process.

Duet/Stitch Context Dependency

A Reaction Video Without Its Stimulus Is Literally Meaningless
  • The content IS the juxtaposition: Duet and Stitch videos only make sense paired with their original source video.
  • Garbage features: Sentiment analysis of the response alone is wrong, and engagement prediction without the original context is random.
  • The scale: ~15-20% of platform content is contextually dependent — a pipeline that doesn't join responses with originals trains on incomplete observations for a significant fraction of the corpus.
