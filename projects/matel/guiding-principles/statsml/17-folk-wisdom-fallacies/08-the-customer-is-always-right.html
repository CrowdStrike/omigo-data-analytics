"The customer is always right"

Folk wisdom dissected into its component fallacies — what the saying hides and why it feels true anyway.

Why people believe it: customer-centric businesses often succeed, so the maxim gets generalized to an absolute.
#FallacyHow it hides
1Universal quantifier fallacy"Always" admits no exceptions. A single counterexample (fraudulent return, unreasonable demand) disproves it.
2Authority fallacy (inverted)Grants epistemic authority to someone based on role (buyer), not expertise or evidence.
3Ecological fallacyWhat's true in aggregate (customer preferences matter) is applied to every individual (this specific customer is right).
4Ignores adversarial actorsSome "customers" are scammers, abusers, or bad-faith actors. The saying has no exception clause.
5Composition fallacyWhat individual customers want may contradict what's good for the customer base collectively.
Undefined terms: "customer" (paying? browsing? returning fraudulently?), "right" (factually? morally? in preference?), "always" (no exceptions?)
Counterexamples:
  • Customers demanding medically dangerous products (supplements that don't work)
  • Serial returners abusing return policies — "right" at the cost of other customers
  • Henry Ford: "If I asked people what they wanted, they'd have said faster horses"
