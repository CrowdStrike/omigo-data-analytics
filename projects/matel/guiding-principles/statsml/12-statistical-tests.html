Statistical Tests & Metrics Reference

When each test is valid, when it breaks, and what to use instead — with real data examples

HYPOTHESIS

1. t-test (Student's and Welch's)

Whether two groups have significantly different means. Breaks with skew and heavy tails.

means two-groups normality
HYPOTHESIS

2. z-test / Proportion test

Whether an observed proportion differs from expected. Fails when np < 10 or clustering present.

proportions binomial sample-size
HYPOTHESIS

3. Chi-squared test

Whether observed frequencies differ from expected. Inflated with sparse cells (expected < 5).

categorical contingency independence
HYPOTHESIS

4. KS test

Whether two samples come from the same distribution. Power collapses with ties or discrete data.

distributions CDF continuous
INFO-THEORY

5. Cross-entropy

Loss function measuring predicted vs true distribution. Catastrophic penalty for overconfident wrong predictions.

loss-function classification calibration
INFO-THEORY

6. Gini impurity

Decision tree split criterion. Favors balanced partitions, can miss rare but important classes.

decision-trees splits imbalance
INFO-THEORY

7. Information gain

Feature selection metric. Biased toward high-cardinality features like IDs.

feature-selection cardinality entropy
INFO-THEORY

8. Entropy

Uncertainty measure of a distribution. Same value can mean very different structures.

uncertainty information-theory distribution-shape
HYPOTHESIS

9. ANOVA (F-test)

Whether means differ across 3+ groups. Unreliable with unequal variances or unbalanced designs.

multiple-groups variance homoscedasticity
