Texas Sharpshooter Fallacy

Hypothesis generated by data then tested on same data guarantees a result

Target Drawn Around the Bullet Holes After Shooting
  • Cancer clusters: "There are 5 cancer cases on this street! It must be the power lines!" But: with millions of streets, SOME will have clusters by pure chance. You noticed THIS cluster and drew the target around it. You didn't predict it in advance — you found it after the fact and called it significant.
  • Feature selection: "We discovered that Feature X perfectly predicts churn!" After testing 500 features. At p<0.05, you EXPECT 25 false positives. You found one, drew a circle around it, and called it a discovery. The "target" was painted after the "shot."
  • Epidemiology: "Disease X is 3× more common in people who eat Y!" After testing 200 food–disease combinations. One hit significance. Published as breakthrough. The 199 non-results are invisible. The published one is noise dressed as signal.
  • Algorithmic trading: "Our backtest found a pattern: sell on the 3rd Tuesday after earnings miss when VIX > 20." After testing 10,000 pattern combinations. SOME will fit historical data by chance. You found the coincidence and declared it a strategy.

Why it's pseudoscience: The hypothesis was generated BY the data, then tested ON the same data. This guarantees a result — you're confirming that the data contains itself. Real science: hypothesis from theory → test on NEW data. If you used the data to find the pattern AND to validate it, you've proven nothing.
