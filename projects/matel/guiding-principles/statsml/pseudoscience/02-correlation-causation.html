Correlation Presented as Causation

Flawed Reasoning — Actionable conclusions from observational data without experimental validation

"Data Shows X Correlates with Y — Therefore DO X to Get Y"
  • Health: "People who eat blueberries have lower cancer rates." → "Eat blueberries to prevent cancer!" But: people who eat blueberries also exercise, don't smoke, have healthcare access, are wealthier. The blueberry is a MARKER of a healthy lifestyle, not the CAUSE of health.
  • Business: "Companies that do stand-ups grow 2× faster." But: fast-growing companies adopt trendy practices (stand-ups). Growth → stand-ups, not stand-ups → growth. Cargo cult: copy the ritual, expect the outcome.
  • Tech: "Teams using Kubernetes deploy 3× faster." But: teams with resources/talent to adopt K8s are ALREADY high-performing. K8s is a marker of team capability, not the cause of it. Giving K8s to a struggling team won't make them 3× faster.
  • Education: "Students who take AP courses get into better colleges." So: put all students in AP courses! But: students who self-select into AP are already high-performing. The AP class didn't make them smart — smart kids chose AP.

Why it's pseudoscience: Actionable conclusions from observational data without experimental validation. The intervention ("do X") has never been TESTED independently of the selection effect ("people who already do X are different from those who don't").
