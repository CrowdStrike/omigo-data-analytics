Ecological Fallacy

Structural — Group Stats Applied to Individuals

Group-Level Statistics Applied to Individuals (Simpson's Paradox Territory)
  • Country-level correlations: "Countries with higher chocolate consumption win more Nobel Prizes!" Therefore chocolate makes you smarter? No: wealth → chocolate consumption AND wealth → education → Nobel Prizes. The correlation exists at country level but tells you NOTHING about individuals eating chocolate.
  • Demographic stereotyping: "Group X has average income of $Y, therefore individual from Group X earns $Y." The distribution within groups is ENORMOUS. Using the group mean to predict individual outcomes throws away all within-group variance — which is typically 10× larger than between-group variance.
  • ML fairness: "Zip code Z has high default rates → deny credit to everyone in Z." Penalizing individuals for group-level statistics. Within that zip code: most people pay their loans. The aggregate hides the individual reality.
  • Medicine: "Drug works for the average patient." Average patient doesn't exist. Drug might work for 30% (strong responders) and do nothing for 70%. The "average effect" is a fiction — it's not what any individual experiences.

Why it's pseudoscience: Conclusions about individuals derived from aggregate data. The aggregate masks heterogeneity. What's true of the GROUP is often false for most INDIVIDUALS in the group. Applying group statistics to individual decisions is statistically invalid and often discriminatory.
