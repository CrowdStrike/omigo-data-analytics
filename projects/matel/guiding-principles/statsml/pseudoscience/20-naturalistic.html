Naturalistic Fallacy / Appeal to Tradition

"Natural = Good" or "We've Always Done It This Way = It's Correct"

"Natural = Good" or "We've Always Done It This Way = It's Correct"
  • "Natural" products: Arsenic is natural. Cyanide is natural. Smallpox is natural. "Natural" is not a synonym for "safe" or "effective." The word is a marketing category, not a scientific one. Nature is indifferent to your health — evolution didn't optimize for human comfort.
  • "Traditional" methods: "We've always cleaned data this way." Tradition is evidence that something SURVIVED, not that it's optimal. Bloodletting survived 2000 years. Tradition tells you what people DID, not what works. Your traditional ETL pipeline might be 10× slower than necessary — "we've always done it" is not a defense.
  • "Organic" data: "Use organic growth metrics, not paid." Why? If paid users convert and retain at the same rate, the source doesn't matter. "Organic" = natural = good is a value judgment wearing the costume of an analytical distinction.
  • "Human intuition over algorithms": "A human should make the final decision." Why? Human intuition is biased, inconsistent, and fatigued. In contexts where algorithms demonstrably outperform humans (radiology, bail decisions, loan approvals), insisting on "human judgment" is appeal to nature — the belief that human = better by definition.
  • "We've never needed X before": You also never had the current scale, complexity, or competitive landscape before. Past absence of need ≠ current absence of need. The world changed; the tradition didn't account for that.

Why it's pseudoscience: "Natural" and "traditional" are emotional labels, not evidence. Whether something works is determined by TESTING, not by how long it's been around or whether it occurs in nature. Hemlock is natural and ancient. It will still kill you.
