Broscience / Influencer Pseudoscience

Platform visibility + confidence mistaken for expertise

Looks + Confidence + Platform = "Expert" (Without Evidence)
  • Fitness broscience: "I'm muscular → my routine works → do what I do." Missing: genetics (high testosterone responder), age (22, peak anabolic state), drugs (undisclosed PEDs), photographic deception (pump, lighting, angles, filters). The "result" precedes the "method" — they look good DESPITE their routine, not because of it.
  • Diet influencer: "I lost 50 lbs eating only [X]!" But: ANY caloric deficit works. The specific food is irrelevant. The constraint (restriction of X) created the deficit incidentally. They attribute success to the SPECIFIC restriction when the mechanism is TOTAL calories regardless of source. But "eat less" isn't marketable; "secret food hack" is.
  • Business influencer: "My morning routine made me a millionaire. Wake 4am, cold shower, journal, meditate..." They were already wealthy (inheritance, connections, luck). The morning routine is CORRELATED with success because successful people have the LUXURY to optimize mornings. The causation is reversed.
  • Tech influencer: "Learn these 5 languages and get a $300K job." Survivorship: people who learned those languages AND had FAANG connections AND had the right degree AND were in the right city AND interviewed well → $300K. The languages are 5% of the equation. The other 95% (network, luck, interview skills) isn't filmable content.

The mechanism: Platform algorithms reward CONFIDENCE not accuracy. Hedged, nuanced, honest content ("it depends, here are the factors...") performs 10× worse than confident proclamations ("Do THIS one thing!"). The incentive structure selects for overconfident pseudoscience and against careful science.
