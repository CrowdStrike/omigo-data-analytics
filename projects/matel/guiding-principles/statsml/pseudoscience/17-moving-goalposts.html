Redefining Success After the Fact So You Never Fail
  • Product launch: Goal: "10K signups in first month." Result: 2K signups. New narrative: "But engagement is high! The QUALITY of signups matters more than quantity." The metric of success was redefined AFTER the result was known. No failure is possible if you can always change what success means.
  • ML model: Goal: "Beat baseline by 5% accuracy." Result: -2% accuracy. New narrative: "But look at the precision on the minority class! That's what matters for this use case." The goalposts moved from overall accuracy to a sub-metric that happened to look better.
  • Diet/supplement: "This will help you lose weight!" No weight loss. "But it improved your energy!" No energy change. "But it detoxified your liver!" (unmeasurable). Each failed claim is replaced with a vaguer one until you reach unfalsifiable territory.
  • Strategy: "This initiative will increase revenue." Revenue flat. "Well, it preserved market share during a downturn." Market share also flat. "It positioned us for future growth." (unfalsifiable). The initial claim keeps transforming until it can't be disproven.

Why it's pseudoscience: Pre-registration exists to PREVENT this. Define success criteria BEFORE the experiment runs. If you can change what counts as success after seeing results, you can never fail — and a claim that cannot fail is not scientific. It's rationalization.
