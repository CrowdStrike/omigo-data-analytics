Password Composition Rules

How password rules evolved from storage-era length caps through mandatory-complexity checklists to the modern length-first reversal — and why human behavior, not combinatorics, drove every turn.

The bet: forced character classes strengthen human-chosen passwords. The verdict: leaked corpora settled it the other way — length, breach screening, and no scheduled expiry won.

1. The historical arc

  • Era 1 — caps: early systems capped passwords near eight characters.
  • Cause: legacy hashing schemes consumed a fixed-size input.
  • Effect: short passwords were a constraint, not a choice.
  • Era 2 — complexity: uppercase, digit, and symbol became mandatory.
  • Rotation: forced password changes roughly every 90 days.
  • Spread: audit-friendly rules traveled via compliance checklists.
  • Speed: checklists spread faster than the evidence.
  • Era 3 — reversal: modern national guidelines flipped to length-first.
  • Dropped: forced character classes and scheduled expiry.
  • Added: screening candidates against known-breached lists.
  • Rational then: each era's policy fit its own constraints.
Inertia: the complexity checklist outlived its evidence by two decades.

2. Why the reversal happened

  • Flaw — substitutions: rules were satisfied by predictable transforms.
  • Pattern: capital first letter, trailing digit, one common swap.
  • Result: realized entropy gain far below the theoretical.
  • Flaw — expiry: rotation produced trailing-counter increments.
  • Result: one expired password usually derives the current one.
  • Flaw — displacement: stricter rules pushed one password reused everywhere.
  • Also: users wrote their compliant passwords down.
  • Trade: theoretical on-site gain for concrete cross-site risk.
  • Net effect: rules moved predictability, never reduced it.
Theme: human behavior beat the policy — measure the realized distribution, not the theoretical one.

3. Country and sector differences

  • Banking stacks: short numeric PINs beside or instead of passwords.
  • Backstop: strict lockout and card possession carry the load.
  • Floors: minimum lengths vary by jurisdiction and sector.
  • Consumer: consumer services settle near the low end.
  • Regulated: government and regulated sectors mandate longer floors.
  • Numeric-only: feature-phone-era entry methods fixed a digit alphabet.
  • Persistence: numeric habits outlived the arrival of full keyboards.
  • Corporate lag: enterprise rules stayed strict after guidance reversed.
  • Cause: compliance audits update slower than the research.
  • Nature: differences are qualitative and regime-driven.
Fragmentation: one user faces a different rulebook per sector, which fuels reuse and workarounds.

4. Length vs composition math

  • Math: entropy grows linearly with length.
  • But: only logarithmically with alphabet size.
  • Per character: each one multiplies the space by the whole alphabet.
  • Per class: a new character class only nudges the multiplier.
  • Win: sixteen lowercase characters carry roughly 75 bits.
  • Compare: eight full-keyboard characters carry roughly 52 bits.
  • Caveat: the uniform model still flatters complexity rules.
  • Reality: human "complex" strings cluster in a modeled subspace.
  • Wrong question: raw combinatorics is not guessability.
  • What matters: only realized-distribution entropy counts.
Guessability: attackers search the human-chosen distribution in probability order.
