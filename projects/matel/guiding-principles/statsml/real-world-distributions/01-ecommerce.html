E-Commerce & Marketplace — Distribution Patterns

5 distributions you will encounter in any online marketplace dataset

GEOMETRIC DECAY

Position CTR (Click-Through Rate by Rank)

Click-through rate decays geometrically by position (#1=30%, #2=15%, #3=8%...) regardless of content quality. The smoothness proves humans scan with a fixed give-up probability — position trust dominates content quality. Swap experiments confirm CTR barely changes when results are reordered.

  • Geometric decay = fixed give-up probability per position
  • Position trust > content quality — the distribution IS the evidence
  • Same shape in Google, Amazon, any ranked list
  • Breaks independence assumptions in A/B tests on ranked items
LOG-NORMAL + SPIKES

Cart Value (Free-Shipping Threshold Scars)

Cart values form a right-skewed log-normal hill (center ~$35) with sharp needle spikes at $25, $35, $49, $75 — exactly at free-shipping thresholds. You can read the company's shipping policy directly from the distribution shape.

  • Spikes are behavioral scars from free-shipping thresholds
  • Log-normal body = natural spending variation
  • Spikes violate continuity assumptions in parametric models
  • Company policy changes are immediately visible as spike migration
BIMODAL

Time to Purchase (Impulse vs Retargeted)

Two populations mixed: impulse buyers (spike at day 0-1) and retargeted researchers (bump at day 12-14), with a dead zone at days 3-10 where nobody lives. The "average" time-to-purchase describes neither group.

  • Dead zone = nobody converts here, "average" is meaningless
  • Two populations: impulse (day 0-1) and retargeted (day 12-14)
  • Marketing attribution fails if it doesn't segment these
  • Bimodality = two different psychological processes at work
J-CURVE

Star Ratings (Selection Bias Made Visible)

Star ratings show spikes at 5★ and 1★, with a desert at 2-3★. This reflects motivation to review, not product quality — satisfied and angry people write reviews, moderate experiences produce silence.

  • 3-star experiences = silence (selection bias)
  • Shape reveals reviewing motivation, not quality
  • "Average rating" conflates two opposite populations
  • Platforms with mandatory ratings show completely different (normal) shape
ZERO-INFLATED PARETO

Revenue Per User (Two Universes)

70% of users spend exactly $0, then among spenders the distribution follows a power law where 1% generate 40% of revenue. "Average revenue per user" is meaningless — it averages across two completely different populations.

  • 70% at exactly $0 — two-universe problem
  • Among spenders: power law (1% = 40% of revenue)
  • t-tests and ANOVA on this shape = nonsense
  • Must model as zero-inflation + separate Pareto for spenders
