Checkout & Impulse Buying — Distribution Patterns

5 distributions that reveal how psychology, patience, and proximity shape every impulse purchase

GEOMETRIC DECAY

Items Added in Queue (Geometric Decay)

Most people add 0-1 impulse items while waiting in the checkout queue. Each additional item has only a 30% chance of being added — the geometric distribution captures this "one more?" decision chain perfectly.

  • P(0 items) = 70% — most resist entirely
  • P(1 item) = 21% — one grab is common
  • P(2+ items) = 9% — rare multi-grabbers
  • Each decision is independent with constant give-up rate
PAIN THRESHOLD

Impulse Item Price (Concentrated < $5)

Impulse items cluster overwhelmingly below $5 — the "no-think zone" where the brain skips ROI computation. Above $5, friction appears. Above $10, the purchase becomes deliberate, not impulsive. The price distribution IS the pain threshold map.

  • $0-5: no cognitive friction, auto-grab
  • $5-10: hesitation zone, conversion drops 60%
  • $10+: rational computation activates
  • $5 = the boundary where impulse becomes decision
PATIENCE CLIFF

Queue Abandon Time (Exponential + Cliff)

Patience follows an exponential decay — memoryless, with a constant hazard rate. But at 4 minutes, a hard psychological cliff appears: virtually nobody waits past this threshold. The distribution is smooth then catastrophic.

  • Exponential decay (lambda=0.3) for 0-4 min
  • Hard abandon cliff at 4 minutes
  • Memoryless property holds until the cliff
  • Only 5% of customers survive past 4 min
NATURAL SEGMENTATION

Self-Checkout (Bimodal at ~12)

Cart size is bimodal — self-checkout users cluster around 5 items, staffed lane users around 22. The valley at 12 items is not arbitrary — it's the natural behavioral boundary where self-service becomes slower than waiting for staff.

  • Self-checkout peak: mean=5, sd=2 (quick trips)
  • Staffed lane peak: mean=22, sd=6 (full shops)
  • Valley at ~12 items = natural segmentation boundary
  • 65% choose self-checkout, 35% staffed
EVERY FOOT HALVES CONVERSION

Distance from Register (Exponential Decay)

Impulse conversion rate drops exponentially with distance from the register. Items at arm's reach convert 10x more than items 4 feet away. Every additional foot of distance halves the probability of a grab — placement IS pricing.

  • 1 foot: ~10% grab rate
  • 2 feet: ~5% grab rate
  • 3 feet: ~2.5% grab rate
  • 4+ feet: effectively invisible to impulse buyers
