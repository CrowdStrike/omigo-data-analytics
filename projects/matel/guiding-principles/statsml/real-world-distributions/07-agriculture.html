Agriculture & Climate — Distribution Patterns

Zero-Inflated Gamma
Daily Rainfall (mm)

Most days are dry (0 mm), and rainy days follow a gamma distribution. The zero-inflation makes mean daily rainfall deeply misleading as a summary statistic.

  • Zero-inflated: ~50% of days have exactly 0 mm precipitation
  • Gamma is the standard model for positive rainfall amounts (shape=2, scale~3mm)
  • Mean daily rainfall conflates dry frequency with wet intensity — both matter separately for agriculture
  • Dry spell length (consecutive zeros) drives crop stress more than average rainfall
  • Seasonal decomposition needed: monsoon vs. dry season are different distributions entirely

Left-Truncated Normal
Crop Yield Per Hectare (tonnes)

Near-normal distribution but truncated at zero (yield cannot be negative), with slight left skew from drought and pest years creating a heavier left tail.

  • Weather extremes (drought, flooding, frost) create the left tail — bad years cluster
  • Genetic improvement and better inputs shift the mean rightward over decades
  • Hard floor at zero: a destroyed crop yields nothing, never negative
  • Exceptional years (optimal weather + inputs) produce a small right bump
  • Year-over-year variance driven primarily by weather, not farmer skill

Overdispersed Poisson
Pest Count Per Plant

Negative binomial distribution: most plants have 0-2 pests, but some are heavily infested. The variance far exceeds the mean, making Poisson a poor fit.

  • Clustering effect: pests attract more pests (pheromones, egg-laying behavior)
  • Poisson assumes variance = mean; real pest data has variance 3-10x the mean
  • Spatial autocorrelation: infested plants cluster geographically in the field
  • Negative binomial (r=2, p=0.3) captures the overdispersion naturally
  • Sampling design must account for spatial clustering or prevalence is underestimated

Bounded Normal
Soil pH

Approximately normal between hard bounds of 4-9, with most agricultural land falling in 5.5-7.5. Chemical buffering creates effective bounds tighter than the theoretical 0-14 scale.

  • Chemical buffering systems keep pH within a narrow range — extreme values are rare
  • Different crops require different ranges: blueberries (4.5-5.5) vs. alfalfa (6.5-7.5)
  • Liming shifts the distribution rightward; sulfur application shifts it leftward
  • Regional geology creates sub-populations: acidic soils (granitic), alkaline soils (limestone)
  • A single normal fit misses the bounded nature — beta distribution on [4, 9] fits better

Multi-Modal (Climate Zones)
Growing Season Length (Days)

Near-normal within each climate zone, but combining across zones produces a clearly multi-modal distribution with latitude-dependent peaks.

  • Each climate zone has its own mode: temperate ~180d, subtropical ~240d, cold ~120d, tropical ~330d
  • Climate change is shifting all modes rightward — growing seasons are lengthening globally
  • Frost-free period defines the growing season; last-spring-frost to first-fall-frost
  • A single global mean is meaningless — must condition on latitude/zone
  • Bimodality in mid-latitude data often signals a threshold (continental vs. maritime influence)
