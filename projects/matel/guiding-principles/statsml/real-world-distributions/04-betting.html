Betting & Gambling — Distribution Patterns

HARD WALL AT ZERO

Roulette Profit/Loss — Going Broke Is a One-Way Door

Imagine you walk into a casino with $100. Most nights, your results look like a gentle hill — some wins, some losses. But there's a giant spike at "lost everything." Why? Because once your money hits zero, you're done. You can't un-broke yourself. That's the one-way door: the game ends permanently the moment your wallet empties, creating a pileup of players at -100%.

  • Most sessions create a smooth hill of results (some up, some down) because you're adding up many small bets
  • The spike at -100% is everyone who hit zero and got kicked out of the game permanently
  • The hill leans slightly left because the house always has a small edge on every spin
  • Play long enough and hitting zero isn't bad luck — it's a mathematical certainty
VERY NARROW BELL

Sports Bettor Returns — The House Cut Is Inescapable

Picture thousands of sports bettors tracked over their lifetimes. The shape of their returns is a surprisingly skinny bell, centered right around -5%. Almost nobody ends up above zero. The more bets someone places, the narrower this bell gets — meaning the "randomness" that lets some people feel lucky early on gradually disappears, and everyone converges to the same losing outcome.

  • The bell sits at -4% to -7%, which is exactly the house cut baked into every bet
  • Virtually no one ends up above +2% over a lifetime of betting
  • More bets = less randomness = more certainty you'll land at the house edge
  • "Skilled bettors" are usually just people who haven't bet enough times yet for their luck to average out
NO MEMORY — NO PATTERNS

Slot Payout Gaps — The Machine Doesn't Remember You

How many spins between wins? The shape here is a steep downhill slope: most wins come quickly, but long dry spells are totally normal. The key insight is that this shape proves the machine has no memory. Whether you just won or haven't won in 50 spins, your odds on the next spin are identical. There is no "hot streak" or "due for a win" — the machine literally cannot track what happened before.

  • The steep downhill slope is what "no memory" looks like — it means every spin starts fresh
  • People see patterns in random data, but this shape mathematically disproves streaks
  • After a long drought, the machine doesn't "owe" you anything — next spin has the same odds as always
  • The shape itself is the proof: if patterns existed, you'd see bumps or waves instead of a smooth decline
SPIKE + LONG SLOPE

Poker Tournaments — Two Games Happening at One Table

Look at when people get eliminated in a poker tournament and you'll see two distinct groups hiding in one chart. There's a spike of players who bust out almost immediately (the "all-in every hand" crowd), and then a gradual spread of everyone else who played cautiously and accumulated chips slowly. Same table, same cards, but two completely different games being played.

  • The early-bust spike = aggressive players who go all-in and flame out fast
  • The gradual spread = patient players who build their chip stacks slowly over time
  • Two completely different strategies are visible in a single chart — that's remarkable
  • The ratio between the spike and the spread tells you what playing style dominates the field
MOSTLY ZEROS WITH RARE JACKPOTS

Daily Fantasy Winnings — It's a Lottery, Not a Skill Game

Here's the shape of daily fantasy sports winnings: 80% of players win literally nothing, a small group wins back 1-3 times their entry fee, and a tiny fraction hits absurd payouts (10,000 times their bet). If skill were the main factor, you'd see a smooth gradient from worst to best. Instead, the shape is identical to a lottery ticket — a massive pile at zero and a few extreme winners.

  • 80% of players walk away with nothing — the single biggest bar is at $0
  • The shape looks exactly like a lottery, not like a skill-based competition
  • The extreme jackpots (10,000x) reward lucky variance, not superior knowledge
  • Top players enter thousands of lineups hoping to get lucky on a few — that's a lottery strategy, not a skill strategy
