Rental Properties — Distribution Patterns

5 distributions that expose the hidden dynamics of rental income, compounding effects, and why even tiny annual increases transform long-term wealth

COMPOUNDING SPREAD

Annual Rent Increase — Even 5% Creates Exponential Divergence

A 5% annual rent increase on $2,000/month rent doesn't feel like much in year 1 ($100/month). But it's compounding against a FIXED mortgage. By year 15 you're collecting $4,158/month on the same $1,400 fixed payment. The spread is exponential — the gap itself accelerates.

  • Year 1: $2,000 rent − $1,400 mortgage = $600/mo cash flow
  • Year 10: $3,258 rent − $1,400 mortgage = $1,858/mo
  • Year 20: $5,307 rent − $1,400 mortgage = $3,907/mo
  • Year 30: $8,644 rent − $1,400 mortgage = $7,244/mo
  • Without increase: stuck at $600/mo for 30 years (inflation eats it)
WEALTH DIVERGENCE

Cumulative Cash Flow — 5% Increase vs Flat (20 Years)

The cumulative effect is staggering. With flat rent, 20 years of $600/month = $144K total cash flow. With 5% annual increases, the same property generates $396K — nearly 3x more. The curves start identical and slowly diverge, then the gap EXPLODES after year 10.

  • Flat rent 20-year total: $144,000
  • 5% increase 20-year total: $396,000
  • Difference: $252,000 — from "just 5%"
  • The first 5 years look similar — the magic is in years 10-20
  • At 3% increase: $266K (still 1.85x flat)
FAT-TAILED SHOCKS

Maintenance Cost — Zero-Inflated Pareto

Most months cost $0 in maintenance. But when something breaks, the distribution of repair costs is Pareto — heavy right tail. A $200 faucet fix is common; a $15,000 roof replacement is uncommon but NOT rare. Budgeting the mean ($200/month) fails because the MEDIAN is $0 and the tail drives everything.

  • 60% of months: $0 maintenance
  • 30% of months: $50-$500 (minor fixes)
  • 8% of months: $500-$3,000 (appliances, plumbing)
  • 2% of months: $3,000-$20,000 (roof, HVAC, foundation)
  • Mean ≠ typical — plan for the tail, not the average
MEMORYLESS RISK

Vacancy Duration — Geometric (Memoryless)

Each month a vacant unit has a fixed probability p ≈ 0.4 of being filled (in a good market). The resulting duration is geometric — memoryless. A unit vacant 3 months isn't "due" to be filled; it has the same 40% chance next month as it did the first. This kills the "it's been empty so long, someone will come soon" fallacy.

  • Median vacancy: ~1.4 months (not the same as mean)
  • Mean vacancy: 2.5 months
  • 5% chance of 6+ month vacancy (market-dependent)
  • Each vacant month = full mortgage payment from savings
  • 1 month vacancy = losing 8.3% of annual rent income
DISTRIBUTION SHIFT

Net Annual Return — Year 1 vs Year 10 vs Year 20

The return distribution doesn't just shift right — it WIDENS and becomes right-skewed. Year 1 return is tight around 3-5% (rent barely covers costs). By year 10, rent has compounded but costs are split between fixed (mortgage, insurance) and variable (maintenance), so the distribution spreads. By year 20, even bad scenarios are profitable.

  • Year 1: mean return 4%, range -2% to 8% (can lose money)
  • Year 10: mean return 9%, range 3% to 14%
  • Year 20: mean return 16%, range 8% to 22%
  • Tax depreciation ($10K/yr for 27.5 years) shifts everything up
  • Loan interest deduction strongest in early years
