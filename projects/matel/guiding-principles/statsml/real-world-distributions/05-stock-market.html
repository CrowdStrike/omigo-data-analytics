Stock Market & Trading — Distribution Patterns

FAT-TAILED (LEPTOKURTIC)

Daily Returns (10σ Events Happen 1000× Too Often)

Looks Gaussian at the center, but 10σ events happen 1000× more often than normal predicts. The fat tails caused 2008, LTCM, every "once in 10,000 years" crash that happens every decade. The derivatives industry prices options using Gaussian tails — the mispricing of the true tail shape IS where trading profit exists.

  • Center looks normal — passes naive tests
  • 10σ events occur 1000× more than Gaussian predicts
  • Fat tails caused 2008, LTCM, every "impossible" crash
  • Options mispriced by Gaussian assumption = trading edge
U-SHAPED (BATHTUB)

Intraday Volume (Who's Trading When)

High volume at market open (9:30am), drops to valley at lunch, spikes again at 3:30pm close. The U-shape reveals institutional behavior — funds trade at open (overnight news) and close (index rebalancing). Midday = retail traders only.

  • Open spike = institutions reacting to overnight news
  • Close spike = index rebalancing, end-of-day positioning
  • Midday valley = retail-only, low liquidity
  • Shape tells you WHO is in the market at each hour
LOG-NORMAL + SPIKES

Bid-Ask Spread (Regulation Visible in the Shape)

Right-skewed hill with needle spikes at 1¢, 2¢, 5¢. The spikes are regulatory artifacts — minimum tick sizes force discretization. The log-normal body is the "true" liquidity cost, but regulation rounds it up.

  • Spikes at 1¢, 2¢, 5¢ = minimum tick size (regulatory artifact)
  • Log-normal body = true liquidity cost
  • Regulation forces discretization on continuous underlying cost
  • Market structure rules readable from distribution shape
HEAVY-TAILED CLUSTERED

Time Between Trades (Herding, Not Random Arrivals)

NOT exponential (random arrivals). Instead: many rapid-fire bursts (millisecond scale) plus long quiet gaps. The clustering proves herding — traders react to each other, not independent information. Bursty shape = algorithmic cascade = flash crash precursor.

  • Not exponential = arrivals NOT independent (herding)
  • Rapid-fire bursts = algorithmic cascades
  • Long quiet gaps = no information, no activity
  • Clustering pattern = flash crash precursor signal
LAPLACE (DOUBLE EXPONENTIAL)

Earnings Surprise (Companies Hit the Number on Purpose)

Sharp peak at zero (analysts "nail it"), then symmetric exponential tails both directions. The sharp peak isn't analyst skill — it's companies managing earnings to match consensus. The tails = when they can't hide it anymore (fraud unraveling, genuine shock).

  • Sharp peak at zero = companies managing to match consensus
  • NOT analyst prediction skill — it's corporate manipulation
  • Symmetric exponential tails = genuine surprises (can't be hidden)
  • Tail events = fraud collapse or real macro shock
ASYMMETRIC FEAR

Overnight Gap Returns (Fear Falls Faster Than Greed Rises)

The distribution of overnight gaps (close-to-open returns) is fat-tailed and asymmetric. Negative gaps are larger and more frequent than positive gaps of the same magnitude. This reflects news arriving after hours — bad news triggers immediate selling at open, while good news gets absorbed more gradually. The skewness means symmetric risk models (VaR assuming normal) systematically underestimate downside overnight exposure.

  • Negative skew: -0.4 to -0.8 (fear moves faster)
  • Tail index: ~3.5 (finite variance, but barely)
  • Magnitude: average gap ±0.3%, but 2%+ gaps happen 10x more than normal predicts
  • Earnings nights: gap distribution becomes bimodal (beat/miss)
  • Weekend effect: Monday gaps have fatter tails (2 nights of news)
PRICED TAIL RISK

Options Implied Volatility Smile (U-Shaped Pricing)

If the market believed returns were normally distributed, implied volatility would be flat across strike prices. Instead it forms a "smile" (U-shape) or "smirk" (left-skewed U). Deep out-of-the-money puts have higher IV because the market knows left-tail events are more likely than normal suggests. The smile IS the market's admission that it doesn't believe in Gaussian returns — and the steepness tells you how scared the market is right now.

  • ATM (at-the-money): baseline IV, ~20% for SPX typically
  • OTM puts (left wing): IV 25-35% — market prices crash risk
  • OTM calls (right wing): IV 22-28% — mild, less extreme
  • Steepness of left wing = market's "fear gauge" (correlated with VIX)
  • Post-2008: smile became permanent smirk (left wing never flattened)
  • Pre-1987 (Black Monday): smile didn't exist — market believed normal
