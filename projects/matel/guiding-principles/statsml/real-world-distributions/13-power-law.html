Power Law — Real-World Examples

A few items dominate; the vast majority are tiny. No meaningful average exists — the distribution is scale-free. P(X > x) ∝ x(-α). Often misidentified; many claimed power laws are actually log-normal.

City Populations (α≈1.1, Zipf's Law)

HEAVY TAIL
  • Preferential attachment — big cities attract more people, creating a feedback loop.
  • Zipf's law: rank × size ≈ constant.
  • Mean city size is meaningless because α < 2 implies infinite variance.

Word Frequencies in Text (Zipf, α≈1)

ZIPF'S LAW
  • "the" appears ~7% of the time, next word ~3.5%, etc.
  • 80% of text uses only 20% of vocabulary.
  • Connected to information theory — frequent words carry less information per occurrence.

Wealth Distribution (α≈1.5)

INFINITE VARIANCE
  • Multiplicative returns compound inequality — wealth begets wealth.
  • α < 2 means infinite variance; no stable mean emerges even with large samples.
  • Gini coefficient close to 1 for the upper tail.

Crater Sizes on the Moon (α≈2)

SCALE-FREE
  • Impactor size distribution is itself power-law.
  • Energy ∝ mass × velocity² creates scaling between impactor and crater size.
  • Small craters vastly outnumber large ones — ratio is predictable from α.

Forest Fire Areas (α≈1.3)

SELF-ORGANIZED CRITICALITY
  • Self-organized criticality — fires can spread without bound until fuel runs out.
  • Fire suppression may increase α (fewer large fires), but this is debated.
  • Area distribution spans several orders of magnitude.

Earthquake Energy Release (Gutenberg-Richter)

LOG-SCALE
  • Magnitude scale is log10(energy); each unit = 31.6× more energy.
  • The b-value ≈ 1 for most tectonic regions.
  • Predicting large events from small-event statistics is the holy grail of seismology.
