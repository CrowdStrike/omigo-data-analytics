Self-Driving — Distribution Patterns

BIMODAL BETA

Detection Confidence (The Dangerous Middle Is the Safety Metric)

Mass piled at >0.95 (confident detections) and <0.1 (confident negatives), with thin dangerous middle (0.3–0.7). The thinness of the middle = safety metric. A fat middle means the car doesn't know what it's seeing too often. You can read "is this car safe?" from the confidence shape.

  • Mass at >0.95 = confident correct detections
  • Mass at <0.1 = confident negatives (nothing there)
  • Thin middle (0.3–0.7) = dangerous uncertainty zone
  • Fat middle = unsafe model, thin middle = safe model
WEIBULL (MATURITY METRIC)

Disengagement Intervals (Weibull k = Software Maturity)

Early software = exponential (k=1, memoryless random failures). Mature software = Weibull k>2 (failures cluster in specific conditions). The shift from exponential to high-k Weibull proves graduation from random bugs to known hard scenarios. The shape parameter IS the maturity metric.

  • Early: k=1 (exponential) = random failures everywhere
  • Mature: k>2 (Weibull) = failures only in specific scenarios
  • Shift from k=1 to k>2 = "random bugs" → "known hard cases"
  • Shape parameter k = direct safety maturity measurement
FAT-TAILED (CAUCHY)

Pedestrian Prediction Error (No Finite Safety Margin Exists)

Tight prediction for straight-walking pedestrians, but extreme errors for sudden direction changes. Tails never converge. Proves you cannot bound pedestrian unpredictability. Gaussian error models systematically underestimate worst-case — no fixed safety margin is sufficient.

  • Tight center = predictable straight-line walkers
  • Extreme tails = sudden direction changes (children, distracted)
  • Tails never converge (no finite variance)
  • Any fixed safety margin is eventually exceeded
BIMODAL (FAST VS ARBITRATION)

Sensor Fusion Latency (Agreement Is Instant, Conflict Is Blind)

Spike at 10–15ms (sensors agree, direct pipeline) and second mode at 50–80ms (sensors disagree, arbitration). The gap between modes = cost of conflict resolution. At 60mph, the 50ms mode = 4 feet of travel blind. The shape quantifies when the car is flying blind.

  • Spike at 10–15ms = sensors agree (fast path)
  • Second mode 50–80ms = sensors disagree (arbitration)
  • Gap between modes = conflict resolution cost
  • 50ms at 60mph = 4 feet of blind travel
RIGHT-SKEWED (LOG-NORMAL)

Miles Between Scenarios (99% of Test Driving Generates Zero Learning)

Extremely right-skewed — most miles are boring (straight highway, clear day). Interesting scenarios cluster together (construction zones, school zones). Proves random test driving is absurdly inefficient. Simulation amplifies the rare left tail artificially — explains why billions of sim miles are needed.

  • Most miles = zero learning value (boring, repetitive)
  • Interesting scenarios cluster (not uniformly distributed)
  • Random driving = absurdly inefficient for testing
  • Simulation exists to amplify the rare-scenario tail
