Energy & Utilities — Distribution Patterns

Bimodal Household Power Consumption

Weekday pattern (high, HVAC + appliances) vs weekend/night (low baseline). Daily and weekly seasonality baked into the distribution shape.

  • Peak cluster: HVAC, cooking, laundry during daytime hours
  • Off-peak cluster: standby loads, refrigeration, overnight baseline
  • Transition periods create a middle bridge between modes
  • Rare spikes from high-draw events (EV charging, space heaters)

Why it matters: Bimodal nature breaks single-Gaussian assumptions. Time-of-use pricing creates additional structure. Any model assuming unimodal consumption will misestimate both peaks and troughs.

Weibull Wind Speed

Classic Weibull(k=2, lambda=7) shape: right-skewed, bounded below at 0, rarely exceeds 25 m/s. This is the theoretical distribution for wind speed at most sites.

  • Shape parameter k approx 2 for most inland and coastal sites
  • Scale parameter lambda reflects mean wind resource (site-specific)
  • Turbine cut-in speed (~3 m/s) and cut-out speed (~25 m/s) create operational windows
  • Energy output proportional to wind speed cubed — tail matters disproportionately

Why it matters: Weibull is the accepted theoretical model. Normal approximations fail badly due to the zero-bound and right skew. Capacity factor calculations depend on getting the tail right.

Zero-Inflated + Bounded Solar Panel Output

Zero at night (roughly 50% of day), bounded 0-1 (fraction of max capacity), beta-like shape when producing.

  • Structural zeros from nighttime — not missing data, not errors
  • Bounded above by panel rated capacity (never exceeds nameplate)
  • Cloud intermittency creates high variance during daylight hours
  • Beta(2,3) shape when producing: left-skewed, rarely at full capacity

Why it matters: Zero-inflation requires mixture models. Standard regression on raw output conflates "no sun" with "low production." The bounded support rules out normal/exponential families.

Tight Normal + Rare Excursions Grid Frequency Deviation

Extremely tight normal around 50.00 Hz (49.95–50.05 Hz typical) with rare excursions during demand-supply imbalances.

  • Power grid must maintain exactly 50 Hz (or 60 Hz) — deviations measured in millihertz
  • 99% of the time, normal assumption works perfectly
  • Rare events (generator trips, sudden load changes) create critical tail excursions
  • Under-frequency relay trips at ~49.8 Hz — system protection activates

Why it matters: The normal model is accurate for operations but dangerous for risk. The tails matter most — a 200 mHz deviation triggers load shedding. Extreme value theory needed for protective relay settings.

Log-Normal + Digit Heaping Monthly Electricity Bill

Right-skewed with seasonal peaks, spikes at round numbers ($100, $150, $200), floor at minimum connection fee (~$15).

  • Multiplicative factors: sq footage x rate x season x occupancy
  • Round-number bunching from budget billing and levelized payment plans
  • Minimum charge floor: even zero-usage accounts pay connection fee
  • Heavy right tail from large homes, commercial accounts, or extreme weather

Why it matters: Digit heaping violates continuity assumptions. The floor truncation and round-number spikes create a mixture that no single parametric family fits. Seasonality means the distribution shifts month to month.
