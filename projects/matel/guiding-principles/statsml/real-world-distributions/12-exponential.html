Exponential Distribution — Real-World Examples

The waiting time distribution. Memoryless: however long you've waited, the expected remaining wait is the same. Continuous counterpart of the geometric distribution.

MEMORYLESS Time Between Earthquakes (λ=0.02/day for M>4)

Days between magnitude 4+ earthquakes in a seismic region.

  • When events follow a Poisson process (constant average rate, independent occurrences), inter-arrival times are exponential
  • The memoryless property: P(wait > s+t | already waited s) = P(wait > t) — past waiting gives no information about future waiting
  • This is why "overdue for an earthquake" is a fallacy — the exponential has no memory of elapsed time
  • Mean wait = 1/λ = 50 days. The mode is always 0 (most likely interval is very short)
  • Generated: 1000 draws from Exp(0.02)

POISSON PROCESS Radioactive Decay Intervals

Time between successive alpha particle emissions from a radioactive sample.

  • The original example that motivated the Poisson/exponential framework (Rutherford & Geiger, 1910)
  • Each atom decays independently with a fixed probability per unit time — quantum mechanical independence guarantees the memoryless property
  • At λ=5/sec, mean inter-arrival = 0.2 seconds. Geiger counter clicks sound irregular because exponential variance is high relative to mean (CV=1 always)
  • Half-life relates to λ: t½ = ln(2)/λ. The exponential CDF gives the fraction decayed by time t
  • Generated: 1000 draws from Exp(5), values in fractions of seconds

INSPECTION PARADOX Time Until Next Bus (λ=0.1/min)

Wait time when you arrive at a random moment at a bus stop (average headway 10 min).

  • If buses arrive as a Poisson process, your wait is Exp(λ) regardless of when you arrive — this is the memoryless property in action
  • The inspection paradox: your average wait is NOT half the headway. You're more likely to land in a long gap than a short one, so average experienced wait = mean gap (10 min), not half of it
  • For regular (non-Poisson) buses with fixed 10-min intervals, average wait IS 5 min. The exponential case is the worst-case scenario for the rider
  • Real transit: somewhere between (scheduled but with variance). The more random, the closer to exponential
  • Generated: 1000 draws from Exp(0.1), units in minutes

CONSTANT HAZARD Light Bulb Lifetime (Constant Hazard Rate)

Hours until failure for bulbs with no wear-out mechanism (idealized constant failure rate).

  • Constant hazard rate h(t) = λ means the bulb is equally likely to fail in the next hour whether it's brand new or 5000 hours old — no aging
  • Real incandescent bulbs violate this: filament thinning creates an increasing hazard (Weibull with shape > 1)
  • LEDs are closer to exponential in early life (random manufacturing defects dominate), then transition to wear-out failure modes
  • Mean lifetime = 1/λ = 1000 hours. Only 37% survive past one mean lifetime (e&supmin;¹ ≈ 0.368)
  • Generated: 1000 draws from Exp(0.001), units in hours

SPATIAL POISSON Distance Between Roadkill on a Highway

Kilometers between animal carcasses on a straight stretch of highway.

  • A spatial Poisson process: events distributed along a one-dimensional line (the road) with roughly constant rate per km
  • Independence assumption: each animal crossing is independent of others, occurring at random points along the route
  • At λ=0.5/km, mean spacing = 2 km. Clustering near habitat crossings (forests, streams) violates the constant-rate assumption
  • Ecologists test for non-homogeneous Poisson (varying λ) to identify high-risk wildlife corridors
  • Generated: 1000 draws from Exp(0.5), units in kilometers

APPROXIMATION LIMITS Time Between Volcanic Eruptions (Regional)

Years between eruptions for a moderately active volcanic region.

  • At λ=0.15/year, mean inter-eruption interval ≈ 6.7 years. The exponential is a first-order approximation for "well-behaved" volcanoes with steady magma supply
  • The memoryless assumption works when eruptions are driven by independent random pressure events rather than pressure accumulation
  • Violations: clustered eruptions (aftershock sequences), pressure-buildup volcanoes where hazard increases with time since last eruption (Weibull better)
  • Historical records are often too short (few hundred years) to reliably estimate λ or test exponentiality for rare volcanoes
  • Generated: 1000 draws from Exp(0.15), units in years
