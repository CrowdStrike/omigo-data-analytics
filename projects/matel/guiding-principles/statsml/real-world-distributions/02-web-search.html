Web Search / Ranking — Distribution Patterns

POWER LAW (ZIPF)

Query Frequency (Zipf's Law)

Query frequencies follow a steep power law — the top 100 queries account for 20% of all traffic while billions of unique queries form the long tail. The head needs hand-tuning, the tail has no training signal. You can't serve both with one system.

  • Top 100 queries = 20% of traffic
  • Long tail has billions of unique queries with no learning signal
  • Head vs tail requires fundamentally different engineering
  • Log-log plot is perfectly linear — textbook power law
EXPONENTIAL DECAY

Position CTR (Trust Drives Clicks, Not Relevance)

Click-through rate decays exponentially by rank position — same curve regardless of what content is placed there. Google proved via swap experiments that CTR barely changes per position. The ad auction model charges for position trust, not relevance.

  • Geometric decay (#1=30%, #2=15%...) regardless of content swapped
  • Position trust > relevance — proved by swap experiments
  • Ad revenue IS this distribution shape
  • Same shape cross-platform (Google, Bing, Amazon)
BIMODAL

Dwell Time (Bounce vs Engaged — No Middle)

Dwell time shows a spike at 2-5 seconds (bounce/misclick), a dead zone at 5-15 seconds, and a broad hill at 30-120 seconds (engaged reading). No "moderate interest" exists — users either bounced or committed.

  • Two populations: bouncers (2-5 sec) and engaged readers (30-120 sec)
  • Dead zone 5-15 sec = no moderate interest exists
  • "Average dwell time" conflates two opposite signals
  • Must segment before using dwell as quality metric
GEOMETRIC

Queries Per Session (Constant Give-Up Rate)

40% of sessions have exactly 1 query, 25% have 2, 15% have 3... a constant "stop" probability after each query. The slope directly measures search success rate. Paradox: better search = steeper decay = fewer queries = less ad revenue.

  • Constant stop probability readable from slope
  • Steeper decay = higher per-query success rate
  • Better search → fewer queries → less ad revenue (paradox)
  • Geometric shape proves each query is independent coin flip of "found it?"
MIXTURE

Query Reformulation Gap (Failure Signature)

Time between consecutive queries shows a spike at 3-8 seconds (quick reword = dissatisfaction) and an exponential tail out to minutes (new intent/topic). The boundary between fast and slow = where "one search task" ends.

  • Fast spike (3-8 sec) = failure/rewording same intent
  • Long tail = new topic/intent entirely
  • Boundary between them = session stitching decision point
  • Spike mass / total = query failure rate (directly measurable)
