Finance & Markets — Distribution Patterns

Common shapes encountered in financial data, each with the modeling pitfall they create.

FAT TAILS Daily Stock Returns

Looks Gaussian at first glance, but the tails contain 5-10x more mass than a normal distribution predicts.

  • Excess kurtosis of 3-10 (vs 0 for Gaussian) means extreme moves happen far more often than expected
  • VaR models using normal assumptions underestimate tail risk — the 2008 crash was a "25-sigma event" under Gaussian assumptions
  • Generated here as a mixture: 90% N(0,2) + 10% N(0,10) — the heavy-tailed component models panic/euphoria regimes

FLOOR + SKEW Bid-Ask Spread

Bounded below by the minimum tick size (e.g., $0.01), with extreme right skew for illiquid names.

  • Market microstructure imposes a hard floor — spreads cannot go below the tick
  • Liquid large-caps cluster tightly near the minimum; illiquid small-caps have wide, variable spreads
  • Log-transform helps but does not fully normalize because the floor creates a point mass
  • Generated: floor at 0.01, mixture of exponentials (fast/slow decay) plus a uniform tail

CENSORING + SPIKES Loan Default Timing

A mixture of continuous survival times and discrete spikes at term boundaries.

  • ~60% of loans survive to maturity (spike at 360 months) — this is right-censoring, not a "mode"
  • Early defaults follow an exponential-like hazard in the first 2-3 years
  • Clusters appear at 60-month and 120-month marks due to refinancing and ARM resets
  • Standard regression on default time fails without survival analysis (Kaplan-Meier, Cox PH)

REGIME SWITCHING Portfolio Volatility

Volatility is not constant — calm periods dominate, but crisis regimes produce extreme readings.

  • 70% of days live in a low-vol regime (VIX ~12-15), creating the tall left mode
  • Occasional regime shifts to medium-vol (VIX 20-30) and high-vol (VIX 40+) create a long right tail
  • GARCH models capture volatility clustering; Markov-switching models capture discrete regimes
  • Using a single distribution for vol underestimates crisis-period risk by 3-5x

MIXED NORMAL Interest Rate Changes

Tiny daily fluctuations punctuated by rare large jumps at central bank meeting dates.

  • ~90% of days show noise-level moves (a few basis points) — almost zero drift
  • ~10% of days (scheduled meetings, surprise announcements) produce jumps of 25bps or more
  • The mixture creates excess kurtosis and bimodality in the tails, not the center
  • Jump-diffusion models (Merton) or mixed-normal GARCH handle this; plain diffusion cannot
