Insurance & Actuarial — Distribution Patterns

Claim Amounts (Zero-Inflated Pareto)

SEVERITY VS FREQUENCY

Most policyholders never claim (zero), non-zero claims have extreme right tail.

  • Severity vs frequency separation — modeling them independently is standard actuarial practice
  • Mean claim is meaningless when distribution is zero-inflated with heavy tail
  • Reinsurance thresholds define layers where different carriers absorb risk

Time Between Claims (Exponential)

MEMORYLESS PROPERTY

Memoryless: each day equally likely to have a claim.

  • Poisson process for claim frequency — arrivals are independent
  • Homogeneous assumption breaks for seasonal perils (hurricanes, winter storms)
  • Credibility weighting blends individual experience with portfolio-level rates

Loss Ratio (Bounded)

CAN EXCEED 1

Premiums vs claims, bounded but can exceed 1 (unprofitable).

  • >1 means paying out more than collected — underwriting loss
  • Catastrophe years create right spike well above 1
  • Actuarial tables use censored data to handle incomplete development

Policy Duration (Survival)

PERIODIC SPIKES

Most policies renew annually (spikes at 12, 24, 36 months), some lapse early.

  • Annual renewal creates periodic spikes in duration distribution
  • Early lapse is anti-selection — higher-risk policyholders leave sooner
  • Survivorship bias in long-duration cohorts skews profitability estimates

Catastrophe Loss (Extreme Value)

EXTREME VALUE

Very rare, very large: hurricane/earthquake losses.

  • Generalized Extreme Value distribution governs block maxima
  • Normal approximation is catastrophically wrong for tail risk
  • Reinsurance pricing depends on modeling 1-in-100 year events accurately
