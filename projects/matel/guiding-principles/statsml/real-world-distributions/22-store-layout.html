Store Layout & Planograms — Distribution Patterns

5 distributions that reveal how retail science engineers every square foot for maximum spend per visit

SPATIAL POWER LAW

Zone Traffic (Power Law)

Foot traffic follows a power law — entrance zones and endcaps get 10x the visits of mid-aisle positions. A tiny fraction of floor space captures most of the eyeballs, making placement the single highest-leverage variable in retail.

  • Pareto(alpha=1.5, xmin=10) — heavy right tail
  • Entrance/endcap zones = 10x mid-aisle traffic
  • 20% of floor space captures 80% of attention
  • Rent per sq ft should follow the same power law
EYE LEVEL = BUY LEVEL

Eye-Level Shelf (Normal 120-150cm)

Customer gaze fixation follows a tight normal centered at 135cm — the "eye level" zone. Products placed here get 2.5x the conversion rate of knee or top-shelf positions. Brands pay premium slotting fees for this narrow band.

  • Normal(135, 12) — tight concentration at eye height
  • Eye level (120-150cm) = 2.5x conversion
  • Top shelf: aspirational brands (low volume, high margin)
  • Floor level: kids' products (their eye level)
GRAB VS BROWSE

Dwell Time by Aisle (Bimodal)

Dwell time is bimodal — quick-grab shoppers (exponential, mean 15s) and browsers (normal, mean 200s). The "average dwell time" of 90s describes nobody. Planogram strategy must serve both modes or lose one entirely.

  • Mode 1: Grab shoppers — exponential(mean=15s)
  • Mode 2: Browse shoppers — normal(200s, sd=50)
  • Grocery aisles skew grab; electronics skews browse
  • Mean dwell time (90s) describes neither population
PATH ENGINEERING

Fresh Perimeter vs Center (Gradient)

Spending per minute is bimodal — perimeter (fresh/high-margin) averages $8/min while center (packaged goods) averages $3/min. Store layout forces the longest possible path through high-margin perimeter zones before reaching essentials.

  • Perimeter: Normal(8, 2) — fresh, deli, bakery
  • Center: Normal(3, 1.5) — packaged, canned, dry
  • Layout forces longest path through impulse zones
  • Essentials (milk, eggs) always at the back
MOST EXPENSIVE REAL ESTATE

End-Cap Conversion Lift (Log-Normal)

End-cap conversion lift follows a log-normal — most endcaps give a modest 1.5-3x lift, but the right combination (new product + seasonal + promo) can deliver 8x+. The fat right tail is why brands pay $50K/week for prime endcap slots.

  • Log-normal: exp(Normal(0.7, 0.5))
  • Median lift ~2x, but tail extends to 8-10x
  • New product launches get highest multipliers
  • $50K/week slotting fee justified by conversion math
