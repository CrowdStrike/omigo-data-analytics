Real Estate — Distribution Patterns

MIXTURE OF NORMALS

Home Prices (School District Shatters Aggregate)

Aggregate looks log-normal (smooth right skew). Split by school rating and it shatters into 3-4 tight normals with different means and variances. The "housing market" doesn't exist — there are N parallel markets stacked. A home crossing from a 6-rated to 8-rated school zone jumps $80K overnight with zero physical change.

  • Aggregate: looks log-normal (misleading smooth curve)
  • By school district: shatters into 3-4 tight normals
  • "The housing market" is N parallel markets stacked
  • School zone boundary = $80K jump, zero physical change
DISCRETE SPIKES

Lot Size (Fossil Record of Zoning Codes)

Not continuous at all. Needle spikes at 0.15, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0 acres. Almost nothing between. Lots come in whatever the developer platted in that era. 1960s = 0.25 acres. 2010s infill = 0.12 acres. The distribution is a fossil record of zoning codes. You can date neighborhoods from lot size spikes.

  • Needle spikes at 0.15, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0 acres
  • Almost nothing between (not continuous)
  • Each spike = a developer/era's standard plat
  • Can date neighborhood construction from spike positions
BIMODAL (TWO REGIMES)

Price/SqFt (Land-Value vs Structure-Value)

One mode at $150-$250/sqft (suburbs — you're paying for the house). Second mode at $800-$1500/sqft (urban core — you're paying for location). In the left mode, renovation adds value. In the right mode, teardowns sell for nearly the same price. The valley = where investment logic flips.

  • Left mode ($150-250): structure dominates (renovation adds value)
  • Right mode ($800-1500): land dominates (teardown ≈ renovated price)
  • Valley between = geographic line where logic flips
  • "Price/sqft" means two different things depending on mode
EXPONENTIAL + SPIKE

Days on Market (Spike = Market Thermometer)

Spike at 0-3 days (bidding wars, sold over asking), then exponential decay for the rest. The spike mass IS the market temperature — more predictive than median price or inventory. Hot market = 40% sell in 72 hours. Cold = spike shrinks to 5%. Detects turning points 2-3 months before headlines.

  • Spike at 0-3 days = bidding wars (sold immediately)
  • Exponential tail for rest of market
  • Spike mass = most predictive market health metric
  • Detects turning points months before Zillow/headlines
STEP FUNCTION

School Rating Premium (Binary, Not Linear)

Price premium is flat from rating 1-5, then a vertical jump at rating 7, then flat again 7-10. Buyers don't pay proportionally — they pay for a threshold. Below 7 = "bad schools" (undifferentiated). Above 7 = "good schools" (also undifferentiated). The cliff proves buyers use binary heuristic.

  • Flat from 1-5 (all "bad" = no premium difference)
  • Vertical jump at 7 (the threshold)
  • Flat from 7-10 (all "good" = undifferentiated)
  • Cliff location varies by city but always exists
