Domain Pitfalls: Employee Surveys

Pitfall Visualization
1. Social Desirability Bias
Employees say what they think leadership wants to hear; "I'm highly engaged!" = safe answer; "I hate my job" = risky answer; results skew positive; the delta between survey and reality = the fear factor
2. Non-Response from Disengaged
40% response rate; the 60% who didn't respond are disproportionately: unhappy, checked out, looking elsewhere; responding requires CARING enough to bother; your data oversamples the engaged
3. Likert Scale Compression
5-point scale: nobody uses 1 or 2 (too negative, feels risky); actual range used: 3-5; meaningful differences are between 3.8 and 4.2 — a 0.4 delta that looks trivial but is actually significant in compressed scale
4. Survey Fatigue Over Time
Q1 survey: 80% response, thoughtful answers; Q2: 60% response, shorter answers; Q4: 35% response, random clicking; longitudinal trends confounded by declining response quality; you can't tell if engagement dropped or just survey participation dropped
5. Anonymity Distrust
"This survey is anonymous" — but I'm one of 3 people on this team; with demographic questions: the unique combination identifies me; distrust of anonymity → dishonest answers → data is fiction; small teams can't have real anonymity
6. Action Gap
Survey → results presented → no visible change → next survey: "why bother answering, nothing happens"; each survey without follow-through REDUCES future response quality; surveying without acting = actively harming future data collection
