Airline Scheduling / Cascading Delays

Concept Visualization
One Delayed Flight Cascades to 50+

Aircraft and crew are REUSED across the day. Flight 1 (NYC→CHI) delayed 90 min → same plane does Flight 2 (CHI→LAX): also 90 min late → Flight 3 (LAX→SEA): 90 min late. One delay propagates through the entire day's schedule for that aircraft. Multiply by: crew on that first flight also delayed → their NEXT flight (different aircraft) also delayed. Exponential propagation from single initial event.
Weather at Hub Affects Entire Network

Thunderstorm at Atlanta (Delta hub): 200 flights delayed/cancelled. But: passengers connecting THROUGH Atlanta from 100 cities to 100 other cities → 10,000 itineraries disrupted. The HUB topology amplifies local weather into network-wide chaos. Models predicting "will my flight be delayed?" must model EVERY other flight at EVERY hub the route passes through.
Crew Timeout Regulations Force Cancellations

Pilot legally can't fly after 8-10 hours of duty. Flight delayed 3 hours → crew "times out" at destination → no crew for return flight → cancellation. The delay didn't just shift the schedule — it REMOVED capacity entirely. AND: replacement crew must be flown in (deadhead) → occupying a seat on another flight → reducing available inventory.
Passenger Rebooking Creates Secondary Overload

200 passengers from cancelled flight rebooked to next 3 flights → those flights now oversold → bumps → more rebookings → cascade continues for 24-48 hours after initial event. The DATA shows "flight oversold by 40%" — looks like terrible planning. Actually: consequence of cancellation 6 hours ago on different route.
Maintenance Slot Missed → Aircraft Out of Service

Aircraft had scheduled maintenance at midnight in DEN. Delay means it arrives at 2am instead of 11pm. Maintenance window missed (crew went home). Aircraft now out of service until next available slot (18 hours later). One delay → aircraft removed from service for entire next day → 4-6 flights cancelled that have NOTHING to do with original delay.
Fuel Tankering Decisions from Stale Weather

Airline decides at departure: carry extra fuel (heavy, expensive) or refuel at destination (depends on destination fuel price + availability). Decision based on weather forecast from 3 hours ago. If weather changed → divert → insufficient fuel for diversion → emergency. The DATA used for the decision was correct when computed but STALE when executed.
Ground Truth Takes 24-48 Hours to Settle

"Was this flight delayed?" Seems simple. But: gate departure vs wheels-off vs wheels-on vs gate arrival all different. A flight that left gate on time but sat on taxiway 90 min: "on time" by departure metric, "90 min late" by arrival. DOT metrics use specific definitions — airlines optimize to the METRIC not the passenger experience. Also: cancellation vs "extreme delay" is a classification decision with financial implications.
Schedule Recovery Optimization = NP-Hard

After disruption: reassign 500 aircraft, 3000 crew members, 50,000 passengers to minimize total cost. Constraints: crew legalities, aircraft maintenance, passenger connections, gate availability, fuel, curfews. This is a combinatorial optimization that can't be solved optimally in real-time. Every airline uses heuristics → suboptimal recovery → some passengers stranded for days (Southwest meltdown Dec 2022: 16,700 flights cancelled over 10 days because recovery algorithm failed).
