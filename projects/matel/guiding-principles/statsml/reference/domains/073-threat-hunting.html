Domain Pitfalls: Threat Hunting

Statistical and analytical traps in proactive threat detection and adversary pursuit

Pitfall Visualization
1. Hypothesis-driven search bias
Hunter has theory, searches for confirming evidence, finds it in a dense graph where EVERYTHING connects at 3 hops; the connections are real but meaningless without base-rate comparison; confirmation bias amplified by data richness
2. Dwell time estimation impossible
Attacker has been inside for HOW long? You don't know what you don't know; first evidence found ≠ first intrusion; actual dwell time is always LONGER than detected dwell time; you're always underestimating exposure
3. MITRE ATT&CK coverage gaps
Framework has 200+ techniques; your detection covers 40; the 160 unmonitored techniques are where attackers operate; you hunt in the light, they work in the dark; coverage map shows your blind spots
4. Lateral movement invisible in single-source
Attacker moves: endpoint log shows "login"; network log shows "connection"; auth log shows "ticket granted"; NO SINGLE SOURCE shows the full movement chain; must correlate 3+ sources in real-time
5. Analyst fatigue false-negative
After reviewing 500 alerts, analyst misses alert 501 which was the real one; cognitive capacity depletes; the most important alert arrives when you're mentally exhausted; human-in-the-loop = human-as-weak-link
6. Indicator of Compromise decay
IOC: malicious IP 1.2.3.4 → attacker moves to 5.6.7.8 next week → IOC is stale; IP/domain IOCs half-life: 3-7 days; hash IOCs: one recompile = new hash; hunting with last month's IOCs = hunting ghosts
