Insurance Lines of Business — Each Line Has Unique Data Challenges

General actuarial pitfalls are in doc #7. This covers what's UNIQUE to each line of business.

Line of Business & Pitfall Visualization
1. Homeowners / Property
Catastrophe correlation: 1000 homes all claim simultaneously after one hurricane. Individual claims are "independent" until nature makes them perfectly correlated. Reconstruction cost inflation post-disaster (demand surge: every contractor in the state is booked → 40% cost increase). "Replacement cost" estimated from pre-disaster prices is WRONG post-disaster. Also: hidden renovation value (owner improved kitchen but didn't notify insurer → underinsured).
2. Auto / Motor
Telematics data privacy vs pricing accuracy. Device in car tracks: speed, braking, cornering, time-of-day, locations visited. Perfect risk signal BUT: who owns the data? Can insurer share with police? Does tracking teenager driving to therapy clinic constitute health info disclosure? Also: repair cost inflation from OEM-only parts mandates. A $200 bumper repair in 2015 = $2000 sensor recalibration in 2024 (ADAS cameras in every panel).
3. Health Insurance
Pre-existing condition definition drift. What counts as "pre-existing" changes with diagnostic technology (whole genome sequencing: EVERYONE has a "pre-existing genetic risk"). Also: claims data reflects what's BILLED not what's WRONG with the patient (upcoding for revenue, defensive medicine, procedure-happy providers). And: network effects — patient's cost depends on WHICH hospital they go to, not just what's wrong with them (10× price difference for same procedure across hospitals).
4. General Liability
Settlement takes 5-10 YEARS. Claim filed today → lawsuit → discovery → trial → appeal → final resolution in 2034. You set a reserve in 2024 that must predict the outcome of a lawsuit in 2034 with different judges, juries, laws, and social norms. Also: "social inflation" — jury awards increasing 10-15% annually regardless of actual damages. Your historical data understates future costs by the rate of social inflation.
5. Umbrella / Excess Liability
Stacking coverage gaps. Umbrella sits ABOVE primary policies (auto + home + general). If any underlying policy has a coverage exclusion → gap falls into umbrella. But umbrella doesn't know about primary policy changes. Primary insurer changes exclusion → creates a gap the umbrella didn't price for. Also: the umbrella only triggers for LARGE claims — which by definition are rare (n=tiny) and extreme (fat-tailed). Pricing from n=5-10 historical claims over 20 years.
6. Business / Commercial Property
Business interruption: how do you quantify "lost revenue" for a business that was GROWING when the event happened? "What would revenue have been?" is a counterfactual that can't be observed. Both sides (insurer and insured) have incentives to manipulate the counterfactual estimate. Also: supply chain disruption — factory burns in country X → 100 businesses in country Y can't get parts → all file BI claims → correlated losses from single upstream event.
7. Cyber Insurance
The "uninsurable" line. Cyber risk is: (a) fat-tailed (one breach = $1B), (b) correlated (SolarWinds hit 18,000 organizations simultaneously), (c) rapidly evolving (yesterday's risk model = tomorrow's joke), (d) adversarial (attackers STUDY what's insured and target accordingly — "ransom demand = exactly the policy limit"). Also: no actuarial history. Cyber insurance exists since ~2010. N=15 years of data for a risk that changes every 6 months. Can't price from history.
8. Life Insurance
Longevity risk vs mortality risk (opposite directions). Life insurance company profits when people die ON SCHEDULE. Live too long → annuity business loses money. Die too soon → life policy pays out early. COVID: mortality spike → life insurance losses. But: also revealed that some "dead" policyholders were alive (fraud). Also: underwriting based on health snapshot at APPLICATION time. Person was healthy at 35 → policy issued → person becomes obese at 45 → still paying healthy rates → adverse selection after issue.
9. Workers' Compensation
"Is the injury work-related?" is a LEGAL question not a medical one. Same back pain: if it happened at work = covered. If it happened at home = not covered. But: did it REALLY happen at work or did the employee just report it there? Malingering detection vs legitimate claim: model must predict INTENT, not just medical facts. Also: return-to-work incentives — if benefits are too generous, injured workers have no incentive to recover. Duration of disability claims correlates with benefit generosity, not just injury severity.
10. Reinsurance (Insurance of Insurance)
The "risk of the risk." Reinsurer covers insurer's catastrophic losses. Problem: information asymmetry — insurer knows more about their book than reinsurer. Insurer may CEDE their worst risks selectively. Also: retrocessional layers (reinsurance of reinsurance of reinsurance) — create interconnected risk where one major event cascades through 5 layers. Nobody knows the TOTAL exposure because it's distributed across layers. AIG 2008: nobody knew how much risk was concentrated until it collapsed simultaneously.
