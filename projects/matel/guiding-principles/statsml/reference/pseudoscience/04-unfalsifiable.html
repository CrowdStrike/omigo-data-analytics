Unfalsifiable Claims

Logical Fallacy — Claims structured so no possible evidence could disprove them

"It Works — You Just Need More Data / More Time / To Believe Harder"
  • Homeopathy: "The study didn't show an effect? The sample was too small. Also the measuring instrument wasn't sensitive enough. Also you have to take it for 6 months before it works." No possible evidence could DISPROVE the claim — it's unfalsifiable.
  • ML model: "Model accuracy is only 52%? We need more training data. More features. More epochs. A bigger model. Different hyperparameters." At what point do you conclude the signal doesn't exist? If NO result could make you abandon the approach, it's faith not science.
  • Business strategy: "OKRs didn't improve team performance? You're not doing them RIGHT. Need better alignment. More check-ins. Different cadence." The methodology can never fail — only the practitioner can fail. Unfalsifiable by design.
  • Technical debt: "Rewrite will fix everything!" Rewrite didn't fix it? "We need ANOTHER rewrite. The first one didn't go far enough." Infinite regress where the cure for failure is more of the same treatment.

Why it's pseudoscience: Karl Popper's demarcation criterion: science must be FALSIFIABLE. If you define what evidence would make you ABANDON the claim, and then observe that evidence, and STILL don't abandon it — you're not doing science. Pre-define your failure criteria: "If accuracy is <60% after 100K samples, the signal doesn't exist and we stop."
