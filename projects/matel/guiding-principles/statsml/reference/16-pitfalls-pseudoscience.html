Pseudoscience & Broscience in Data Analysis

Practices that LOOK scientific (use numbers, charts, p-values) but are actually cargo-cult reasoning — conclusions without valid methodology. Common in industry, research, and especially in fields where someone "looked successful" and reverse-engineered a story.

The litmus test: (1) Can the claim be disproven? If not → pseudoscience. (2) Is there a control group / counterfactual? If not → anecdote, not evidence. (3) Was the analysis plan defined BEFORE seeing data? If not → p-hacking / narrative fitting. (4) Does the person promoting it benefit from you believing it? Check evidence 10× harder. (5) Would you bet your own money at fair odds? If not → you don't actually believe it, you just like the story.
REASONING

1. Survivorship Storytelling

"I did X and succeeded, therefore X works." Nobody studies the 1000 who did X and failed.
REASONING

2. Correlation Presented as Causation

"People who do X succeed" ≠ "Doing X causes success." Direction is reversed — success causes the practice.
REASONING

3. Cherry-Picking Time Windows

Same data, any story you want. Start date chosen to produce desired conclusion.
REASONING

4. Unfalsifiable Claims

"It works, you just need more time/data." If no evidence could disprove it → faith, not science.
REASONING

5. Argument from Authority

"Big tech does it" is not evidence it works for you. Expert's opinion is a hypothesis to test, not a conclusion.
REASONING

6. N=1 Generalization

One anecdote beats 10,000 data points in a meeting — but shouldn't. Vivid stories override statistics.
REASONING

7. Overfitting a Narrative to Noise

Random data → brain finds pattern → story created. Pattern exists in THIS data, not reality.
METHOD

8. Cargo Cult Statistics

Report p-value without understanding. R² without validation. The FORM of science without the FUNCTION.
METHOD

9. Broscience / Influencer Pseudoscience

Looks + confidence + platform = "expert." Algorithms reward confidence over accuracy.
METHOD

10. Tech Industry Broscience

Microservices, ML, test coverage — adopted as gospel via conference talks, never controlled experiments.
METHOD

11. Texas Sharpshooter Fallacy

Target drawn around bullet holes after shooting. Hypothesis generated BY the data, tested ON the same data.
METHOD

12. Base Rate Neglect

"99% accurate test!" on a rare event = almost always wrong when it fires. Bayes' theorem is not optional.
METHOD

13. Post Hoc Ergo Propter Hoc

"After X, therefore because of X." 4 things changed. 1 got credit. The most VISIBLE, not the most CAUSAL.
METHOD

14. Regression to the Mean

Extreme values always drift back. Any treatment applied at extremes APPEARS to work. Math did it, not you.
STRUCTURAL

15. Ecological Fallacy

Group average ≠ individual reality. What's true of the GROUP is often false for most INDIVIDUALS in it.
STRUCTURAL

16. Science-Jargon Mysticism

"AI-powered" = if-else. "Proprietary algorithm" = linear regression. Jargon deflects scrutiny.
STRUCTURAL

17. Moving the Goalposts

Redefine success after the fact. Each failure → new definition. Eventually unfalsifiable territory.
STRUCTURAL

18. Publication Bias / File Drawer

Only positive results get published. The literature is a BIASED SAMPLE of all evidence ever generated.
STRUCTURAL

19. Data Deluge / Gish Gallop

Overwhelm with volume so no single claim gets scrutinized. Counter: "Show your SINGLE strongest evidence."
STRUCTURAL

20. Naturalistic Fallacy

"Natural = good." Arsenic is natural. "Traditional = correct." Bloodletting survived 2000 years.
REASONING

21. Invented Probabilities

Assigning probabilistic relationships without proving they exist. Two outcomes ≠ 50-50. Temporal sequence ≠ causal probability.
STRUCTURAL

22. The Secret Sauce Illusion

99% of results from boring fundamentals. The 1% "proprietary" part gets all credit because it's sellable.
STRUCTURAL

23. Unquantified Problem Solving

Build without mapping the problem space. Works on today's distribution. Breaks when customer mix shifts.
