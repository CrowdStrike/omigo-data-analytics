Lottery Paradox: Every Ticket Will Lose, Yet One of Them Wins

A belief can be safe on its own and still belong to a set that is certainly wrong

A Million Tickets, A Million Safe Bets, One Certain Winner

core ideaone drawall at once
  • The setup — a fair draw sells a million tickets and exactly one of them wins.
  • Any single ticket — saying "this one loses" is right 999,999 times out of a million.
  • Name your bar — whatever level of certainty you demand, that claim clears it.
  • So accept them all — the same rule licenses the same claim about every ticket.
  • Now the trouble — believing all million at once says nobody wins, which is false.
  • Nothing was sloppy — no single step was careless, yet the set is still wrong.
  • The lesson about rules — "accept anything past a high bar" fails claim by claim.
  • What survives — the odds on each ticket, not the pile of accepted claims.

Believe the first 500,000 "this ticket loses" claims and you are right about all of them barely half the time; take all 1,000,000 and you are right zero percent of the time.

Key point: High confidence in each piece says nothing about confidence in the whole set.

Two Hundred Reliable Steps Make One Unreliable Pipeline

data workpipelinesdashboards
  • Same arithmetic — each step is a claim that it worked, and you need all of them.
  • A long pipeline — 200 steps working 999 times in 1,000 finish clean 82% of runs.
  • Read that again — a run in five breaks somewhere, with no weak step to blame.
  • A dashboard — 50 tiles each right 99 times in 100 are all right about 61% of days.
  • What gets reported — the per-step number, because it flatters and is easy to measure.
  • What is never reported — the chance the whole board is clean, which decisions use.
  • Length is the driver — adding steps lowers the clean-run chance with nothing degrading.
  • The fix is arithmetic — a 95% promise over 200 steps needs about 99.97% per step.

A 200-step pipeline at 99.9% per step succeeds end-to-end 81.9% of the time; a 50-tile dashboard at 99.0% per tile is fully correct 60.5% of the time.

Key point: Per-item reliability is not system reliability, and only the second one ships.

One Hundred Published Findings, Thirty-Six That Held Up

documented caserepeat the studyhonest caveat
  • The project — a large team repeated 100 published psychology studies with bigger samples.
  • Each original — had cleared the usual bar for being publishable on its own terms.
  • The repeat run — about 36 of the 100 came back in the same direction and size range.
  • Read as a set — a shelf of individually accepted findings did not hold together.
  • Why it fits here — the accept-each-one rule was followed and the shelf still failed.
  • Honest caveat — smaller true effects, method drift and what gets published all matter.
  • Not the whole story — the all-at-once problem is one contributor, not the explanation.
  • The usable part — never quote a per-study confidence as confidence in a literature.

Of 100 repeated studies, about 36 reproduced — a shelf where every single item had individually passed the bar for publication.

Key point: A body of work is only as sound as the joint claim, never as the best single paper.

Open Science Collaboration (2015), Estimating the reproducibility of psychological science, Science 349(6251).

When One Claim At A Time Keeps A High Bar Honest

boundaryset sizepractice
  • The trap needs two things — many claims, and one decision that needs all of them.
  • Acting on one claim — if each decision stands alone, a high bar is the right rule.
  • Independent bets — 50 unrelated experiments are fine if no call needs all 50 right.
  • Where it returns — when a report, model or launch rests on the whole set being true.
  • Two dials, not one — how sure you are per claim, and how many claims you stack up.
  • State the joint number — quote the confidence for the exact set you will act on.
  • Raise the bar as the set grows — ten claims and two hundred cannot share a threshold.
  • Keep the odds — carry probabilities forward instead of flipping claims to flatly true.

At 95% per claim the set is more likely wrong than right by the 14th claim; at 99.9% per claim that point does not arrive until roughly the 693rd.

Key point: A high bar is safe for one claim at a time and needs raising for every claim you add.
