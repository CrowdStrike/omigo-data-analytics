Base Rate Fallacy

Ignoring how rare something is makes accurate tests look useless.

Your fraud model is 99% accurate. A transaction gets flagged. Sounds like it's almost certainly fraud, right?

Not even close. Only 0.1% of transactions are fraudulent. Out of 100,000 transactions: 100 are actual fraud (model catches 99), but 99,900 legit ones produce 999 false alarms. Your ops team gets 1,098 alerts — and 91% are fake.

The model is excellent. The problem is that fraud is so rare that even a tiny false-positive rate floods you with noise.

Lesson: Always ask "how common is this thing?" before trusting a detection rate. Accuracy means nothing without prevalence.
