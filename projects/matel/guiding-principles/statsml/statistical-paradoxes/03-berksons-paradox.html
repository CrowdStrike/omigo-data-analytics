Berkson's Paradox

Filtering your data can create correlations that don't exist.

You analyze your engineering hires and notice something odd: people with strong coding skills tend to have weaker communication skills, and vice versa. You conclude "these traits are negatively correlated" and design interviews accordingly.

But in the full applicant pool, coding and communication are completely independent. The illusion appears because you only hired people who were strong in at least one. If someone is weak at both, they didn't get in. That selection filter forces an apparent trade-off that isn't real.

Lesson: When you filter data to a subset (hired, churned, hospitalized), check whether your filter could create fake patterns between unrelated variables.
