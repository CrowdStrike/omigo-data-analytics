Lindley's Paradox

With enough data, meaningless differences become "statistically significant."

You have 10 million daily active users. You A/B test a button color change. The result: p<0.0001, a "highly significant" difference in click rate. Ship it?

The actual lift is 0.002% — about $4 in extra revenue per month. The sample was so large that even a rounding error becomes "significant." Statistical significance told you the effect is real. It didn't tell you it matters.

Lesson: Always pair p-values with effect size. Ask "significant AND big enough to care about?"
