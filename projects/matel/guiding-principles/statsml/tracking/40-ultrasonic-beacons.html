Ultrasonic Beacons

Inaudible sounds hidden in TV ads and store speakers let companies link your TV watching to your phone — without you hearing or knowing anything.

What is it?

Ultrasonic beacons are high-frequency sounds (above 18kHz — too high for humans to hear) embedded in TV commercials, store audio systems, or even websites. Your phone's microphone picks them up through apps that have mic permission. This links what you're watching on TV to your phone identity, or confirms you're physically in a specific store. You can't hear it, see it, or block it easily.
What does it collect?
  • Which TV commercials you were exposed to
  • Which stores you physically entered
  • How long you stayed in range of the beacon
  • Your phone's identity (device ID, advertising ID)
  • Whether you're watching live TV or streaming
  • Which other people's phones are near yours (co-location)
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: Nothing — this happens entirely without disclosure.

What they don't tell you: Advertisers want to know if their TV ad made you visit the store. Ultrasonic beacons are the bridge between TV viewing and phone tracking. They can also confirm two people are in the same room by detecting the same beacon on both phones. It's invisible surveillance through sound.
How much can they see?

They can confirm your physical presence near any beacon source — a specific TV during a specific ad, a specific store aisle, a specific event. Combined with other data, this is the missing link that connects your TV behavior to your phone identity to your shopping habits. All through a sound you never knew was there.
