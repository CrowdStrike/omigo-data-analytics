Clipboard Snooping

Apps silently read whatever you last copied — passwords, addresses, private messages — without asking permission.

What is it?

When you copy text on your phone (a password, an address, a message), it sits on the clipboard. Many apps secretly read this clipboard the moment you open them. TikTok was caught doing this in 2020 when iOS 14 started showing a warning banner. Before that warning existed, nobody knew it was happening.
What does it collect?
  • Passwords you copied from password managers
  • Addresses and phone numbers
  • Credit card numbers copied from emails
  • Private message snippets
  • URLs and search queries
  • Any text you copy-pasted between apps
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: "To provide a better paste experience" or nothing at all — most never admitted it.

What they don't tell you: Apps use clipboard to fingerprint you across devices. If you copy a URL on your laptop and it syncs to your phone clipboard, the app now connects your laptop browsing to your phone identity. Some apps harvest passwords and financial data directly.
How much can they see?

Whatever you last copied. One snapshot at a time, but continuously checked every time you open the app. Over days and weeks, this builds a collection of your most sensitive copy-paste moments — the things important enough to copy are usually the things most worth stealing.
