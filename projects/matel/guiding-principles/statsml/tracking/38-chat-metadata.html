Chat & Messaging Metadata

Even with end-to-end encryption, the platform knows who you talk to, when, how often, and from where — content is hidden but patterns aren't.

What is it?

WhatsApp, Signal, iMessage — they encrypt your message content so nobody can read it. But the metadata (who, when, how often, how long, group membership) is NOT encrypted. The platform sees your entire social graph: who you talk to at 2am, how often you message your ex, which group chats you're in, and your response speed. Patterns reveal as much as content.
What does it collect?
  • Who you message (phone numbers, contacts)
  • Exact time of every message sent and received
  • How quickly you reply (response latency)
  • Message frequency between any two people
  • Group membership and who talks to whom within groups
  • Online/offline status and "last seen" timestamps
  • Which device you're using and your IP address
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: "We need metadata to deliver messages — we can't read the content because it's encrypted."

What they don't tell you: Metadata reveals relationships, schedules, and social hierarchies without reading a single word. The NSA director said "We kill people based on metadata." WhatsApp shares metadata with Facebook for ad targeting. Your "private" encrypted chat still tells them everything about your social life — just not the exact words.
How much can they see?

They see your complete social graph — every relationship, its intensity, its timing. They know you message your doctor, then your spouse, then searched "treatment options." They know you're in a "Job Search" group chat. They know your boss messages you at 11pm. They know you stopped messaging someone suddenly. Content is hidden; life patterns are completely exposed.
