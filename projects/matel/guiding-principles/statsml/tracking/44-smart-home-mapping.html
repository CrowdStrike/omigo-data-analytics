Smart Home Mapping

Your robot vacuum maps your floor plan. Your thermostat knows when you're home. Door sensors log every entry and exit. Light patterns reveal your daily routine.

What is it?

Smart home devices — robot vacuums, thermostats, door sensors, smart lights — collect detailed information about your home layout, your presence, and your habits. They build a picture of your life inside your own walls. This data goes to company servers.

Your robot vacuum has a more accurate floor plan of your home than you do. It knows every room's exact dimensions, furniture placement, and obstacle locations.
What does it collect?
  • Complete floor plan with room sizes and furniture positions (robot vacuum LIDAR)
  • Home/away patterns throughout the day (thermostat motion sensors)
  • Every door open and close with exact timestamps (door sensors)
  • Light on/off patterns showing wake and sleep times
  • Which rooms you use most and when
  • Temperature preferences per room and time of day
  • How many people live in the home (motion patterns)
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: To clean more efficiently, save energy, automate your lights, and make your home more comfortable.

What they don't tell you: Your floor plan reveals your income (home size). Your occupancy patterns tell insurers your risk profile. iRobot considered selling floor plan data to advertisers. Amazon then bought iRobot entirely. A large home with many rooms means different ads than a studio apartment.

Home size is one of the strongest predictors of household income. Your vacuum hands this data over for free.
How much can they see?

They know the size of your home and how many rooms it has. They know when you wake up, when you leave, when you return, and when you go to sleep. They know which rooms you spend time in and whether anyone else is home.

Combined, these devices create a complete behavioral profile of life inside your home. Every routine, every absence, every deviation from normal.
