Commercial Satellite Imagery

Companies photograph Earth constantly. Hedge funds count cars in parking lots before earnings reports. They track construction, shipping, and crop health from space.

What is it?

Private companies run hundreds of satellites that photograph every spot on Earth repeatedly. Some take new photos daily. Images are sold to investors, governments, and corporations. Resolution is now 30cm per pixel. They can see individual cars, shipping containers, and construction equipment.

Planet Labs alone operates over 200 satellites. They image the entire Earth's landmass every single day.
What does it collect?
  • Parking lot fullness at retail stores (proxy for sales performance)
  • Construction progress at factories and data centers
  • Ship movements and port congestion
  • Crop health and harvest timing across farmland
  • Oil storage tank levels (measured by shadow inside the tank)
  • Military base and airfield activity
  • Mining output and stockpile volumes
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: Environmental monitoring, urban planning, disaster response, climate research, and agricultural optimization.

What they don't tell you: Hedge funds pay millions for satellite photos of Walmart and Target parking lots days before quarterly earnings. They count cars to predict revenue before it's public. This gives them insider-like trading advantages without technically breaking the law.

One satellite analytics firm charges $1M+ per year for "alternative data" feeds used exclusively by quantitative trading firms.
How much can they see?

Every outdoor space on Earth. No consent needed. It's from space — no trespassing law applies. At 30cm resolution, they distinguish individual cars, trucks, and large equipment. They can track how busy a store is over weeks, whether a factory is expanding, how many ships wait at a port, and whether crops are thriving or dying.

They cannot read license plates or identify faces. But they don't need to. Patterns over time tell the story.
