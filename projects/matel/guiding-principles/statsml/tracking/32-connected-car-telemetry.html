Connected Cars

Your car reports where you go, how you drive, and who's inside — then sells that data to insurance companies.

What is it?

Modern cars from Tesla, GM, Ford, and others have built-in internet and dozens of sensors. They constantly upload data about your driving — speed, braking, routes, even cabin cameras. This data gets shared with insurance companies, data brokers, and law enforcement.
What does it collect?
  • Every route you drive (GPS breadcrumbs)
  • Speed at every moment
  • Hard braking and acceleration events
  • How often you drive and at what hours
  • Cabin camera footage (Tesla, Rivian)
  • Phone connections via Bluetooth
  • Voice commands and in-car conversations
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: "To improve safety features and provide navigation."

What they don't tell you: GM's OnStar was caught selling driving data to insurance companies through LexisNexis. Your "safe driver discount" app reports every detail. Tesla uses cabin cameras for AI training. Your car is a surveillance device you paid $50,000 for.
How much can they see?

They know where you go, when you left, how fast you drove, who was in the car, and what you said to the voice assistant. The car builds a complete life pattern — work schedule, gym visits, late-night trips. Insurance adjusts rates in real time based on your actual driving.
