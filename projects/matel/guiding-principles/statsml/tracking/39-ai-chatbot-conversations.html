AI Chatbot Conversations

Everything you tell ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini is stored — people share things more intimate than therapy, and it's kept for training.

What is it?

AI chatbots like ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini store your conversations by default. People share health fears, relationship problems, work frustrations, creative ideas, and legal questions. It's more intimate than a diary because you're asking for advice. Unless you specifically opt out, these conversations are used to train future AI models.
What does it collect?
  • Every question and follow-up you ask
  • Personal problems you describe (health, money, relationships)
  • Work documents and code you paste in
  • Creative ideas and business plans
  • Your writing style and vocabulary
  • How you think through problems (reasoning patterns)
  • Time of day you use it and how long sessions last
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: "To improve our AI models and provide better responses."

What they don't tell you: Your conversations train the next version of the AI. Your phrasing, your problems, your ideas become training data. Employees can review conversations for safety. Some platforms share data with third parties. You're pouring out your deepest thoughts into a system that treats your vulnerability as a product improvement resource.
How much can they see?

Everything you type. People treat chatbots like therapists, lawyers, and confidants combined — sharing things they'd never post online. The AI knows your health anxieties, relationship dynamics, career fears, financial situation, and creative ambitions. It's the most complete psychological profile ever voluntarily created, and most people don't realize it's being stored and studied.
