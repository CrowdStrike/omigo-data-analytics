Palantir-Style Data Fusion

They combine government records, social media, phone data, bank transactions, and camera footage into one profile per person. No single source is dangerous alone — the combination is totalizing.

What is it?

Companies like Palantir build software that merges data from dozens of separate sources into one unified view of a person. Alone, each database is limited. Combined, they create a complete picture — where you go, who you know, what you buy, what you believe.

Think of it as a search engine for people. Type a name, and every fragment of data ever collected about that person appears on one screen — cross-referenced, timestamped, and connected to everyone they know.
What does it collect?
  • Government records — licenses, taxes, benefits, voter registration
  • Social media posts, likes, and friend connections
  • Phone location history (every place you've been)
  • Bank and credit card transactions
  • CCTV and surveillance camera footage
  • Flight records and travel history
  • License plate scans from road cameras
  • Medical records and pharmacy purchases
  • Internet browsing history
  • Email and messaging metadata
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: "To fight terrorism, solve crimes, and keep people safe. We help law enforcement connect the dots faster."

What they don't tell you: The same system is used for immigration enforcement, predictive policing (flagging people before they commit crimes), tracking protesters, and monitoring entire communities. Once built, the tool can be pointed at anyone — journalists, activists, political opponents. The definition of "threat" is decided by whoever holds the controls.

The danger is not any single database. It is the fusion. Each source alone looks harmless. Together, they eliminate privacy entirely.
How much can they see?

Everything. That is the entire point. If one source misses you, another catches you. They know where you were, who you were with, what you bought, what you searched, and what you said — all cross-referenced with timestamps.

There are no gaps. Your phone tracks location. Your card tracks spending. Cameras track movement. Social media tracks relationships. Together, they reconstruct your life down to the minute.

You cannot opt out of most of these sources. You did not agree to license plate readers. You did not consent to CCTV. The fusion happens without your knowledge or permission.
