QR Code Tracking

Every QR code scan links your phone identity to a specific place and time — a habit normalized during COVID that never went away.

What is it?

QR codes exploded during COVID — restaurant menus, check-ins, payments, event tickets. Each scan sends your phone's identity (IP, device info, sometimes location) to the code creator. What seemed like a temporary health measure became permanent tracking infrastructure. That restaurant menu QR isn't just a menu — it's a check-in system.
What does it collect?
  • Your phone's IP address and device type
  • Exact time of scan
  • Physical location (the QR code's placement tells them where you are)
  • How often you visit (repeat scans = regular customer)
  • Which marketing campaigns brought you (unique codes per ad)
  • Your browsing behavior after scanning (what you looked at on the linked page)
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: "Contactless and convenient — no need for paper menus."

What they don't tell you: Each QR code is a unique tracking URL. They know exactly which physical location you visited, when, and how often. Marketing teams use unique QR codes on different ads to measure which billboard or flyer drove you to the store. It's a physical-world cookie.
How much can they see?

They see that your specific phone was at a specific place at a specific time. Combined with cookies from the linked webpage, they can connect your physical visit to your online identity. Repeated scans build a pattern of your routine — which restaurants, how often, what time you eat lunch, whether you came alone or with the same group.
