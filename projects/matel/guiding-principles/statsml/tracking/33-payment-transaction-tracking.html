Payment & Transaction Tracking

Every credit card swipe, Apple Pay tap, and Venmo transfer is recorded — payment processors see more of your life than your spouse.

What is it?

Every time you buy something with a card or digital wallet, the transaction is logged by your bank, the card network (Visa, Mastercard), the payment processor (Stripe, Square), and often the store itself. These records build a complete picture of your daily life — where you eat, what you buy, your vices, your health spending.
What does it collect?
  • Exact amount, merchant name, and category
  • Date, time, and location of purchase
  • How often you buy from the same place
  • Spending patterns (income timing, splurges, financial stress signals)
  • Whether you split bills (Venmo, Zelle shows social connections)
  • Recurring subscriptions and memberships
Why do they collect it?

What they tell you: "Fraud protection and transaction records"

What they don't tell you: Banks sell aggregated spending data. Credit card companies build advertiser profiles. Your "anonymous" purchase data can be re-identified with just 4 transactions. Financial stress patterns get sold to lenders who target you with high-interest offers when you're vulnerable.
How much can they see?

Complete financial X-ray. They know your salary (from deposits), your rent, your grocery habits, your guilty pleasures, whether you're seeing a therapist, if you bought a pregnancy test, and when you're financially struggling. Four credit card purchases are enough to uniquely identify 90% of people.
