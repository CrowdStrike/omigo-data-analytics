Cognitive Biases in Data Analysis

How human psychology leads analysts astray — and how automated pipelines can defend against it.

The meta-principle: Every bias is a shortcut that works most of the time but fails catastrophically in specific situations. Statistical analysis is exactly the domain where these shortcuts fail most — because data is counter-intuitive by nature. The pipeline's job is to be the adult in the room: no excitement, no stories, just numbers and thresholds.
ANALYSIS

1. Confirmation Bias

Failures get debugged line by line while successes get celebrated uncritically. The scrutiny applied depends on whether you liked the answer.
ANALYSIS

2. Anchoring Bias

The first number seen becomes the baseline everything else is judged against. It keeps its grip even when it came from an unrelated context.
ANALYSIS

3. Narrative Fallacy

A causal story built after the fact around what was only noise. It explains everything that happened and predicts nothing that follows.
ANALYSIS

4. Texas Sharpshooter

Drawing the target after the bullets have landed. The few cases that fit get shown and the rest quietly disappear.
ANALYSIS

5. Clustering Illusion

Random events clump — that is what random means. The eye reads the clump as a pattern and demands an explanation.
ANALYSIS

6. Hindsight Bias

Once the outcome is known, memory rewrites what you believed beforehand. "I knew it all along" makes an unpredictable event look forecastable.
ANALYSIS

7. Denominator Neglect

A percentage reads as solid regardless of how few rows produced it. Three out of four and three hundred out of four hundred both print as 75%.
ANALYSIS

8. Availability Bias

The example that comes to mind fastest feels like the most common one. Vividness beats frequency, so the loudest signal displaces the largest.
ANALYSIS

9. Overconfidence in Small Samples

Early results swing widely before the signal has settled. Calling it at that point means acting on variance and defending it later.
ANALYSIS

10. Recency Bias

The latest tick gets treated as the whole story and overwrites the trend behind it. One reading replaces months of evidence.
ANALYSIS

11. Gambler's Fallacy

Independent events carry no memory of what came before. After a long run of one outcome the odds have not shifted an inch.
DEFENSE

12. Deciding in Advance

Write the rule before you know which way it falls. One decision made calmly then replaces twenty made under pressure.
CAUSALITY

13. Correlation vs Causation

The same cloud of points supports at least five other explanations. Reverse causation, a common cause, selection, coincidence — each fits the picture equally well.
SAMPLING

14. Sampling Bias

How the rows were gathered decided who is in them. Collecting sixteen times as many forms the same way returns the same wrong answer with tighter error bars.
MEASUREMENT

15. Recall Bias

When people remember it, the remembering is part of the instrument. A flat year gets recalled as a climb, and the data records the recall.
SYSTEMS

16. Feedback Loops & Structural Biases

Self-fulfilling prophecy, omitted variable bias, and collider bias in one place. Each is a case of the system manufacturing the evidence it is later measured by.
REASONING

17. Reasoning Fallacies

Gambler's fallacy, conjunction fallacy, McNamara fallacy, Goodhart's law, lead time bias. Each one feels intuitively right and is mathematically wrong.
UI & EVAL

18. Display Framing

How a number is drawn decides what it appears to mean. The same twelve months on two y-axis scales produce two opposite verdicts.
EXPOSURE

19. Manufactured Reference Frame

Curated exposure quietly sets what a person believes is normal or expected. Unlike classical anchoring it accumulates over volume instead of landing as one salient number.
EXPOSURE

20. The Fault You Watched Fail Jumps the Queue

A failure you have seen cannot be unseen, so it gets fixed regardless of size. The problems nobody happened to look at keep accumulating unpriced.
HEURISTIC

21. Newer, Bigger, Pricier

The price tag gets read as a rating of the product. But the seller writes the price and can raise it without touching what is in the box.
AI INTERACTION

22. Attachment to AI Personas

Attachment forms toward generated content that imitates human expression and emotion. The simulated warmth re-anchors expectations until real people feel slow, critical, and unrewarding.
AI INTERACTION

23. The Judgment-Free Chatbot

People disclose more to a chatbot than to a person because no reputation is at stake. Each channel therefore measures a different person, not the same one twice.
MEASUREMENT

24. Survey Position Bias

Ask for the best song and the winner depends on where it sat in the list. Voters read from the top, tire, and pick early — nobody lied, the ballot chose.
HEURISTIC

25. Familiarity Feels Like Quality

Mere exposure makes the familiar feel better made than it is. A dogfooding team's read on a redesign measures their own retraining cost, not the design.
HEURISTIC

26. One Bad Thing Outweighs Five Good Ones

A single complaint colours the whole review while twenty quiet successes average into nothing. Leaning toward alarm is right for hazards and wrong for grading someone who is trying.
MEASUREMENT

27. Nobody Counts What Never Breaks

What always works stops being noticed, so the work keeping it working lands in no number. A visible outage with a clean recovery earns more credit than never having failed.
SAMPLING

28. The People Who Can't Complain

Ask for feedback and you hear from whoever can afford to give it. Take the name off the form and the complaints appear — that gap is the measurement, not noise.
MEASUREMENT

29. A Bad Week Rewrites the Year

Ask about the past year right after something goes wrong and the year comes back heavier. Each new bad event drags the old ones up, so complaints cluster into a fake worsening trend.
