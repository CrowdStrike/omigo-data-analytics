# Clustering Illusion: Chance Arrives in Lumps

Scatter a hundred dots at random and some corner of the page ends up crowded. Nothing crowded it.

## 1. Random Dots Land in Clumps

Tags: core idea | lumps and voids | nothing caused it

- **The knot that draws the eye** — a tight group of dots in one patch, and a bare stretch nearby
- **What put them there** — nothing; each dot was placed without reference to any other
- **Divide the page** — twenty-five equal squares, four dots apiece if the hundred spread out evenly
- **The fullest square** — holds ten, two and a half times its four-dot share, reads as a cause
- **The bare square** — one of the twenty-five drew no dots at all, and nobody ever asks why
- **Evenly spread is the odd one** — four in every square needs a force, and would be news

**Key point:** Clumps and voids are not departures from randomness — they are what randomness produces. A lump means something only once it beats the lumps chance already delivers.

## 2. Six Shots in a Row

Tags: streaks | hot hand | already expected

- **What the room decides** — six baskets running, and the player is officially on fire
- **The stand-in** — fifty tosses of a fair coin, where nothing can possibly be on fire
- **What came out** — 29 heads in the fifty, and the longest head-run in that order was six
- **How ordinary** — 31% of fifty-toss stretches contain a head-run of six somewhere in them
- **Runs of four** — those show up in 83% of fifty-toss stretches, near enough to guaranteed
- **The question people ask** — "what are the odds of six in a row", answered by one in 64
- **The question that settles it** — how often fifty tries hold any six-run: one in three

**Key point:** A streak is evidence of a hot hand only if it beats what a fair coin already does over the same number of tries. Six in a row does not — a fair fifty contains one 31% of the time.

*Illustrative Example — one seeded toss sequence; every printed chance is exact, computed at render time.*

## 3. A Shuffle That Feels Broken Is Working

Tags: where it bites | shuffle | complaints

- **The complaint** — listeners insist shuffle keeps serving up the same singer twice over
- **The playlist** — thirty songs, six singers, five each, ordered by a genuinely fair draw
- **What a fair draw does** — it averages 4.0 places where two songs by one singer sit side by side
- **One such order** — a fair draw of the thirty put four same-singer pairs back to back
- **The order people expect** — zero back-to-back repeats, which only 2% of fair draws deliver
- **So the request** — "make shuffle random" is really "make shuffle stop looking random"
- **What music apps ship** — a draw that deliberately spaces each singer out, and is not fair
- **Why it survives** — the unfair version is the one that gets described as random

**Key point:** People report a fair process as broken when it produces the lumps fairness requires. A process tuned until it feels random has stopped being random.

*Illustrative Example — figures computed from seeded shuffles of a constructed playlist.*

## 4. How Big Must a Lump Be Before It Counts

Tags: the boundary | what clears the bar | common mistake

- **The setting** — forty break-ins across a hundred streets in one month, spread by chance alone
- **Per street** — that averages 0.4 break-ins, and in one such month 66 of the hundred recorded none
- **The worst street** — took three, seven and a half times the 0.4 average, and the residents notice
- **Three is nothing** — chance hands some street three or more in 56% of months, every other month
- **Four starts to count** — chance reaches four in 6% of months, about once in sixteen
- **Five is worth acting on** — chance gets there in under 1% of months, roughly one in two hundred
- **The mistake** — comparing the worst street to the 0.4 average, not to chance's worst
- **The fix** — ask what the worst street would look like in a month where nothing was wrong

**Key point:** The comparison that decides it is your lump against the biggest lump a fair process throws up — never your lump against the average. Below that line the lump explains itself.

*Illustrative Example — the reference spread comes from 4,000 seeded months over the same hundred streets.*
