# Mood-Congruent Recall: When You Ask Decides What Happened

Ask someone about the past year a few days after something went wrong and you get a heavy year. Ask the same person in a calm stretch and the same year comes back light.

**The one construction behind every figure on this page.** One fixed list of **twelve bad weeks** in a 52-week year — weeks **4, 9, 13, 18, 22, 26, 30, 35, 39, 43, 47 and 51** — which every chart here reads. Each memory starts at a **strength between 0.70 and 1.00** on its own week. Between reminders it fades by **exp(−weeks ÷ 10)**, a half-life of **6.9 weeks**. A reminder at week *t* lifts it back by **0.40 × exp(−(t − its own week) ÷ 26)**, never above its starting strength — so a reminder restores a recent memory more than an old one. A memory is **nameable** when its strength is **0.32 or more**. The two askings are week **51.5**, days after the twelfth event, and week **58**, seven quiet weeks later. Both read the same list under the same rule; only the week of the question differs.

Every quantity the bullets quote, and how it follows from that list and that rule:

| Quantity | Value | How it checks out |
|---|---|---|
| Bad weeks on the list | 12 | weeks 4 through 51; none is added or removed anywhere |
| Quiet weeks between the two askings | 6.5 | week 58 − week 51.5 |
| What those quiet weeks do to a memory | × 0.522 | exp(−6.5 ÷ 10), the same factor for all twelve |
| Nameable at the first asking | 11 of 12 | eleven strengths reach 0.32; the oldest is 0.27 |
| Bar the first asking must clear to survive | 0.613 | 0.32 ÷ 0.522 — the fade is uniform, so the line moves |
| Nameable at the calm asking | 6 of 12 | 11 − 5, the five sitting between 0.32 and 0.613 |
| Reachable at the calm asking, no reminders | 1 of 12 | only week 51's memory is still 0.32 or more |
| Reachable with each new event reminding | 6 of 12 | the same rule as the calm asking, same answer |
| Reachable with going over it as well | 11 of 12 | reminders at weeks 51, 53, 55 and 57 hold the pile up |
| Peak while the events kept coming | 11 of 12 | reached at week 47, the eleventh bad week |
| Total starting strength of the pile | 9.99 | the twelve starting strengths added up |
| Standing verdict at the calm asking | 81% | 1 − exp(−9.99 ÷ 6) = 0.811 |
| Doubt left in that verdict | 18.9% | 100% − 81.1% |
| Verdict at each of the next four years | 85%, 89%, 91%, 93% | the 18.9% of doubt shrinks by exp(−0.25) a year |
| Sting left at the calm asking | 63% | each start faded by exp(−its age ÷ 60), over 9.99 |
| Sting at each of the next four years | 26%, 11%, 5%, 2% | each year multiplies what is left by exp(−52 ÷ 60) |
| Nameable across the five askings | 6, 4, 1, 0, 0 | each memory on its own clock of 25 to 500 weeks |

Two things here cannot be redone with a pen. The **twelve starting strengths** come from a fixed seeded stream, so the 11-and-6 split, the 0.613 bar, the 81% verdict and the 63% sting are read off that draw rather than from round numbers. The **long fade clocks** behind 6, 4, 1, 0, 0 come from a second seeded stream. Everything else above closes by arithmetic once those two draws are fixed.

## 1. One Year, Asked Twice

Tags: core idea | same year | different answer

- **The year** — twelve things went wrong, on twelve dated weeks, and that list never changes
- **The first asking** — a few days after the twelfth one, while it is still fresh in mind
- **The second asking** — seven quiet weeks later, same person, same question about the same year
- **Asked while it stings** — 11 of the twelve come back, near enough the whole year
- **Asked in the calm** — 6 come back, and the other six cannot be brought to mind at all
- **What moved between the two** — only the date of the question; not one event was added or removed
- **Why the second answer is shorter** — nothing recent has pulled the older ones back up
- **What this breaks** — an answer collected right after trouble reads worse than the year was

**Key point:** The same twelve months, from the same person, return 11 items or 6 depending on the week you ask. If the timing of the question moves the answer that far, the answer is partly a reading of the question's timing.

*Illustrative Example — one fixed list of twelve dated events shared by both askings; both counts are tallied from the plotted bars.*

## 2. Each New One Drags the Old Ones Up

Tags: the mechanism | no new input needed | arrives in clusters

- **The common belief** — bad memories are built to last, and good ones are the ones that slip away
- **What actually happens** — a new bad event acts as a reminder and pulls the similar old ones up
- **Being pulled up strengthens them** — each time one is brought to mind it returns easier next time
- **With no reminders at all** — plain forgetting applies and only 1 of the twelve is still reachable
- **With each new event reminding** — 6 are reachable, from exactly the same twelve weeks
- **Going over it in your head** — does the same lifting, so the pile holds at 11 with no new events
- **Why that matters** — a grievance list can keep itself alive on nothing but attention
- **A side effect** — each new event drags old ones forward, so complaints arrive in bursts
- **What the bursts look like** — a worsening trend, while the rate of things going wrong has not

**Key point:** The pile is not held up by the badness of the memories. It is held up by how often something pulls them back into mind — a new event, or just going over the old ones. Remove the reminders and the ordinary rate of forgetting takes them.

*Illustrative Example — the same twelve dated events under three reminder rules; every plotted count is tallied at that week from the same shared list.*

## 3. The List Goes, the Verdict Stays

Tags: long run | the sting fades | the record does not

- **Follow the same person on** — no new incidents, just the ordinary passage of four more years
- **Naming the actual weeks** — 6 can be named at the calm asking, 4 a year on, 1 after two years
- **After three years** — none of the twelve can be placed or dated, and the list is simply gone
- **What stays behind** — the one-line conclusion, which holds at four fifths and firms up
- **Why that is worse than a list** — a conclusion nobody can trace cannot be checked or argued down
- **What the other person faces** — a verdict with no instances attached, so nothing to rebut
- **The sting itself** — fades from two thirds to near nothing, faster than the record does
- **So the folk claim is wrong** — what lasts is the record and the verdict, not the hurt
- **The one exception** — for a truly traumatic event this fading fails, and that is its own topic
- **Vividness is not accuracy** — a memory recalled sharply and confidently can still be wrong

**Key point:** Over years the incidents stop being nameable while the conclusion drawn from them stays put and firms up. The feeling fades fastest of the three — which is why "they are inconsiderate" can long outlive both the twelve events and any sting attached to them.

*Illustrative Example — the same twelve events carried forward four years; nameable counts, verdict strength and remaining sting are all computed at render time. The faster fade of the sting than of the record is the direction reported in the fading-affect literature (Walker & Skowronski); the vividness-without-accuracy result is Talarico & Rubin (2003).*
