Tracking Data Collection Methods

Everyday devices produce data as a by-product of working. What each mechanism measures, and what it does not resolve.

Why this matters: Most of these records were created to make something work — route a request, bill a call, time a traffic signal. They get reused later as measurements of behaviour, and that reuse is where the statistical problems start: the unit of observation is usually a device or an account rather than a person, values are often model output rather than measurement, and coverage is rarely uniform across the places and people being compared.

Everyday Phone and Desktop Use

Mechanisms that run while someone uses an ordinary phone or browser.

WEB

1. Server-Side Logging

Every request a web server answers is written to a log — source IP, path, timestamp, user agent. This is a byproduct of how HTTP works.

Location and Movement

Positioning, ranging, and proximity — and why coverage is never uniform across places.

LOCATION

32. Indoor Positioning

Indoors a phone asks a service where it is instead of working it out alone, so the method that covers where people actually spend time is the one that leaves a record.

PHYSICAL

33. Store WiFi & Bluetooth Beacons

Sensors in malls and stores detect nearby phone radios to measure foot traffic, dwell time, and aisle-level paths.

34. WiFi Probe Requests

Phones periodically broadcast probe frames naming known networks. Any receiver in range can log those broadcasts.

HIGH-RESOLUTION

35. WiFi Signals as Radar

Router radio waves reflect off people and objects. Analyzing the reflections detects motion, counts occupants, and in lab work tracks breathing.

36. WiFi Location Databases

Google and Apple hold large maps of router MAC addresses to coordinates, so a phone can fix its position by scanning nearby networks.

PHYSICAL

37. Bluetooth Scanning

Phones, earbuds, and watches advertise BLE packets continuously. Venues run scanners to measure presence and movement.

physical retail broadcast
CROWD-SOURCED

38. Bluetooth Item Trackers

The tag has no GPS. Passing phones hear its broadcast and supply their own location, so coverage depends on how many people are nearby.

QR menus, payments, and check-ins each encode a unique target URL, so a scan links a device to a specific place and time.

Traffic Monitoring

Roadside cameras that record passing vehicles as a by-product of managing traffic.

Cameras at intersections record continuous video to manage signal timing. Every vehicle and pedestrian in frame is part of that recording.
