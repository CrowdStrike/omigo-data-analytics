Oura & Whoop BACKLOG

Two recovery-oriented wearable APIs whose headline outputs — readiness, recovery, strain — are vendor model outputs rather than measurements.

Last verified: August 2026

Overview

What It Provides

Oura — API v2, cloud REST over a finger-worn ring. Sensors are finger PPG plus skin temperature. Base path https://api.ouraring.com/v2/usercollection/…. Collections split into daily records keyed to a day, and detailed sleep-period records keyed to the period itself.

  • Daily composites: daily_sleep, daily_readiness, daily_activity, daily_spo2, daily_stress
  • Detailed sleep periods with a coarse hypnogram plus HR and HRV interval arrays
  • heartrate timeseries, workout, session, tag
  • personal_info, ring_configuration

Whoop — cloud REST over a wrist strap, wrist PPG only. The organising unit is the physiological cycle, roughly wake-to-wake and variable in length, not the calendar day.

  • Cycles — accumulated strain, average and max HR, kilojoules
  • Recovery — per cycle: recovery score percentage, resting heart rate, HRV as RMSSD in milliseconds, and on supported hardware SpO2 and skin temperature
  • Sleep — stage summaries (light, slow-wave, REM, awake), sleep performance / consistency / efficiency percentages, disturbance count, sleep-need decomposition
  • Workouts — strain, average and max HR, kilojoules, HR-zone durations
  • Profile and body measurement
Composite scores are proprietary model outputs, not measurements. This is the single most important fact on this page. Oura Readiness / Sleep / Activity Scores and Whoop Recovery and Strain are computed by undisclosed vendor algorithms that have been revised across firmware and app releases; both vendors have shipped score-model changes that moved users' numbers with no physiological change behind them. Three consequences follow. (1) Non-stationarity is injected by software releases — a long score series contains level shifts that are release artefacts, so any trend, changepoint or seasonality claim is confounded with the vendor's deployment calendar. (2) A score change is not attributable to the subject, which breaks the causal reading that every dashboard invites. (3) Any regression or A/B analysis using a score as the outcome needs the firmware and app version as a covariate, or a control period spanning the same releases — and is better off using the underlying components (HRV RMSSD, resting HR, stage durations, respiratory rate) instead. Contributor breakdowns are no escape hatch: they are themselves derived and bounded 0–100, so ceiling effects compress variance precisely at the healthy end of the population where most of your subjects sit.
Strain and Recovery are bounded, non-linear, vendor-defined constructs. Whoop Strain sits on a proprietary bounded scale of roughly 0–21; Recovery is a 0–100% output. Neither is on a physical unit scale, so a difference of "3 strain" is not a fixed quantity of anything and is not comparable across the range. Ceiling and floor effects are real, not hypothetical — a genuinely harder session near the top of the scale registers a smaller increment than the same increase in the middle. Treating either as interval-scaled in a linear model asserts something the construct does not support.
Authentication & Scopes

Oura — OAuth 2.0 for multi-user apps, plus Personal Access Tokens for single-user and self-access work, which makes n=1 and small-cohort research trivial to start.

personal daily heartrate workout session tag spo2

Whoop — OAuth 2.0 with verb-namespaced scopes and offline access for refresh tokens.

read:recovery read:cycles read:sleep read:workout read:profile read:body_measurement offline
Granularity
  • Oura sleep period: bedtime_start, bedtime_end, total_sleep_duration, time_in_bed, latency, efficiency, per-stage durations (deep / light / REM / awake), average and lowest heart rate, average HRV, respiratory rate, plus sleep_phase_5_min — a compact hypnogram encoded as one digit character per 5-minute bin. heart_rate and hrv appear as interval arrays within the period.
  • The 5-minute bin matters. Oura's stage series has 5-minute resolution; Fitbit's has 30-second segments. These are not the same object and cannot be compared bin-for-bin. Any stage transition shorter than five minutes is unrepresentable in the Oura encoding — so brief-awakening counts, sleep-onset latency at fine resolution and fragmentation indices are not comparable between the two, and pooling them measures the bin size as much as the sleep.
  • Oura heartrate returns timestamped bpm values with a source field (awake / rest / sleep / workout). Sampling is not uniform — the ring measures opportunistically, duty-cycled, so density varies by state and by motion. Treating this as a regularly sampled series is wrong: any window mean is weighted by however many samples the ring happened to take, and source is a confounder for any comparison of levels because the states with the most samples are also the states with the lowest heart rates.
  • Whoop exposes stage summaries and per-cycle scalars rather than an epoch-level hypnogram, plus HR-zone durations for workouts.
Sampling interval and derivation are not comparable across vendors. A chest strap producing per-beat R-R intervals, an optical wrist PPG that is periodic, duty-cycled and motion-sensitive, a finger PPG on a ring, and a spot reading are four different measurements sharing the label "heart rate". Sleep staging from actigraphy plus PPG is an inference, not polysomnography: agreement with PSG for 4-class staging is moderate at best and is worst exactly where these devices are used most — discriminating light from deep. Pooling sleep-stage minutes across Oura, Whoop and Fitbit without adjusting for sensor site, sampling regime and classifier is a methodological error, not a nitpick; the between-device variance can exceed the effect you are looking for.
Key Endpoints

Oura — under /v2/usercollection/: daily_sleep, daily_readiness, daily_activity, daily_spo2, daily_stress, sleep, heartrate, workout, session, tag, personal_info, ring_configuration. Query with start_date/end_date, or start_datetime/end_datetime for heartrate. Cursor pagination via next_token. Webhook subscriptions exist for data-created and data-updated events.

Whoop — cycle collection and cycle-by-id, recovery collection and recovery for a given cycle, sleep collection and sleep-by-id, workout collection and workout-by-id, user profile, body measurement, webhook registration. Cursor pagination via next_token.

Whoop has published successive API versions, and endpoint paths and field names have changed across them. Pin a version explicitly in anything you build and treat an unpinned client as a scheduled outage. This page deliberately does not assert a current version string.

Rate Limits
  • Both vendors enforce per-token and per-application request limits, return HTTP 429 on exhaustion, and expect exponential backoff. No figures are quoted here on purpose: published quotas have changed across versions and differ by partner tier.
  • Both paginate with cursor tokens, so a full-history backfill is a token walk, not a single range call — cost scales with page count, and a naive backfill hits the limit long before a rate-aware one does.
  • Webhooks (both vendors) and Oura date-bounded queries reduce quota pressure versus polling. As with Fitbit, a notification is a change signal; you still pay quota to fetch the record.
Business Scenarios
  • Athlete monitoring and load management — Whoop's cycle-level strain against recovery is the native fit, and the cycle boundary is a feature here rather than a nuisance.
  • Sleep and recovery coaching — Oura's contributor breakdown at least gives something to explain a score with, which a bare composite does not.
  • Research cohorts — both allow server-side pull with stored tokens, so no participant-facing app is needed. Oura Personal Access Tokens make small self-tracking studies easy to start and impossible to scale.
  • Employer and insurer wellness — technically feasible, but a vendor-computed composite as a contractual metric means the vendor can change what you are paying for without telling you.
  • Symptom and illness-onset tracking — Oura temperature deviation and tags are the useful primitives; treat them as signals, not diagnoses.

Across all of these, prefer the closest-to-raw field the API exposes — stage durations, resting HR, RMSSD, respiratory rate — over the composite. Components are far more stable across vendor model releases, and they carry units.

Key Restrictions & Gotchas
Whoop cycle alignment is a silent join bug. A cycle boundary is defined by sleep and wake, not by calendar midnight, and cycles vary in length. Joining Whoop cycle data to calendar-day data from any other source misaligns records without raising an error, and the misalignment can shift an effect by up to a full day — enough to invert a lead-lag conclusion. One late night makes a cycle span two dates; one very early wake leaves a date with two cycles or none. Oura's daily_* records are likewise attached to a day by Oura's own day-boundary convention, not yours. Join on intervals, keep the cycle identifier with its start and end timestamps, and state explicitly how a straddling cycle is attributed.
Records are mutable — vendors recompute history. Backfill and recompute mean a record you fetched yesterday can differ today, with no version field to tell you. Store the fetch timestamp alongside every record, keep prior versions rather than upserting in place, and never assume a historical series is frozen. An analysis rerun on "the same data" can produce different numbers for this reason alone.
  • Never provided: raw PPG waveform, raw accelerometer samples, the full beat-to-beat R-R series (Oura exposes interval-averaged HR and HRV arrays, Whoop a summary RMSSD scalar — neither is raw R-R), the score formulas themselves, or other users' data.
  • Gating: Whoop developer access requires registering an app on their developer platform and passing review; access has historically been limited and partner-oriented, so a research plan that depends on it should confirm access before design, not after. Oura is comparatively open — Personal Access Tokens for self-access — but commercial and multi-user use goes through app registration.
  • Non-wear produces absence, not nulls. A night without the device yields no sleep record at all, so a naive night count silently drops it. Non-wear correlates with illness, travel, disrupted routine and heavy training — exactly the conditions of interest — so complete-case averages are biased upward. Carry a wear indicator as a covariate and report wear-time next to every average.
  • Device-dependent fields. SpO2 and skin temperature depend on hardware generation, so any cohort mixes device capabilities and a missing field can mean "older device" rather than "not measured that night".
  • Timezone offsets are carried per record in Oura timestamps. Normalising to UTC and then bucketing by day silently reassigns nights across the date boundary for travellers.
  • Version transitions have been breaking. Oura's v1 to v2 migration changed both paths and payload shapes; Whoop has shipped multiple API versions. Pin, and expect the next one.
