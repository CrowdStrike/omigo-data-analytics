Personal Location History BACKLOG

Negative finding: neither major platform exposes an individual's historical location trace to third parties. Google moved Timeline on-device; Apple never offered it.

Last verified: August 2026

Overview

This page is a negative finding. There is no API — on either platform — that returns a user's historical location trace to a third party. Read the rest as documentation of why and of what the remaining substitutes actually are, not as an integration guide.

What It Provides

Nothing queryable. The two paths that people expect to exist do not:

  • Google Location History / Timeline — moved to on-device storage. The historical timeline is now held on the user's phone rather than as a server-side record that Google serves back. There is no Timeline read API, and the former server-side Timeline is no longer the system of record. Users who did not migrate lost history in the transition, which by itself puts a hole in any archive
  • Apple Significant Locations — computed and stored on device, end-to-end encrypted, surfaced only in Settings. No API, no export, no third-party read. This was never available; there is no deprecation to work around

Authentication & Scopes

There is no OAuth scope to request on either platform for historical location. This is the cleanest way to confirm the finding: the permission does not exist, so no amount of consent obtains the data.

no timeline read scope no significant-locations entitlement

What does exist is live-position permission — CoreLocation on iOS, fused/platform location on Android — granted to your app, while your app runs. That yields current and recent position for sessions you observed. It is a fundamentally different object from a historical trace: it starts when your app is installed and permitted, covers only your app's runtime, and cannot be backfilled.

The One Semi-Programmatic Route: Google Takeout

User-initiated export via Google Takeout is the only way a location history reaches an analyst's hands. Broadly two layers appear:

  • Raw records (Records.json historically) — a long list of timestamped lat/lng points with an accuracy estimate and, for some points, an activity type or source indicator. High volume, irregular sampling
  • Semantic segments (semanticSegments / semanticLocationHistory) — Google's already-inferred interpretation: place visits matched to place IDs and activity segments with a travel mode, each carrying a confidence or probability
This is an export, not an API. It is manual, per-user, consent-driven, and produced on Google's schedule. You cannot poll it, cannot schedule it for a cohort, and cannot guarantee two participants' exports were produced by the same pipeline version. The file layout and field names have shifted with the on-device migration, so a parser written against an older export is not safe to assume still applies.

Granularity

  • Raw point — timestamp, lat/lng, accuracy radius. Sampling rate is opportunistic and depends on device state, battery mode and OS version, so gaps are not missing-at-random
  • Inferred visit — a place match with a confidence value; the boundary times are themselves model output
  • Inferred activity segment — travel mode with probabilities across candidate modes
  • No cohort-level or cross-user unit exists at all

Consequence: Individual Longitudinal Mobility Is Out of Reach

This is the centrepiece. Both major platforms shifted personal location history to on-device storage. Programmatic individual mobility data — where a given person was, day over day, for months — is now largely unavailable to third parties. What remains is aggregate or place-level: busyness aggregates inside first-party UIs, published aggregated mobility datasets, transit feeds, and whatever a study collects itself.

Anyone designing a mobility study therefore has exactly three honest options, and should pick one explicitly rather than discover the constraint mid-project:

  • Consented device-side collection — ship an app, obtain background-location permission, collect prospectively. Highest fidelity, but the panel begins at recruitment: no history, real attrition, and permission state is itself a variable that changes mid-study
  • Participant Takeout donation — retrospective coverage, but small n, self-selected donors, heterogeneous schemas, and inference layers you did not control
  • Aggregate proxies — place-level or area-level series. No individual trajectories, so no within-person effects, no sequences, no dwell-time distributions per person

These are not interchangeable. A question phrased at the individual level cannot be answered with aggregate proxies without an ecological-inference assumption that usually goes unstated.

Key Restrictions & Gotchas

Methodological note — inferred labels are not ground truth. Semantic segments contain model output: activity type, place match, and confidence. Treating an inferred visit as an observation imports the inference error of a model you cannot inspect, cannot version, and cannot evaluate. There is no held-out set and no published error rate. Downstream standard errors computed as if visits were measured will be too small, because they ignore the label's own uncertainty.
The same raw points can be relabelled. Two exports taken at different times may assign different visits or travel modes to identical underlying coordinates, because Google's inference model changed in between. Any behavioural "change" detected across export vintages is then indistinguishable from a model revision. If you must compare waves, hold the export vintage fixed and re-derive labels yourself from raw points, or restrict the analysis to raw geometry.
  • Accuracy radius varies by fix source (GNSS vs. Wi-Fi vs. cell). Mixing them without weighting treats a 5 m fix and a 500 m fix as the same evidence
  • Sampling gaps correlate with battery saver, airplane mode, indoor time and OS version — that is, with behaviour. Imputing across gaps assumes exactly the thing that fails
  • Coordinates plus timestamps are directly re-identifying even after name removal; a handful of visits usually pins a home and a workplace. Consent and storage controls are a design requirement, not a compliance afterthought
  • CoreLocation background modes are policy-reviewed and user-revocable at any time. A revoked permission looks in the data like a person who stopped moving
  • Do not read the migration as a change in human mobility. The break in any time series that spans it is an availability artifact: the measurement instrument was withdrawn, not the phenomenon
