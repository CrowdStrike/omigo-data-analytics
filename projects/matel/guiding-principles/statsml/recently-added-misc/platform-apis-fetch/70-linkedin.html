LinkedIn BACKLOG

Sign-in, own-asset publishing and ad reporting. No people search, no connection graph, no third-party profile lookup — the professional graph is not an API product.

Last verified: August 2026

Overview

What It Provides

LinkedIn's API surface is narrow and almost entirely about assets you already own: your app's signed-in member, your organization's pages, your ad accounts. The platform data — members, titles, employers, the network — is not exposed.

  • Sign In with LinkedIn using OpenID Connect — replaced the older profile-permission flow. A minimal profile: subject identifier, name, profile picture, locale, verified email. That is close to the whole of it.
  • Share on LinkedIn / community management — post content as a member (w_member_social) or as an organization (w_organization_social), and retrieve and manage your own organization's posts, comments and reactions.
  • Organization pages — follower statistics and page/visitor statistics for pages you administer: impressions, clicks, engagement, follower counts, with aggregate demographic breakdowns by seniority, function, industry, company size and geography. Aggregate only, bucketed, never a follower list.
  • Marketing / Advertising API — campaign management and reporting for ad accounts you have access to, analytics with pivots and metrics, Lead Gen form responses, and Conversions/CAPI for offline conversion upload and audience matching. This is the largest and best-supported surface by a wide margin.
  • Learning, Talent Solutions (recruiter / ATS, job posting) and Sales Navigator integrations — partner-only. Access requires approval into a specific programme, not merely registering an app. Do not plan on these without a signed partnership.

Authentication & Scopes

OAuth 2.0: three-legged for member context, two-legged / client-credentials for some application-level calls. Products are requested and approved per app, and many require verification of the associated company page before they can be enabled at all.

openid profile email w_member_social w_organization_social

The sign-in surface is served by three OIDC scopes. There is no scope that widens a member's profile into their CV, and none that opens a search or graph endpoint.

Granularity

  • Member: one thin identity record for the authenticated member, and nothing about any other member.
  • Organization: aggregate, bucketed statistics — counts per seniority band, per function, per industry, per company-size band, per region. Never a follower list, never an identified visitor.
  • Ads: metrics by campaign / creative and by date, with pivot dimensions. The most genuinely analytical granularity on the platform, and it is about your own spend.

The Negative Findings

These absences are the substance of this page.
  • No people search API. There is no endpoint to search members by title, company, skill or location. The older people-search capability was withdrawn.
  • No connection graph. You cannot enumerate a member's connections, their first- or second-degree network, or mutual connections. Not for the authenticated member, not for anyone.
  • No third-party profile lookup. You cannot fetch an arbitrary member's profile by identifier or vanity URL.
  • Not the full CV even for your own signed-in member. No position history, no skills, no education, no endorsements, no connection count.
  • Scraping is explicitly against the User Agreement and LinkedIn actively enforces against it, technically and legally. Whatever the state of the case law on scraping public pages, building a pipeline on it is a business risk, not a technical decision.
Consequence: any product premised on "we will get candidate, company or professional-graph data from the LinkedIn API" is not buildable as designed. The professional-graph datasets sold in the market do not come from a sanctioned public API. This is one of the most commonly, and most expensively, misunderstood limitations in the whole platform landscape — it is usually discovered after the roadmap has been committed.

Rate Limits & Pricing

  • Per-application and per-member daily throttles, applied per product, visible in the developer portal.
  • Exhaustion returns HTTP 429. Counters reset on a daily boundary rather than a rolling window, so a burst early in the day can strand the remainder of it.
  • Because limits are per-member as well as per-application, a large connected-user base does not proportionally increase what any single member's data can be polled for.
  • The APIs are not sold per call. The gate is product approval, not price.

Business Scenarios

  • Social sign-on with a verified email address — the strongest reason most apps integrate at all.
  • Employer-brand and organic content reporting for pages you administer.
  • Paid campaign measurement, offline conversion upload and audience matching.
  • ATS and job-posting integrations, for approved partners only.

Key Restrictions & Gotchas

  • Demographic breakdowns are bucketed and suppressed at low counts, so small pages get sparse or missing segments — an absent segment in the response is not a zero.
  • Organization statistics are only for pages you administer; losing admin rights silently ends the data series.
  • Partner-programme access can be revoked, and product availability differs by app, so two teams reading "the LinkedIn API" may have materially different surfaces.
  • The OIDC email is verified, which makes it usable as an identity key — but it is the only durable joinable field the sign-in flow hands you.
  • Versioned monthly. The modern endpoints require a LinkedIn-Version: YYYYMM header and older versions are sunset on a schedule, so an integration needs periodic revalidation even when you change nothing. Legacy endpoints — r_liteprofile, r_emailaddress, the old /v2/me profile call, and the earlier UGC Post and Share APIs — have already been superseded, so tutorials more than a year or two old describe calls that no longer work.
  • Engagement metric definitions are not stable. LinkedIn has revised how impressions, unique impressions and engagement rate are computed, and has changed which demographic facets are exposed. A multi-year engagement trend spanning a definition change is not a trend: the same field name denotes a different quantity on each side of the break. Store the API version and the exact metric name alongside every value, or you cannot later distinguish a behaviour change from a definition change.
  • Statistics arrive late and settle. Recent-window figures are provisional and revise upward over subsequent days, so a job that snapshots yesterday and never revisits bakes in a systematic undercount at the recent end of every series.
  • The visible corpus is survivorship-biased. Comments removed by a page admin or by LinkedIn, posts deleted by their authors, and content from accounts later restricted simply do not appear in what the API returns. Any prevalence or sentiment figure computed from it is measured after moderation has pruned the sample, and therefore understates whatever was pruned — a shortfall that is not random, because removal correlates with exactly the spam, abuse and crisis content such analysis is usually about.
Methodological note — the break is in the access policy, not in the labour market. LinkedIn's API contracted sharply: the withdrawal of profile and search access reshaped what third parties could measure. Any historical dataset containing rich profile or search-derived fields either predates the current API or came from a non-sanctioned source, and its provenance must be established before it is used for anything. A panel spanning that change has a break in field coverage that is an artifact of access policy — treat a pre/post comparison across it as unidentified, not as evidence of a change in hiring, titles or mobility.
