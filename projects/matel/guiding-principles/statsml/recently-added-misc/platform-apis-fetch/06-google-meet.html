Google Meet BACKLOG

Meet REST API v2 — conference records, participants and their sessions, recording and transcript artifacts, and transcript entries.

Last verified: August 2026

Overview

The Google Meet REST API v2 has two halves. spaces is forward-looking: create a meeting space, read or configure it, end an active conference. conferenceRecords is backward-looking: after a conference finishes, read who was present and what artifacts were produced. The data model is deliberately three levels deep — conference, participant, participant session — and most integration errors come from collapsing those three levels into one.

What It Provides
  • Spaces — name, meetingUri, meetingCode, and config (accessType, entryPointAccess). Also activeConference when one is in progress.
  • Conference records — one per actual conference held in a space, with startTime, endTime, expireTime and a back-reference to the space.
  • Participants — one per distinct attendee of that conference, with earliestStartTime and latestEndTime, and an identity union of signedinUser, anonymousUser or phoneUser.
  • Participant sessions — one per continuous connection by that participant, each with its own startTime and endTime.
  • Recordings — artifact objects with a driveDestination reference and state.
  • Transcripts and transcript entries — a transcript artifact plus per-utterance entries carrying participant, text, languageCode, startTime, endTime.
A participant is not a session. Every participant has one or more participantSessions, and a reconnect — network drop, browser refresh, switching from laptop to phone — creates an additional session. The participant's own earliestStartTime/latestEndTime spans the whole range including the gaps when they were absent, so it overstates presence. Summing session durations understates nothing but can double-count when a person is connected twice at once (two devices). Real attended time requires listing sessions, merging overlapping intervals, and summing the union. Neither of the two obvious shortcuts is correct.
Authentication & Scopes
meetings.space.createdmeetings.space.readonlymeetings.space.settingsdrive.readonly
  • OAuth 2.0 only. Principal scopes: https://www.googleapis.com/auth/meetings.space.created (act on spaces the app itself created), meetings.space.readonly, and meetings.space.settings.
  • meetings.space.created is the narrowest and most commonly misunderstood: it grants access to conference records for spaces created by your application, not to every conference in the organisation. Collecting org-wide Meet data is not simply a matter of adding this scope.
  • Domain-wide delegation with a service account, authorised by a Workspace super admin, is the route to impersonating users for broader collection. Recording and transcript artifacts additionally require Drive scopes to fetch the file itself.
  • These are sensitive/restricted scopes — a published app faces Google's verification process, and admin consent is required in the Workspace admin console.
Granularity

Timestamps are RFC 3339 with sub-second precision. Resource names are hierarchical paths, so a transcript entry's name embeds its conference record and transcript: conferenceRecords/{record}/transcripts/{transcript}/entries/{entry}. Transcript entries are the finest grain available and are utterance-level, not word-level — there are no per-word timings and no confidence scores.

Key Endpoints
  • POST /v2/spaces, GET /v2/spaces/{space}, PATCH /v2/spaces/{space}, POST /v2/spaces/{space}:endActiveConference.
  • GET /v2/conferenceRecords (filterable on space.name and start_time), GET /v2/conferenceRecords/{record}.
  • GET /v2/conferenceRecords/{record}/participants, GET .../participants/{participant}.
  • GET /v2/conferenceRecords/{record}/participants/{participant}/participantSessions.
  • GET /v2/conferenceRecords/{record}/recordings, GET .../recordings/{recording}.
  • GET /v2/conferenceRecords/{record}/transcripts, GET .../transcripts/{transcript}/entries.
  • Google Workspace Events API — subscriptions on a Meet space delivering events such as conference started/ended, participant joined/left, and artifact generated, via Pub/Sub.
Rate Limits
  • Standard Google API quota model: per-project and per-user quotas measured in requests per minute, enforced by the Cloud console for the Meet API service, returning 429 RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED.
  • Quotas are visible and adjustable in the Google Cloud console for your project rather than being a fixed published constant, so read them there instead of assuming a number. Back off exponentially on 429 and 503.
  • The listing pattern is inherently fan-out heavy: conferences, then participants per conference, then sessions per participant, then transcript entries per transcript. A month of a large tenant is a large number of calls, so budget for it and cache aggressively.
Business Scenarios
  • True attendance — merged-interval presence per participant, for training completion or client-billable time.
  • Connection-stability signal — session count per participant is a usable proxy for network trouble even though the API exposes no packet-level quality metrics.
  • Meeting content mining — transcript entries as the text corpus for topic extraction, action-item detection, or talk-time distribution by speaker.
  • Talk-time equity — seconds of speech per participant from entry startTime/endTime, a common inclusion metric.
  • External-participation measurement — how often conferences include participants outside the directory.
Key Restrictions & Gotchas
  • Conference records expire. The documented retention for a conferenceRecord is 30 days, surfaced as expireTime on the resource. After that the record and its participant and session data are no longer retrievable. Any longer history must be extracted and stored within that window — it cannot be recovered later.
  • Artifacts live in Drive, not in this API. A recording resource returns a driveDestination with a file id and export URI; the API does not stream media bytes. The file's lifetime is governed by Drive and Vault retention, which is independent of the 30-day conference-record window — so the video can outlive the metadata that explains who was in it.
  • Recordings and transcripts only exist if they were generated live. They must have been enabled and started during the conference. There is no endpoint to record or transcribe after the fact, and eligibility depends on the Workspace edition — several editions do not include recording or transcription at all.
  • Anonymous and phone participants are not resolvable. anonymousUser gives only a display name; phoneUser gives only a partially masked number. Neither maps to a directory identity, so external attendance cannot be reliably deduplicated across meetings and a "who attended" report will contain unjoinable rows. This is a hard limit, not a permissions issue.
  • No real-time stream in the REST API. Polling conferenceRecords tells you about conferences that have ended. Live signals require the Google Workspace Events API with Pub/Sub subscriptions, which is a separate integration with its own subscription lifetime and renewal requirement.
  • No media-quality telemetry. Unlike some competitors, this API exposes no bitrate, jitter, packet loss, resolution or CPU figures. Quality investigation has to use the Admin SDK Reports Meet audit activity, which is a different API with a different retention window.
  • No chat, reactions, polls, Q&A, breakout rooms or hand-raises. In-meeting interaction data is largely absent from v2. Chat is not exposed as structured data.
  • Transcript language is per entry. languageCode can vary between entries in one transcript; assuming a single language per meeting will misclassify multilingual calls. Transcription accuracy is not reported, so entry text carries no confidence signal.
  • Speaker attribution can be missing. An entry's participant may reference a participant that cannot be resolved to an identity for the same anonymous/phone reasons above.
  • A space is reusable; a conference record is not. One space accumulates many conference records over time. Keying analytics on meetingCode or space name silently merges separate meetings into one.
