Meta Quest BACKLOG

Two disjoint surfaces: a server-backed Platform layer for accounts, commerce and social that is queryable — and an on-device sensing layer (scene mesh, hands, eyes, face) that is not.

Last verified: August 2026

Overview

What It Provides

The Quest developer surface splits cleanly in two, and confusing the halves is the single most common planning error. One half is a service backed by Meta's servers; the other half is a sensor stream that exists only inside the running app process.

  • Platform layer (server-backed). Identity, entitlement, achievements, leaderboards, purchases, cloud saves, matchmaking and presence. Round-trips to Meta. Queryable from the app, and to a degree from the developer's own backend.
  • Sensing layer (on-device). Scene model, hand skeletons, body pose, passthrough, eye and face signals. Delivered into the app by the runtime, frame by frame. Never stored by Meta on the developer's behalf.

Platform Layer — Capabilities

  • Entitlement check — confirm the signed-in account is licensed for this app. Required for store distribution; the standard anti-piracy gate and the first call most titles make.
  • Achievements — simple (boolean), count (progress toward a target) and bitfield (partial completion across slots) variants.
  • Leaderboards — write an entry for the current user, read back paged ranges (top-N, around-me), with optional extra payload per entry.
  • Logged-in user — user ID and profile basics. Friend / user-list access is scope-gated and has been progressively narrowed over successive platform revisions; do not assume a readable friend graph.
  • In-app purchases — add-ons, DLC and subscriptions, with server-verifiable purchase records.
  • Cloud storage — cloud saves so app state follows the account across devices.
  • Multiplayer plumbing — rooms and matchmaking, voice, group presence, invites and deep-linked join.

Store Analytics for Developers

The developer dashboard reports installs, retention curves, revenue, funnel steps and engagement for your own title. This is aggregate and developer-scoped. It is a LiveOps instrument, not a research dataset: no per-user rows for arbitrary users, no cross-title comparison, no export of another developer's cohort.

Sensing Layer — On Device Only

  • Scene API / Scene Model — room mesh plus scene anchors carrying semantic labels (wall, floor, ceiling, desk, couch, screen, door, window). Built from a user-run Space Setup capture, so its existence and quality are a user action, not a guarantee.
  • Hand tracking — per-hand joint skeleton with poses plus pinch and confidence signals.
  • Eye and face tracking — available on supported headsets (Quest Pro among them) and exposed to the app as abstracted signals intended to drive avatar expression and social presence. The shape of the API is "move this face rig", not "here is an attention log". Explicit user permission is required.
  • Passthrough, depth, body / upper-body tracking, controller and hand pose — all per-frame runtime state.
None of the sensing layer is retrievable server-side. Meta does not host an endpoint that returns a user's room mesh, hand-joint history, or gaze. The data is handed to the app process and nowhere else. If the app does not persist and upload it — with consent, and within the developer data-use rules — it does not exist anywhere you can query.

XR Sensor Data Is OS-Mediated By Design

The platform's contract is to give the app the outcome of an interaction and withhold the underlying perceptual trace. Eye and face signals are the clearest case: they are shaped for rendering a face rather than for measuring one, because a raw fixation stream is a direct readout of attention, hesitation and interest in things the user never selected. Scene meshes and hand joints follow the same pattern for the same reason — they are an interaction substrate, not telemetry. This is not an unfinished feature. Anyone designing gaze-based or egocentric analytics needs to know it before the study is designed, not after instrumentation begins.

Data-Use Policy

Meta's developer terms constrain what may be done with sensitive on-device signals. Eye and face tracking data in particular carries use restrictions and cannot be freely repurposed — advertising and profiling uses are out of bounds. Treat this as a hard design constraint alongside the technical one: even where the app can compute a signal, the licence to retain and reuse it is separate and narrower. Read the current terms rather than relying on a summary; the specifics have moved.

Rate Limits & Pricing

Platform SDK calls are asynchronous and throttled in practice rather than published as a headline quota — treat leaderboard and cloud-save writes as batchable and avoid per-frame calls. Store distribution carries the usual platform revenue share on IAP and app sales. Sensing has no rate limit in the API sense; it is bounded by frame budget and by thermal and battery headroom on the headset.

Business Scenarios

  • Entitlement gating as anti-piracy and as a licence check for enterprise deployments
  • Progression, achievements and leaderboards as retention mechanics
  • Social features: presence, invites, matchmaking, in-app voice
  • Room-aware mixed-reality apps that place content against real furniture using scene labels
  • LiveOps tuning driven by your own title's aggregate store analytics

Key Restrictions & Gotchas

  • The app must pass store review before Platform features are usable in production
  • Friend-graph access has been narrowed; a social feature that assumed a readable friend list may not survive a platform revision
  • No cross-app data — you see your own title's users, not their behaviour elsewhere
  • No cross-user sensing dataset exists at any tier of access
  • Sensing fidelity varies by headset model, so a "hand tracking" or "eye tracking" dataset collected in the wild is really a mixture over device generations
  • Scene model depends on a completed Space Setup; missing or stale rooms are a normal state, not an error case
Methodological note: the device-generation mixture is the trap. Pooling sensing records across headsets pools different sensor suites, different tracking algorithms and different permission prompts. Any apparent behavioural shift may be a change in the device mix or in your own app build. Stamp every sensing record with device model and app version, and check the mixture before interpreting a trend.

foreach (var anchor in sceneAnchors) {

    var labels = anchor.GetSemanticLabels();
    var bounds = anchor.GetBounds();
    PlaceContent(labels, bounds);
}
