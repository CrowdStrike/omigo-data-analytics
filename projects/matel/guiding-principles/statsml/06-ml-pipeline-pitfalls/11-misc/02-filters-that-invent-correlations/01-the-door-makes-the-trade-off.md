# The Door Makes the Trade-Off

**Page type:** detail page (three card-sections, each a two-column layout table: text left 50%, canvas right 50%)
**HTML title tag:** The Door Makes the Trade-Off — Filters That Invent Correlations

**Subtitle:** Intuition first: when a group is admitted through a door, two unrelated abilities inside that group can look like rivals — no one changed, one corner of the picture is simply missing.

## Two Skills That Only Look Like Rivals

**Tags:** `the mechanism` (red), `intuition` (blue)

- **Two independent skills** — before the door, skill A and skill B are unrelated; the dots fill a square blob
- **The entry rule** — "get in if you are good at A or good at B" admits everyone except the weak-at-both
- **One corner deleted** — the low-A, low-B corner is empty by construction, not by any real trade-off
- **Inside the survivors** — a weak A must have carried a strong B, or the door would have refused entry
- **The illusion** — the remaining dots slope downhill, so A and B look like rivals competing for room
- **Nobody changed** — every individual's true A and B are untouched; only the picture lost a corner
- **The collider** — the entry decision is caused by both features, so filtering on it links them
- **Seeded figure** — all 500 points: r = +0.038; the 257 admitted: r = -0.501; 243 rejected; 257 + 243 = 500

*Example:* Two skills drawn independently 500 times give r = +0.038 overall, but the 257 who clear the bar A + B > 0.95 give r = -0.501 — a trade-off nobody has.

**Key idea:** The correlation is created by the act of looking only at survivors — the entry decision sits downstream of both skills, so keeping only the admitted rows glues the two skills together.

## Why Pro Basketball Rosters Look Like a Trade-Off

**Tags:** `real anchor` (orange), `qualitative only` (blue)

- **In the general public** — height and an outside shooting touch are separate traits with no real link
- **On a professional roster** — the tallest players tend to be the weaker outside shooters, and vice versa
- **The door** — a roster spot demands being exceptional at something, not merely average at everything
- **Empty corner** — a short player without an elite shot never arrives, so that quadrant stays unfilled
- **Three cells left** — tall/poor-shooting, short/great-shooting and tall/great-shooting all survive the door
- **Downhill by omission** — those three cells alone trace a falling line with no underlying trade-off
- **A second, real cause** — tall players are trained as interior players and take far fewer outside shots
- **Honest verdict** — the roster pattern is part selection filter, part position assignment, not one cause

*Example:* A 5'10" player without an elite outside shot never reaches a roster; a 7'0" player never needs one — so the short-and-cannot-shoot cell is empty by construction. (Qualitative pattern only; no correlation or player statistic is quoted, because none is sourced here.)

**Key idea:** Selection explains part of the pattern and role specialisation explains part of it — claiming selection alone is the same overreach as claiming the trade-off is real.

## How to Tell a Real Relationship From a Manufactured One

**Tags:** `the test` (green), `diagnostic` (blue)

- **Ask the entry question** — what had to be true for a row to exist in this table at all?
- **The dangerous filter** — a gate that both features push on is the one that manufactures a link
- **The safe filter** — a gate on a column unrelated to both features leaves their relationship intact
- **Wrong population** — the correlation you want lives before the door, almost never after it
- **Unseen rejects** — if you cannot see the rejected rows, this table cannot measure the true relationship
- **General form** — conditioning on a downstream consequence of two causes is the trap in one sentence
- **Classic tell** — a surprising negative link inside a selected, elite or qualified group is suspicious
- **Move the bar** — a wider door shrinks the fake effect, a stricter door grows it; that is a live test

*Example:* On the same seeded 500 points, the baseline is r = +0.038 at n = 500; a bar of A + B > 0.60 gives r = -0.285 at n = 401; > 0.95 gives r = -0.501 at n = 257; > 1.30 gives r = -0.541 at n = 113.

**Key idea:** If the effect moves when you move the entry bar and vanishes when you remove it, the effect belongs to the door — not to the two features.
