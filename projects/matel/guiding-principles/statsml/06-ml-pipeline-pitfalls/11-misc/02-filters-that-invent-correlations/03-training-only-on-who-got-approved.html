Training Only on Who Got Approved

Labels only exist for the applicants some earlier gate let through, so the model learns the shape of that gate instead of the shape of the world.

You Only Ever See Who Got In

label availabilitythe setup
  • No loan, no label — a rejected applicant never borrowed, so no outcome exists
  • Training set is the approved set — only admitted rows carry a target at all
  • Lender A's rule — approve on a decent credit score OR a strong income
  • A compensatory gate — a weak score passes when income is strong, and the reverse
  • The deleted corner — low-score plus low-income applicants are absent by construction
  • Inside the approved pool — a low score implies the income that bought approval
  • The invented trade-off — score and income look like substitutes among the approved
  • In the applicant pool — the two are independent and show no relationship at all
  • Seeded figure — all 400 applicants r = +0.027; the 175 approved r = −0.540; 225 rejected

Credit score and income drawn independently 400 times give r = +0.027, but the 175 who clear score + income > 1.05 give r = −0.540 — a substitution no applicant's finances contain. (Illustrative Example.)

Key idea: Approval is caused by both features, so keeping only approved rows conditions on a collider — the labelled sample is not a sample of applicants, it is a sample of the gate's output.

The Model Then Learns the Wrong Trade-Off

consequencequantified
  • The truth model — default probability = 0.30 − 0.25 × score, income entirely absent
  • What the model infers — income gains protective power it does not have in truth
  • Low score, high income — 392 rows, 77 defaults, a 19.6% rate, the worst cell
  • High score, low income — 388 rows, 37 defaults, a 9.5% rate, the safest cell
  • High score, high income — 970 rows, 127 defaults, a 13.1% rate, the bulk of the data
  • Why the two differ — low income forces a higher score to clear the bar, so it is safer
  • Low score, low income — 0 rows, so nothing teaches the model that corner's real risk
  • Arithmetic closes — 392 + 388 + 970 + 0 = 1,750 and 77 + 37 + 127 + 0 = 241
  • Correlation among approved — score and income measure r = −0.513 despite independence

Lender A's approved book shows income associated with lower default, so a model trained on it discounts a low-score, high-income applicant — yet that cell defaults at 19.6% against 9.5% for high-score, low-income, the exact opposite of what income appears to buy. (Illustrative Example.)

Key idea: The gate did not merely shrink the sample, it deleted a whole cell — and a cell with zero rows cannot be learned, only guessed at, no matter how much data the other three cells hold.

What to Do When the Gate Owns Your Labels

remedieshonest limits
  • Log every rejected application — store its features though no outcome will arrive
  • Version the gate itself — a moved threshold changes which corner that vintage lacks
  • Reject inference — estimates missing outcomes, on assumptions you cannot test
  • Random-approval holdout — approve a small random slice regardless of score or income
  • Why it is the only cure — it alone creates the missing labels, at a budgeted cost
  • Propensity weighting — weight approved rows by inverse approval probability
  • Its limit too — the weight is undefined where approval probability is exactly zero
  • Scope the claim — the model is valid for applicants resembling those already approved

Lender A approves 2% of applications at random for one quarter; those rows are the only ones that can measure default risk in the low-score, low-income corner, because no amount of modelling recovers a label that was never generated.

Key idea: Reject inference is an assumption, not a measurement — only a random-approval slice generates real labels outside the gate, and it is the one remedy that costs money instead of credibility.
