Feedback Loops & Structural Biases

1. Self-Fulfilling Prophecy

A model's prediction causes the very outcome it predicted, then that outcome reinforces the model in retraining.

Model predicts churn → team deprioritizes customer → customer churns → model "validated."
Credit score low → denied credit → can't build history → score stays low.

The feedback loop makes it impossible to observe the counterfactual: what would have happened without the prediction?

2. Omitted Variable Bias

When a confounding variable is left out of the model, the estimated effect of included variables absorbs the confounder's influence.

"Education → Income" effect is overstated when "Parental Wealth" is omitted. Parental wealth drives both education access and income independently.

The observed coefficient conflates the direct effect with the confounded path, producing a biased and inconsistent estimate.

3. Collider Bias

Conditioning on a common effect (collider) of two independent causes induces a spurious association between those causes.

Talent and looks are independent in the population. But among famous people (fame requires talent OR looks), they appear negatively correlated: famous people who lack talent tend to be good-looking, and vice versa.

The bias arises from selection: restricting analysis to a subset defined by the collider opens a non-causal path.

4. Position Bias → CTR Feedback Loop

Items shown in top positions get more clicks simply because they're visible. The system interprets clicks as relevance, boosts rank, which generates more clicks. A self-fulfilling prophecy specific to ranking systems.

Search result at position 1 gets 30% CTR regardless of quality. Position 5 gets 4%. System trains on this → position 1 item "wins" every retraining cycle. New items never get enough impressions to compete (cold start starvation).
YouTube autoplay: video plays next → counts as engagement → model boosts it → autoplays more. The user never chose it; the system chose for them and then credited their "engagement."

Unlike credit scores, this loop operates at massive scale and compounds hourly. Mitigation requires explicit exploration (epsilon-greedy, Thompson sampling) or position-debiased CTR models that discount top-slot clicks.
