short-video platform - Platform-Specific Data Pitfalls

1. Extreme Cold-Start on For You Page

Every new video is immediately shown to ~300 random users as the initial test cohort. The 5-second watch rate from this tiny sample determines whether the video enters broader distribution tiers (1K, 10K, 100K+ impressions).

A single noisy signal from 300 people gates all downstream distribution. If the initial cohort happens to be disinterested (wrong time zone, wrong demographic slice), a potentially viral video dies immediately.

Data Problem: The cold-start funnel creates massive survivorship bias. You only observe engagement data for videos that passed the initial gate. The counterfactual (what would have happened with a different initial cohort) is never observed.

2. Watch-Time ≠ Enjoyment

"Car crash videos" achieve 100% completion rate but 0% satisfaction. Users watch the entire video out of morbid curiosity or inability to look away, not because they enjoyed it or want more similar content.

Completion rate is a terrible proxy for content quality. Optimizing for watch-time produces an increasingly toxic feed that users complete but regret watching.

Data Problem: The reward signal (completion) is misaligned with the objective (user satisfaction). Models trained on completion rate will systematically promote addictive-but-regrettable content. Post-session surveys show negative correlation with high-completion content.

3. Trend Half-Life: 24-48 Hours

A sound goes viral on Monday, saturates by Tuesday evening, and is dead by Wednesday. The lifecycle of a short-video platform trend is compressed into 24-48 hours, making any model trained on even day-old data potentially obsolete.

By the time a batch-trained model learns that a sound is trending, the trend is already over. Real-time feature freshness is not optional—it is existential.

Data Problem: Traditional train/test splits are meaningless when feature distributions shift every 24 hours. A model trained on Monday's data evaluated on Thursday's data will show catastrophic performance degradation on trend-related features.

4. Sound/Music as Hidden Feature

The same dance performed to different sounds can achieve 100x different reach. Audio is the actual viral vector on short-video platform—not the visual content, not the creator, not the caption. The sound carries the network effect.

Most ML pipelines heavily feature-engineer visual and text signals while treating audio as a categorical ID. This misses the core distribution mechanism of the platform.

Data Problem: If your feature engineering doesn't deeply encode audio/sound properties (tempo, novelty, meme-ability, current trend status), your model is missing the single most predictive feature for virality. Sound_id alone is too sparse; you need sound embeddings + trend velocity.

5. Creator Burnout Cycles

Creators follow a predictable cycle: high output → viral hit → audience expectations rise → pressure increases → burnout → hiatus (2-8 weeks) → comeback attempt. This creates periodic signals that break static engagement models.

A creator's historical average engagement is meaningless if they are in the burnout phase. Models that weight recent creator performance will systematically underpredict comeback virality and overpredict burnout-phase performance.

Data Problem: Creator state is a hidden variable that modulates all observable features. Without modeling the burnout cycle explicitly, you get unexplained variance that looks like noise but is actually a deterministic periodic process.

6. Duet/Stitch Context Dependency

Duet and Stitch videos only make sense when paired with their original source video. A reaction video without the stimulus is literally meaningless—the content IS the juxtaposition.

Analyzing Duet/Stitch responses as standalone content produces garbage features. Sentiment analysis of the response alone will be wrong. Engagement prediction without the original context will be random.

Data Problem: ~15-20% of short-video platform content is contextually dependent on another piece of content. If your data pipeline doesn't join response videos with their originals, you're training on incomplete observations for a significant fraction of the corpus.
