Cancer Screening

Statistical pitfalls in cancer detection and screening programs

Pitfall Description
Lead Time Bias Screening finds cancer 2 years earlier → "5-year survival" improves from 40% to 70%! But death date unchanged; you just started the clock sooner; screening "improved survival" without extending life.
Overdiagnosis Finding cancers that would NEVER have caused symptoms or death; prostate cancer found at autopsy in 50% of 70-year-old men who died of other causes; detecting it didn't help — it led to unnecessary treatment.
Sensitivity vs Specificity at Population Scale Test: 95% sensitivity, 90% specificity; population prevalence 0.5%; positive result = 4.5% chance actually has cancer (PPV); 95.5% of positives are FALSE; at population scale, specificity dominates.
Incidentalomas Full-body CT scan: finds "something" in 40% of healthy people; most are benign but require follow-up; every follow-up: anxiety, cost, radiation from more imaging, possible biopsy complications; finding things isn't always good.
Screening Interval Optimization Annual mammogram: catches cancer 6 months earlier on average vs biennial; but doubles radiation exposure, doubles false positives, doubles anxiety; marginal benefit vs doubled harm; optimal interval = math, not intuition.
Psychological Cost of False Positives Told "you might have cancer" → 3 weeks of anxiety → told "actually no." The 3 weeks of fear are REAL harm from a "correct" system operating at designed specificity; false positives have health consequences even when corrected.
