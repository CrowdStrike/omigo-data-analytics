N=1 Generalization

Flawed Reasoning — Individual anecdote replacing statistical evidence

One Anecdote Extrapolated as Universal Truth
  • "My grandma smoked until 95": Therefore smoking isn't that bad. N=1 lucky survivor vs N=480,000 smoking-related deaths/year in the US. The anecdote doesn't disprove the population statistic — it IS the outlier the statistics predict will occasionally exist.
  • "We tried agile and it failed": Therefore agile doesn't work. N=1 team, with specific dysfunction, at specific company. Maybe agile failed because of the team, not the methodology. But the single bad experience becomes "evidence" against the approach forever.
  • "I know someone who got COVID twice despite vaccination": Therefore vaccines don't work. N=1 breakthrough case vs N=millions with reduced severity/death. The anecdote is memorable; the millions of non-events are invisible.
  • Product feature: "One customer said they hate feature X." Feature X has 95% satisfaction rate. But: the VOCAL unhappy customer's story overrides quantitative data because stories are more compelling than numbers to human brains.

Why it's pseudoscience: Replacing statistical evidence with individual narrative. The human brain treats vivid stories as more TRUE than abstract numbers. One emotional anecdote beats 10,000 data points in a meeting — but shouldn't.
