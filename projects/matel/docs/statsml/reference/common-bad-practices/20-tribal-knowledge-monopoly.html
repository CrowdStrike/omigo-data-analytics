Tribal Knowledge Monopoly

Technical Sabotage — Never document how the pipeline works. Hold all context in your head. You're "irreplaceable" — and everyone else is helpless.

The Practice
  • The training pipeline has 47 undocumented edge cases. Only you know them. Feature X requires a specific join order or it silently duplicates rows. Only you know this.
  • Column `user_status` has 14 values. Only values 2, 5, 11 mean "active" — the rest are historical artifacts. No documentation anywhere. Only you know this.
  • When someone asks, you "help" — verbally, in chat, never in a doc. The knowledge stays in your head. They'll need you again next week.
The ML Model Variant
  • Model training requires hyperparameters tuned over months of experimentation. You never committed the final config — it's in a local notebook.
  • Feature preprocessing has 6 transformations applied in a specific order. Change the order → model accuracy drops 4%. Documented nowhere.
  • Anyone trying to retrain, improve, or debug the model must schedule time with you. You're always "busy this week."
The Data Schema Variant
  • You designed the schema. Field names are cryptic (`evt_tp`, `usr_sg3`, `flg_a`). You know what they all mean.
  • Some fields have overloaded semantics: `amount` means USD in table A, cents in table B, and units-sold in table C. Only you know which.
  • New hires spend weeks reverse-engineering your schema. You answer questions with "oh yeah, that one's tricky" — never with documentation.
The Production Debugging Variant
  • Model performance drops. Logs are sparse (by design). You're the only one who can diagnose production issues because you know the undocumented failure modes.
  • "Let me look at it" — 3 hours later you push a fix. No one else could have done it. You're a hero. The system stays opaque.

Why it persists: Knowledge hoarding gets REWARDED. You're the "go-to person." You get high impact scores. You're "critical to the team." In performance reviews, being the bottleneck reads as being essential. The incentive structure rewards opacity.

The tell: Bus factor = 1 by design, not by accident. If the person took a 2-week vacation and nothing broke, the knowledge wasn't actually hoarded — they just knew a lot. If everything breaks when they're out, the knowledge was weaponized.

The data cost: In ML/data work, tribal knowledge is uniquely dangerous. Wrong preprocessing = months of model training wasted. Wrong join logic = silently corrupted datasets used for business decisions. The blast radius of undocumented data knowledge is measured in months, not hours.
