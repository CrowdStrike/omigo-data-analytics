Credit Arbitrage

Attribution Gaming — Do 10% of the work. Write the summary. Become "the person who did it."

The Practice
  • Project spans 5 teams, 20 people, 6 months. You contribute 10% of the work.
  • But: you write the launch blog post. You present at the all-hands. You prepare the executive summary. You give the demo to leadership.
  • You become the FACE of the project. In organizational memory (which is 90% narrative), you are now "the person who did X."
The Mechanism
  • The person who COMMUNICATES becomes the person who DID.
  • The people who did 90% are invisible because they didn't narrate.
  • Communication IS attribution. Silence IS invisibility.
Variant — Naming Credit
  • Be the one to NAME the project. "Operation Phoenix" was YOUR idea.
  • The name enters org vocabulary — you're associated with it forever. Zero code written, permanent association.
Variant — The Summary Email
  • Send the weekly summary email that goes to leadership. Phrase everything as "we did X" with you as the sender.
  • The FROM field = the credit line.

Why it persists: Leadership forms opinions from presentations, summaries, and write-ups — not git commits. They can't track individual contributions across 20 people. Whoever tells the story OWNS the story in org memory.

The tell: Compare git/Jira/doc history to who presents. If one person always presents and others always build — credit arbitrage is active. Ask builders: "who do VPs think did this?" vs "who actually did this?"
