Prototype-as-Product

Vision Without Evidence — 'Proved it works!' (on 3 examples in a notebook). Ship as v1. Fixing is someone else's problem.

The Practice
  • Jupyter notebook with 3 hand-picked examples. Works beautifully. Call it "v1." Ship it. Real data: crashes on 40% of inputs, has no error handling, can't scale past 100 requests.
  • The credit theft: Whoever inherits the "v1" does the REAL engineering — error handling, scaling, edge cases, monitoring, reliability. They get no credit because the feature was "already launched."
  • Variant — "POC that became production": "It was just supposed to be a proof of concept!" But it got demo'd, leadership liked it, it's in prod now. Built with zero production engineering because it was "just a POC." 2 years later: still running, still breaks.

Why it persists: The person who "launches" gets 90% of career credit. The person who makes it actually WORK (hardening, scaling, fixing) gets 10%. First-mover advantage in credit attribution means shipping a broken v1 is more career-valuable than engineering a solid v2.

The tell: Check git history. If "v1" was rewritten 80% in the 3 months after "launch" — the original was a prototype shipped as product. Also: is the person who "launched" still maintaining it? If they moved on within weeks, they knew.
